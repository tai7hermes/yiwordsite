Words on the Lines / WENYAN

01 乾 文言 1WENYAN-01-01元者，善之長也。亨者，嘉之會也。利者，義之和也。貞者，事之幹也。君子體仁足以長人，嘉會足以合禮，利物足以和義，貞固足以幹事。君子行此四德者，故曰：「乾，元、亨、利、貞。」
Yuan is the beginning and leading force of goodness; heng is the gathering of favorable conditions; li is the harmony between benefit and what is right; and zhen is the firm foundation on which affairs can be completed. A cultivated person embodies compassion in leading others, brings people together in a manner consistent with proper conduct, benefits things without violating justice, and remains steadfast when carrying out responsibilities. These are not four promises of effortless success. They describe four qualities that make constructive action possible. The Qian hexagram is therefore characterized by yuan, heng, li, and zhen.
01 乾 文言 2WENYAN-01-02初九曰：「潛龍，勿用。」何謂也？子曰：「龍德而隱者也。不易乎世，不成乎名，遯世无悶，不見是而无悶，樂則行之，憂則違之，確乎其不可拔，潛龍也。」
The hidden dragon represents someone with exceptional character who has not yet stepped into public view. Such a person does not change convictions simply to follow fashion or rush to secure recognition. Living apart from public affairs need not cause resentment, and being overlooked need not lead to bitterness. One acts when doing so accords with one's principles and refrains when the circumstances are troubling. The decisive quality is an inner resolve that cannot easily be uprooted. The image of the concealed dragon thus presents restraint as a positive expression of strength, not as evidence of weakness.
01 乾 文言 3WENYAN-01-03九二曰：「見龍在田，利見大人。」何謂也？子曰：「龍德而正中者也。庸言之信，庸行之謹；閑邪存其誠，善世而不伐，德博而化。易曰：『見龍在田，利見大人。』君德也。」
The dragon appearing in the field describes a person of sound character whose influence is beginning to become visible. Trustworthiness should be evident in everyday speech, care in ordinary actions, and sincerity in resisting dishonest impulses. A person can contribute to the welfare of society without boasting of those contributions. When conduct is consistently good, its influence can spread beyond the individual. The commentary calls this the virtue appropriate to a ruler: moral authority begins with how someone behaves in unremarkable situations, well before power and public reputation become prominent.
01 乾 文言 4WENYAN-01-04九三曰：「君子終日乾乾，夕惕若，厲，无咎。」何謂也？子曰：「君子進德脩業。忠信，所以進德也；脩辭立其誠，所以居業也。知至至之，可與言幾也。知終終之，可與存義也。是故居上位而不驕，在下位而不憂，故乾乾因其時而惕，雖危无咎矣。」
The third line portrays a conscientious person working diligently throughout the day and remaining watchful at night. The explanation links this vigilance to the cultivation of character and the responsible pursuit of work. Loyalty and trust develop virtue, while thoughtful language and sincerity help sustain meaningful undertakings. One should recognize the early signs of an opportunity and know when to advance, but also recognize when an undertaking has reached its proper end. Neither holding a higher position nor occupying a lower one should produce arrogance or despair. Attentiveness to changing circumstances makes it possible to navigate danger without incurring blame.
01 乾 文言 5WENYAN-01-05九四曰：「或躍在淵，无咎。」何謂也？子曰：「上下无常，非為邪也。進退无恒，非離羣也。君子進德脩業，欲及時也。故无咎。」
The dragon that may leap but still remains near the depths illustrates a period of uncertain transition. Moving up or down does not by itself indicate dishonesty, and changing between advance and withdrawal does not necessarily mean abandoning one's companions. A thoughtful person continues to develop abilities and character while waiting for a fitting opportunity. The essential point is to distinguish flexibility from opportunism. An action may be right at one moment and premature at another. Because the person responds to the time rather than forcing a fixed course, the text says that blame can be avoided.
01 乾 文言 6WENYAN-01-06九五曰：「飛龍在天，利見大人。」何謂也？子曰：「同聲相應，同氣相求；水流濕，火就燥，雲從龍，風從虎；聖人作而萬物覩；本乎天者親上，本乎地者親下，則各從其類也。」
When the dragon flies in the sky, its influence is fully visible. The commentary explains this by observing that similar sounds answer one another and similar energies seek each other out. Water flows toward damp ground, fire takes readily to dry material, clouds accompany the dragon, and wind accompanies the tiger. In the same way, when a sage emerges, others perceive and respond to that person's influence. Beings associated with heaven tend upward, while those associated with earth tend downward. The image emphasizes resonance and natural affinity rather than the idea that public authority can be established through force alone.
01 乾 文言 7WENYAN-01-07上九曰：「亢龍，有悔。」何謂也？子曰：「貴而无位，高而无民，賢人在下位而无輔，是以動而有悔也。」
The dragon that climbs too high reaches a precarious condition. Someone may appear distinguished yet lack a secure and legitimate position, stand above others without their support, or be surrounded by capable people who are unable or unwilling to help. Height and prestige are therefore not sufficient measures of sound leadership. Once a person becomes isolated from both ordinary people and trustworthy advisers, taking further action can bring regret. The warning concerns overextension: a position that looks impressive may already have lost the relationships and foundations needed to sustain it.
01 乾 文言 8WENYAN-01-08「潛龍勿用」，下也。
The expression 'Hidden dragon; do not act' indicates a condition of being below or out of public sight. This does not condemn the person's abilities. It says that those abilities have not yet reached the stage at which they can be exercised openly. The location of the first line helps explain the restraint called for in the original judgment. Recognizing a low or preliminary position is part of recognizing the correct time for action. The emphasis is on patient preparation, not permanent withdrawal.
01 乾 文言 9WENYAN-01-09「見龍在田」，時舍也。
'The dragon appears in the field' marks a time when valuable character can begin to be recognized. The phrase 'the time allows it to remain' suggests that the emerging individual has found a place where talents may be displayed without rushing beyond the circumstances. Unlike the hidden dragon, this stage permits contact with others and the beginnings of public influence. Yet the position is still one of development rather than complete authority. The lesson lies in accepting the opportunities available at this particular stage.
01 乾 文言 10WENYAN-01-10「終日乾乾」，行事也。
'Diligent throughout the day' points to someone actively engaged in responsibilities. The language is not merely a compliment about being busy. It places continued effort within a phase when decisions and conduct can have consequences. Work requires attention, persistence, and an awareness that one's position is changing. The accompanying warning about vigilance is therefore meaningful: activity should be sustained by reflection rather than performed mechanically. The line describes an active stage of cultivation and practical engagement.
01 乾 文言 11WENYAN-01-11「或躍在淵」，自試也。
'Perhaps leaping, yet remaining in the depths' describes a person testing whether the time is right to rise. The commentary's brief explanation, 'testing oneself,' emphasizes uncertainty rather than failure. Abilities may be ready, but circumstances still need to be assessed. A trial movement may reveal whether advancement is appropriate, and hesitation may prevent a premature commitment. The image captures the point between private preparation and full public action, when judgment matters as much as courage.
01 乾 文言 12WENYAN-01-12「飛龍在天」，上治也。
'The dragon flies in the sky' signifies effective governance from a higher position. The commentary shifts attention from the mere image of height to its public consequences: someone who has reached the appropriate level can now exercise responsibility. Visibility must be matched by the capacity to bring order and benefit to others. The image is therefore not simply about promotion or personal success. It concerns the stage at which developed virtue and ability are placed where they can shape collective affairs.
01 乾 文言 13WENYAN-01-13「亢龍有悔」，窮之災也。
'The overreaching dragon has regret' warns of the distress that follows when development passes its sustainable limit. The phrase 'the calamity of exhaustion' describes a situation in which further progress is no longer possible on the same terms. A person who has reached the extreme may mistake height for permanence and overlook the signs of decline. The statement offers a compact explanation of the warning in the top line: without an awareness of limits, apparent achievement can turn into misfortune.
01 乾 文言 14WENYAN-01-14「乾元用九」，天下治也。
'Qian's use of the nines' is explained as a condition in which the world is well ordered. Unlike the image of a solitary ruler standing above the rest, this statement points to the coordinated operation of strong forces. Its brevity should not obscure the distinction: the mature use of strength is not endless competition to become first. It is the proper arrangement of capacities so that order becomes possible. The passage interprets the special 'use of nine' statement in terms of governance rather than individual display.
01 乾 文言 15WENYAN-01-15「潛龍勿用」，陽氣潛藏。
'Hidden dragon; do not act' is here explained through the language of energy: the yang force remains concealed. The commentary describes the natural condition of an emerging power before it has become manifest. Something can exist and be developing even when it cannot yet be seen. The first stage of Qian is thus a time for conserving and forming strength. Bringing it prematurely into the open would ignore the relationship between development and timing. Concealment is part of the process, not necessarily an obstacle to it.
01 乾 文言 16WENYAN-01-16「見龍在田」，天下文明。
'The dragon appears in the field' is associated here with the world becoming illuminated and civilized. The commentary broadens the earlier account of personal virtue into a social image: when constructive abilities come into view, they can begin to influence the surrounding world. The dragon's appearance does not mean the process is complete. Rather, it marks the emergence of a force whose effects may now be recognized. The emphasis falls on the public visibility of positive character and the gradual spread of its influence.
01 乾 文言 17WENYAN-01-17「終日乾乾」，與時偕行。
'Diligent throughout the day' is interpreted as keeping pace with the time. Constant effort has value because it responds to developing circumstances rather than resisting change. A cultivated person must neither rest upon earlier achievements nor move blindly without observing what has altered. This stage calls for steady work, continual learning, and vigilance. To move with the time means bringing one's conduct into line with what the moment requires. The commentary thus connects diligence with adaptability.
01 乾 文言 18WENYAN-01-18「或躍在淵」，乾道乃革。
'Perhaps leaping, yet remaining in the depths' signifies a turning point in the Way of Qian. The image portrays neither settled withdrawal nor secure ascendance. It is the moment when an established pattern begins to change and a different level of action becomes possible. Because the transformation has not yet been completed, certainty would be misplaced. The commentary encourages awareness of the transition itself: when the underlying conditions shift, the way strength is expressed must also change.
01 乾 文言 19WENYAN-01-19「飛龍在天」，乃位乎天德。
'The dragon flies in the sky' means that a person has reached a position from which heavenly virtue can be expressed. The focus is not on height alone. The appropriate position enables a cultivated individual to put the qualities of Qian into practice in a visible and consequential manner. Strength, sound judgment, and responsibility should now be apparent in conduct. This stage differs from the hidden or testing stages because capacity and opportunity have come together.
01 乾 文言 20WENYAN-01-20「亢龍有悔」，與時偕極。
'The overreaching dragon has regret' is linked to the time itself reaching an extreme. Even a successful development has a limit. When the circumstances that sustained an ascent have been exhausted, continuing to rise in the same manner becomes hazardous. The commentary treats excess as a temporal condition as well as a personal failing. Wise action requires noticing when a period is ending, rather than believing that its earlier momentum will continue indefinitely.
01 乾 文言 21WENYAN-01-21「乾元用九」，乃見天則。
'Qian's use of the nines' allows the pattern of heaven to be seen. The phrase points beyond any single dragon or individual achievement to the principles governing the whole process. Strength is expressed through change, timing, and the proper relation among different stages. Observing all the lines together makes that pattern clearer. The special statement therefore invites the reader to understand Qian as an ordered movement of forces rather than a command to dominate others.
01 乾 文言 22WENYAN-01-22乾元者，始而亨者也。利貞者，性情也。乾始能以美利利天下，不言所利，大矣哉！大哉乾元，剛健中正，純粹精也。六爻發揮，旁通情也。時乘六龍，以御天也。雲行雨施，天下平也。
The originating force of Qian allows things to begin and develop; benefit and steadfastness express the character and proper direction of that development. Qian bestows benefits widely without restricting them to a favored recipient. Its greatness rests on vigor, balance, rectitude, and purity. The six lines reveal different situations through which this force may unfold. The six dragons, moving with their proper times, symbolize the ordering of heavenly processes. Clouds travel, rain falls, and the world is brought into harmony. The passage presents creative strength as generative and widely beneficial, not as mere forcefulness.
01 乾 文言 23WENYAN-01-23君子以成德為行，日可見之行也。潛之為言也，隱而未見，行而未成，是以君子弗用也。
A cultivated person's actions should make acquired virtue visible in daily life. Yet the image of hiding has a specific meaning: character may have developed privately without having reached a form ready for public expression. The work is not complete simply because the intention is good. In the first stage, withdrawal helps protect an unfinished undertaking. The text therefore distinguishes possessing potential from having the conditions and demonstrated practice needed to use it. Restraint can be faithful to the goal of mature action.
01 乾 文言 24WENYAN-01-24君子學以聚之，問以辯之，寬以居之，仁以行之，易曰：「見龍在田，利見大人。」君德也。
A cultivated person gathers understanding through study, clarifies it by asking questions, receives others with generosity, and puts it into action through benevolence. These four practices connect learning with ethical conduct. Knowledge alone is insufficient if it is not tested by inquiry and translated into how others are treated. The passage uses these qualities to explain the dragon appearing in the field: the virtues appropriate to leadership are already taking shape through education, openness, and practical care. Public influence should grow from that foundation.
01 乾 文言 25WENYAN-01-25九三，重剛而不中，上不在天，下不在田；故乾乾因其時而惕，雖危，无咎矣。
The third line contains strong yang energy but does not occupy a central position. It stands above the field without yet reaching the domain of heaven. That unstable middle condition explains why the person must work energetically and remain alert. Neither status nor circumstances offer complete security. Vigilance does not mean panic; it means recognizing the particular risks of an intermediate stage. Even when danger exists, disciplined conduct can prevent blame. The passage reads the line's demanding language through its place in the six-line structure.
01 乾 文言 26WENYAN-01-26九四，重剛而不中，上不在天，下不在田，中不在人，故或之。或之者，疑之也，故无咎。
The fourth line is also strongly yang but not central. It is no longer situated in the field below, has not yet arrived at heaven above, and does not neatly occupy the ordinary human position between them. That uncertainty is why the line says 'perhaps.' The word signals an unresolved choice about advancing or remaining where one is. A cautious trial or pause is appropriate while the situation remains ambiguous. The commentary treats doubt as a feature of transition, and careful judgment as the way to avoid blame.
01 乾 文言 27WENYAN-01-27夫大人者，與天地合其德，與日月合其明，與四時合其序，與鬼神合其吉凶；先天而天弗違，後天而奉天時，天且弗違，而況於人乎？況於鬼神乎？
A great person brings conduct into accord with the virtue of heaven and earth, with the brightness of sun and moon, and with the succession of the seasons. Even the traditional language of spirits and good or bad fortune points to this comprehensive harmony. Acting ahead of an event does not violate the heavenly pattern, while acting afterward still observes the proper time. The passage describes an ideal of wisdom so thoroughly attuned to change that action is neither premature nor belated. Its claims belong to the philosophical and cosmological language of the text, rather than to a testable promise of prediction.
01 乾 文言 28WENYAN-01-28亢之為言也，知進而不知退，知存而不知亡，知得而不知喪；其唯聖人乎？知進退存亡而不失其正者，其唯聖人乎！
To be excessively high is to know only how to advance, not how to withdraw; to notice continued existence but forget the possibility of loss; and to value gain without preparing for deprivation. Such one-sided thinking creates the danger expressed by the overreaching dragon. The passage closes by praising the rare person who understands advance and retreat, survival and decline, gain and loss, while still preserving what is right. True wisdom is not unlimited expansion. It is the ability to recognize opposing possibilities and act without abandoning sound principles.
02 坤 文言 1WENYAN-02-01坤至柔而動也剛，至靜而德方。後得主而有常，含萬物而化光。坤道其順乎！承天而時行。
Kun is supremely yielding, yet its movement can be strong; it is supremely still, yet its character remains upright and firmly shaped. These qualities are not contradictions. Receptivity allows Kun to support the initiating activity represented by Qian and to carry things through to completion. By following the appropriate lead, it keeps to a reliable pattern, embraces the many forms of life, and enables them to flourish. Its distinctive power lies in receiving, nurturing, and acting at the proper time. The passage praises responsiveness as an active virtue rather than simple passivity.
02 坤 文言 2WENYAN-02-02積善之家，必有餘慶；積不善之家，必有餘殃。臣弒其君，子弒其父，非一朝一夕之故，其所由來者漸矣。由辨之不早辨也。易曰：「履霜，堅冰至。」蓋言順也。
A family that steadily accumulates good conduct may leave benefits to later generations, just as persistent wrongdoing can produce harm. The text illustrates this cumulative process with the grave examples of a minister killing his ruler and a son killing his father. Such events do not arise overnight; they are preceded by small developments that were not recognized or corrected early enough. 'Treading on frost; hard ice will come' summarizes this principle. The passage urges attention to early signs because repeated choices can gradually establish a direction that becomes difficult to reverse.
02 坤 文言 3WENYAN-02-03直其正也，方其義也。君子敬以直內，義以方外，敬義立而德不孤。「直方大，不習无不利。」則不疑其所行也。
Straightness refers to inner rectitude, while squareness describes outward conduct shaped by what is right. A cultivated person establishes the inner life through respect and care, then gives external behavior a reliable form through justice. When reverence and righteousness work together, good character is not isolated from action or from other people. The description of being straight, square, and great therefore concerns more than a set of abstract virtues. It portrays someone whose convictions, decisions, and outward behavior fit together so consistently that action need not be accompanied by uncertainty.
02 坤 文言 4WENYAN-02-04陰雖有美，含之。以從王事，弗敢成也。地道也，妻道也，臣道也。地道无成，而代有終也。
Even when receptive strength possesses fine qualities, it may keep them inward rather than display them. In service to the ruler, it helps a larger undertaking reach completion without claiming all the credit. The original passage presents this through the traditional roles of earth, wife, and minister, each understood as assisting a larger process. These are descriptions of a historical model of complementary functions, not a requirement that modern people accept its social hierarchy. Within that framework, Kun's virtue is to support successful completion without insisting on personal prominence.
02 坤 文言 5WENYAN-02-05天地變化，草木蕃；天地閉，賢人隱。易曰：「括囊，无咎，无譽。」蓋言謹也。
When heaven and earth interact, plants and trees flourish; when that interaction closes, worthy people may withdraw from public life. The saying about closing a bag's opening describes caution at a time when open action would be unwise. It promises neither applause nor a spectacular achievement: it speaks of avoiding blame even if no reputation is gained. The commentary links restraint to the conditions of the age. A thoughtful person may need to protect integrity by speaking less and declining involvement until circumstances become more favorable.
02 坤 文言 6WENYAN-02-06君子黃中通理，正位居體，美在其中，而暢於四支，發於事業，美之至也。
Yellow symbolizes balance at the center. The cultivated person is inwardly centered, understands principles clearly, and occupies a fitting position. Goodness is not confined to thought: it becomes visible in bodily conduct and then in public work. The passage moves from inner qualities to outward expression, showing how coherent character affects what a person does. This is called the highest form of beauty because personal values and practical deeds are not separated. The imagery belongs to the traditional symbolism of Kun, where inward steadiness supports effective action.
02 坤 文言 7WENYAN-02-07陰疑於陽必戰，為其嫌於无陽也，故稱龍焉。猶未離其類也，故稱血焉。夫玄黃者，天地之雜也。天玄而地黃。
When yin becomes so powerful that it rivals yang, conflict emerges. The commentary explains why the related line speaks of dragons and blood: the dragon acknowledges the opposing yang force, while blood indicates that yin has not ceased to belong to its own kind. The mingling colors, dark and yellow, symbolize heaven and earth interwoven in the struggle. The passage is a traditional account of complementary forces becoming antagonistic when balance is lost. It describes the imagery of a dangerous extreme rather than celebrating the conflict itself.
