01 乾 大象XIANG-U-01-01天行健，君子以自強不息。Heaven moves with unflagging strength. A responsible person takes this image as a reason to cultivate character and keep improving without giving up.
The word jian denotes enduring generative vigor, not an order to exhaust oneself. Human excellence follows Heaven through persistent ethical cultivation, regular self-correction, and renewed effort when circumstances change. Discipline without moral direction would miss the image’s central point.
01 乾 小象 1XIANG-U-01-02 001Yao潛龍勿用，陽在下也。The hidden dragon should not yet act because the yang line remains at the bottom. Its strength exists, but the appropriate occasion to display it has not arrived.
The first yang line is strong in potential but low in position. Its concealment is not failure: learning, forming character, and testing judgment prepare an ability that cannot yet be exercised responsibly in public. Premature display would disregard the relation between capacity and timing.
01 乾 小象 2XIANG-U-01-03 002Yao見龍在田，德施普也。The dragon appearing in the field signifies virtue spreading outward. Worthy conduct is beginning to benefit people beyond the individual.
In the second place, ability becomes visible without occupying the ruling seat. De (virtue) is recognized through benefits actually extended to others, not through titles. The dragon’s appearance measures development by service and influence rather than by self-promotion.
01 乾 小象 3XIANG-U-01-04 003Yao終日乾乾，反復道也。Working diligently throughout the day means repeatedly returning to the proper course. Constant effort must be guided by principles rather than mere activity.
At the upper edge of the lower trigram, growing responsibility invites danger. Repetition of the Way means examining motives, correcting deviations, and maintaining vigilance through each day; it is neither mechanical busyness nor anxious perfectionism. Increasing success requires increasing self-command.
01 乾 小象 4XIANG-U-01-05 004Yao或躍在淵，進无咎也。The dragon poised to leap from the depths can advance without blame. It is beginning to act at the moment when advancement is appropriate.
The fourth line crosses from the lower into the upper trigram and approaches a change in status. To leap or remain below are both conceivable; neither is right without regard to conditions. Advancement becomes blameless when courage is coupled with assessment of consequences.
01 乾 小象 5XIANG-U-01-06 005Yao飛龍在天，大人造也。The flying dragon in Heaven signifies the emergence of a capable and virtuous leader. The time has come for such a person to accomplish important work.
The fifth yang line combines leadership with a fitting central place. The great person emerges when cultivated virtue, capability, public opportunity, and office converge. The task of authority is then to convert personal excellence into sound governance for others.
01 乾 小象 6XIANG-U-01-07 006Yao亢龍有悔，盈不可久也。The dragon soaring too high brings regret because fullness cannot last indefinitely. Excessive ascent, however impressive, eventually meets its limit.
At the top, the dragon has exceeded the most fitting height. Prosperity and influence contain limits; treating ascent as an end produces regret. Knowing when to yield, distribute power, or step aside is part of the very strength celebrated earlier.
01 乾 小象 7XIANG-U-01-08用九，天德不可為首也。The use of all nine lines teaches that heavenly virtue does not depend on any one dragon claiming supremacy. Strength is best exercised without a contest for first place.
When all six yang lines change, their collective image resists a single dragon claiming primacy. This does not forbid leadership; it cautions against possessiveness and domination. Mature strength makes room for other agents and serves a shared order above personal supremacy.
02 坤 大象XIANG-U-02-01地勢坤，君子以厚德載物。Earth receives and supports everything with its depth and breadth. The exemplary person learns from it to develop generous character and carry the needs of others.
Earth's receptive force is active endurance: it bears diverse lives and allows each to develop. The exemplary person imitates this generous capacity through reliability and responsibility. Kun complements Qian's initiative; gentleness here does not mean an absence of standards.
02 坤 小象 1XIANG-U-02-02 007Yao履霜堅冰，陰始凝也。馴致其道，至堅冰也。The first frost shows that yin has begun to condense. As this small change advances gradually, hard ice will follow; beginnings therefore deserve attention.
Frost announces that the cold has only begun to settle. The passage describes gradual accumulation from first condensation to solid ice, not a sudden catastrophe. In ethics and administration, small recurring distortions should be recognized before they form entrenched patterns.
02 坤 小象 2XIANG-U-02-03 008Yao六二之動，直以方也。不習无不利，地道光也。When the second six acts, it is inwardly straight and outwardly square—sincere and measured. 'Without rehearsed contrivance, nothing is unfavorable' means its right conduct comes naturally. Thus the Way of Earth shines forth in its full, expansive brightness.
Straight describes inner sincerity; square describes conduct conforming to proper measure. 'Without practice' means unforced proficiency, not a rejection of education. The final phrase, 'the Way of Earth shines forth,' positively concludes that receptive virtue becomes evident and widely radiant through upright, well-ordered action.
02 坤 小象 3XIANG-U-02-04 009Yao含章可貞，以時發也。或從王事，知光大也。To keep one's fine qualities within while remaining correct is to reveal them when the time is right. To take part in the king's affairs is to show an understanding that is bright and great, without insisting on personal credit.
Concealed beauty can remain steadfast because it is disclosed when circumstances warrant. Following the king's work need not involve taking its credit. The 'brightness of understanding' praises discernment of timing and responsibility, rather than timid suppression of one's gifts.
02 坤 小象 4XIANG-U-02-05 010Yao括囊无咎，慎不害也。Tying up the mouth of the sack avoids blame because careful restraint prevents harm. There are moments when silence offers better protection than display.
The closed sack evokes careful speech near the center of political power, where a careless word may create disproportionate harm. Caution is justified by actual risk and limited authority; it should not be confused with a blanket duty to remain silent in the face of wrongdoing.
02 坤 小象 5XIANG-U-02-06 011Yao黃裳元吉，文在中也。The yellow lower garment is greatly auspicious because its refinement is held within. Modest appearance can express centered, balanced character.
Yellow symbolizes the center, and the lower garment suggests dignified modesty rather than ostentation. 'Refinement is within' locates worth in balanced character, not impressive display. A ruler in a yielding position is strengthened by measured conduct and inner composure.
02 坤 小象 6XIANG-U-02-07 012Yao龍戰于野，其道窮也。Dragons fighting in the open field show that this course has reached an impasse. When opposing forces become extreme, conflict is difficult to avoid.
At the upper limit, receptive force becomes so extreme that it contends with the creative, producing dragons at war. 'Its Way is exhausted' explains the clash as a loss of proportion. The warning concerns absolutizing an otherwise valuable strength until cooperation breaks down.
02 坤 小象 7XIANG-U-02-08用六永貞，以大終也。Perseverance in the use of six is favorable because it enables a great completion. Receptive strength fulfils its work through steadfast continuity.
All six yielding lines change, and 'lasting correctness' supplies their compass. Receptivity without a firm ethical direction may become mere acquiescence. 'A great completion' affirms Kun's power to bring major work to fruition through faithful support rather than claims of primacy.
03 屯 大象XIANG-U-03-01雲雷，屯；君子以經綸。Clouds and thunder mark the difficulty of beginnings. The exemplary person responds by untangling confusion, organizing affairs, and laying the foundations of good government.
Clouds and thunder signal the turbulence of things first taking shape. Jinglun originally suggests sorting tangled threads; the ruler's task is to identify structure, assign responsibilities, and build workable institutions. Confusion at the beginning is a condition to organize, not a permanent verdict.
03 屯 小象 1XIANG-U-03-02 013Yao雖磐桓，志行正也。以貴下賤，大得民也。Although progress is hesitant, the intention remains directed toward what is right. A person of higher standing gains the people’s support by humbly approaching those below.
The first line hesitates without compromising its intention. A person of high standing who goes down to meet those of lower standing earns the people's allegiance through humility. Founding leadership requires legitimacy and shared trust before decisive movement.
03 屯 小象 2XIANG-U-03-03 014Yao六二之難，乘剛也。十年乃字，反常也。The difficulty of the second line comes from a yielding line riding a firm one. The long delay before marriage reflects an eventual return to an ordinary, fitting course.
The yielding second line rides over the firm first, exposing a strained relationship. The long delay before betrothal figuratively marks an eventual return to normal relations. Integrity is preserved by refusing premature alliances simply to escape hardship.
03 屯 小象 3XIANG-U-03-04 015Yao即鹿无虞，以從禽也。君子舍之，往吝窮也。Pursuing deer without a guide means chasing prey blindly. The wise person abandons the pursuit because pressing onward would end in frustration.
Hunting deer without a knowledgeable guide expresses pursuit without adequate information. The honorable person abandons the chase before desire turns into costly error. This is not contempt for opportunity but recognition that a decision needs orientation and reliable counsel.
03 屯 小象 4XIANG-U-03-05 016Yao求而往，明也。Going forward to seek the marriage shows discernment. The act becomes favorable because the purpose and occasion are finally clear.
The fourth line moves to seek a partner only when the need and direction are intelligible. Its action is called 'clear-sighted' because it resolves an earlier uncertainty. Sound alliances arise from timely judgment, not embarrassment at having previously held back.
03 屯 小象 5XIANG-U-03-06 017Yao屯其膏，施未光也。The rich nourishment cannot yet be shared widely, so its beneficial effects remain limited. Resources alone do not guarantee that help reaches others.
The ruler possesses resources symbolized by rich nourishment, yet its benefits do not spread far. Accumulation has little public value unless it reaches those in need. In the hardship of beginnings, an unequal distribution of support can undermine the emerging order.
03 屯 小象 6XIANG-U-03-07 018Yao泣血漣如，何可長也。Tears of blood flow in the final line: how could such distress continue? Endless paralysis and grief cannot provide a sustainable way forward.
The final image of bloody tears exposes a situation that cannot remain indefinitely unresolved. Grief may disclose genuine suffering but cannot substitute for judgment and reconstruction. The rhetorical question urges a change of course before paralysis becomes habitual.
04 蒙 大象XIANG-U-04-01山下出泉，蒙；君子以果行育德。A spring flows from beneath the mountain. Seeing this image of youthful uncertainty, the exemplary person acts decisively while patiently nurturing moral character.
The mountain spring is young and its course not yet settled. Learning combines decisive action with the gradual cultivation of character: mere punishment without understanding does not dispel ignorance, while reflection without practice cannot build judgment.
04 蒙 小象 1XIANG-U-04-02 019Yao利用刑人，以正法也。Corrective discipline is useful when it establishes a clear rule. Education should release people from ignorance rather than impose punishment for its own sake.
Discipline is defended specifically as a means of establishing right standards. Its legitimacy depends on clarity, proportionality, and an educational purpose. An instructor who confuses correction with humiliation defeats the task of leading people out of uncertainty.
04 蒙 小象 2XIANG-U-04-03 020Yao子克家，剛柔接也。The son’s ability to manage the household arises when firmness and gentleness connect. Successful guidance requires these complementary qualities.
A younger person's capacity to manage a household joins firmness to responsiveness. Effective teaching does not treat a learner as either wholly independent or permanently subordinate. Structure and empathy must work together if responsibility is to mature.
04 蒙 小象 3XIANG-U-04-04 021Yao勿用取女，行不順也。The warning against taking the woman concerns conduct that does not proceed in the proper way. Attraction without integrity leads to an unsuitable union.
The warning about an unsuitable marriage describes conduct that fails to follow an appropriate course. Attraction or immediate benefit is not enough to make a bond sound. The judgment asks whether the relationship respects obligations and the people it will affect.
04 蒙 小象 4XIANG-U-04-05 022Yao困蒙之吝，獨遠實也。The distress of ignorance results from being isolated from what is real. Refusing instruction and remaining distant from trustworthy knowledge brings regret.
The third line remains trapped in ignorance because it distances itself from what is solid and real. Isolation from trustworthy guidance magnifies mistakes. Openness to evidence and to capable teachers is a practical form of intellectual humility.
04 蒙 小象 5XIANG-U-04-06 023Yao童蒙之吉，順以巽也。The fortunate childlike learner is receptive and modest. Willingness to learn is more valuable than pretending to know everything.
The fourth line's youthful ignorance becomes auspicious through compliant receptivity. This is not praise of passive credulity: a learner progresses by admitting what remains unknown, receiving good guidance, and then applying it intelligently.
04 蒙 小象 6XIANG-U-04-07 024Yao利禦寇，上下順也。Defending against bandits succeeds because higher and lower positions cooperate. Proper correction protects people instead of turning force into aggression.
The highest line draws a line between resisting harmful intruders and turning education into aggression. Upper and lower cooperate when protection is exercised rightly. The aim of strong correction is to secure learning and community, not to manufacture more enemies.
05 需 大象XIANG-U-05-01雲上於天，需；君子以飲食宴樂。Clouds gather above Heaven, but the rain has not yet arrived. The exemplary person waits without agitation, enjoying ordinary food, fellowship, and rest.
Clouds above the sky promise rain without delivering it immediately. Waiting is not a loss of agency: healthy enjoyment, nourishment, and composure preserve the strength required for the proper hour. A person need not live in agitation simply because results remain pending.
05 需 小象 1XIANG-U-05-02 025Yao需于郊，不犯難行也。利用恆，无咎，未失常也。Waiting in the outskirts avoids entering danger prematurely. Maintaining a steady way of life prevents mistakes and preserves one’s proper course.
Waiting at the outskirts keeps the subject beyond the dangerous center. 'Constancy' preserves the ordinary course of action without tempting fate. Prudence distinguishes patient readiness from entering a crisis merely to appear brave.
05 需 小象 2XIANG-U-05-03 026Yao需于沙，衍在中也。雖小有言，以終吉也。Waiting on the sand allows room for movement without losing balance. Minor criticism need not spoil an ultimately favorable outcome.
The sandy bank is closer to the water and therefore more exposed, yet still leaves room to maneuver. Small complaints or criticism do not decide the outcome. Maintaining proportion and freedom of movement makes a favorable conclusion possible.
05 需 小象 3XIANG-U-05-04 027Yao需于泥，災在外也。自我致寇，敬慎不敗也。Waiting in the mud places one close to trouble; the threat is still outside. If one has attracted attackers, alert and respectful caution may prevent defeat.
Mud at the river's edge exposes the subject to attack, some of it invited by previous movement. The image does not deny external danger; it asks how personal choices increased vulnerability. Serious alertness and respectful caution can still prevent defeat.
05 需 小象 4XIANG-U-05-05 028Yao需于血，順以聽也。Waiting amid bloodshed calls for a willingness to heed circumstances. Listening and yielding appropriately offers a path away from immediate peril.
Waiting amid blood suggests danger already experienced, not a quiet pause. The line emphasizes heeding what circumstances reveal. Yielding appropriately and listening carefully can save lives and restore options that obstinacy would close.
05 需 小象 5XIANG-U-05-06 029Yao酒食，貞吉，以中正也。Food and drink during the wait are favorable because the person occupies a balanced and proper position. Composure is not the same as neglect.
Wine and food are auspicious when consumed from a centered, correct position. The image praises a settled relation to uncertainty: calm readiness, measured hospitality, and inner steadiness are different from carelessness or withdrawal from duty.
05 需 小象 6XIANG-U-05-07 030Yao不速之客來，敬之終吉。雖不當位，未大失也。Unexpected visitors are treated respectfully, and the ending is fortunate. Though the position is not ideal, the essential requirements have not been seriously lost.
Unexpected guests appear at a stage where position is imperfect. Courtesy instead of hostility can avert a larger mistake. The passage grants that one may not control every arrival, yet can choose the response that preserves dignity and limits harm.
06 訟 大象XIANG-U-06-01天與水違行，訟；君子以作事謀始。Heaven and water move in opposing directions, portraying contention. Before beginning an undertaking, the exemplary person carefully considers its origins and terms.
Heaven and water moving apart portray the divergence from which disputes grow. The remedy begins before litigation: define obligations, anticipate conflicting expectations, and examine the inception of an enterprise. Prevention belongs to justice as much as judgment does.
06 訟 小象 1XIANG-U-06-02 031Yao不永所事，訟不可長也。雖小有言，其辯明也。Not prolonging the dispute acknowledges that contention should not endure. Minor objections may arise, but the issues can eventually be clarified.
Refusing to prolong contention shows that litigation is not itself a virtue. Minor accusations may persist even after a sensible retreat. A distinction between clearing the issue and sustaining personal hostility prevents the latter from consuming the former.
06 訟 小象 2XIANG-U-06-03 032Yao不克訟，歸逋竄也。自下訟上，患至掇也。An unsuccessful litigant withdraws and takes refuge. A subordinate who persists in suing a superior readily brings trouble upon himself in this historical setting.
The lower party loses by confronting a stronger superior and retreats into concealment. The text reflects hierarchical conditions of its period; the transferable lesson is to judge forum, evidence, and power realistically. Legal right and tactical wisdom are not identical questions.
06 訟 小象 3XIANG-U-06-04 033Yao食舊德，從上吉也。Living on inherited virtue and following those above can end favorably. One should undertake service without insistently demanding personal credit.
Serving on the basis of inherited virtue recommends fidelity to legitimate responsibilities instead of obsessive pursuit of status. Obedience is praised here insofar as it serves an ordered relation, not as unconditional submission to any command.
06 訟 小象 4XIANG-U-06-05 034Yao復即命渝，安貞，不失也。Changing course after a failed dispute means returning to one’s proper obligations. Peaceful constancy keeps a person from greater loss.
After a dispute fails, the subject returns to the proper mandate and changes course. The reversal requires the courage to relinquish a losing argument. Quiet adherence to what is right can protect both reputation and future action.
06 訟 小象 5XIANG-U-06-06 035Yao訟，元吉，以中正也。Judging a lawsuit with great good fortune rests on fairness and balance. Justice requires a decision rooted in what is centered and right.
A central and correct decision settles contention because it is not captured by either side. The highest good fortune refers to impartial adjudication, not merely winning a case. Fair process must be grounded in sound reasons and proportional outcomes.
06 訟 小象 6XIANG-U-06-07 036Yao以訟受服，亦不足敬也。An official robe won through litigation is not worthy of honor. A contentious victory does not by itself establish moral respectability.
A robe of rank won by quarrelling may be legally possessed yet morally undeserving. The distinction between obtaining a reward and earning respect matters. Achievement through relentless conflict may impoverish the very standing it seemed to secure.
07 師 大象XIANG-U-07-01地中有水，師；君子以容民畜衆。Water lies within Earth, like a gathered force held in reserve. The exemplary person receives the people generously and fosters their shared strength.
Water stored within the earth symbolizes organized potential. The exemplary leader nurtures and gathers people before asking them to act together. Military strength rests on public support, provisioning, and humane responsibility, not on coercive numbers alone.
07 師 小象 1XIANG-U-07-02 037Yao師出以律；失律，凶也。An army must depart under discipline. When military order breaks down, the expedition risks misfortune, whatever its intentions.
An army's departure requires discipline and commonly understood rules. Good intentions cannot compensate for an undisciplined campaign. The warning treats order as a moral condition for collective force, because disorder magnifies harm to soldiers and civilians.
07 師 小象 2XIANG-U-07-03 038Yao在師中吉，承天寵也。王三錫命，懷萬邦也。Good fortune for the commander comes from receiving the ruler’s trust. Repeated appointments also impose responsibility for protecting the many states.
The commander receives trust and repeated appointments, but these honors bring a greater duty to safeguard the realm. A leader's success must be measured by protection and stability, not simply personal promotion or victory in battle.
07 師 小象 3XIANG-U-07-04 039Yao師或輿尸，大无功也。An army returning with corpses has accomplished nothing worthy of praise. Poor command is measured by human loss as well as failure of objectives.
Bodies carried back in wagons strip military action of the appearance of success. The image points to disastrous command and unnecessary losses. It asks that outcomes be judged in human terms rather than by rhetoric about glory.
07 師 小象 4XIANG-U-07-05 040Yao左次无咎，未失常也。Withdrawing and encamping to one side is not blameworthy. A measured retreat need not violate proper military practice.
Moving to the left and pausing can be a properly disciplined withdrawal. Strategic restraint may preserve capacity to act later. Treating every retreat as shame can force commanders into battles that should never have been fought.
07 師 小象 5XIANG-U-07-06 041Yao長子帥師，以中行也。弟子輿尸，使不當也。The mature leader commands by the central way; placing an unfit younger man in charge invites casualties. Choosing the wrong commander has grave consequences.
The 'eldest son' symbolizes a competent, mature commander; the younger, unfit substitute produces casualties. Appointment based on preference rather than ability is an ethical failure. The line locates responsibility for military disaster in the act of choosing leaders.
07 師 小象 6XIANG-U-07-07 042Yao大君有命，以正功也。小人勿用，必亂邦也。The great ruler apportions honors to recognize genuine achievement. Unworthy people must not be entrusted with government, lest they disrupt the state.
The great ruler properly recognizes meritorious service but must not entrust the state to people lacking character. Wartime achievement does not automatically qualify a person to govern. Postwar appointments test the integrity of the peace that victory is supposed to secure.
08 比 大象XIANG-U-08-01地上有水，比；先王以建萬國，親諸侯。Water spreads over Earth and joins with it. Former kings learned from this image to establish states and maintain close relationships with their regional lords.
Water spread upon the earth portrays attachment across separate places. Establishing many states and relating closely to their lords is an image of political cohesion. Lasting solidarity requires bonds that support difference rather than swallowing it.
08 比 小象 1XIANG-U-08-02 043Yao比之初六，有它吉也。The first line’s sincere attachment may bring unexpected good fortune. Trust formed at the outset can attract help from beyond the original circle.
The first line's sincere approach begins an alliance on a trustworthy foundation. Unexpected benefit may arrive from outside the immediate circle. Early openness is fruitful when it is accompanied by discernment and consistent conduct.
08 比 小象 2XIANG-U-08-03 044Yao比之自內，不自失也。Joining with others from a genuine inner commitment prevents self-betrayal. The relationship is sound because it begins in sincerity.
To join others 'from within' is to commit without betraying the self. Genuine partnership differs from opportunistic dependence. The test is whether attachment grows from conviction and mutual respect rather than fear of standing alone.
08 比 小象 3XIANG-U-08-04 045Yao比之匪人，不亦傷乎！Associating with the wrong people is a painful mistake. Companionship without judgment can injure rather than support a person.
The third line warns that the objects of friendship matter as much as the desire for company. Joining harmful people can injure one's character and others. Affiliation should be evaluated by conduct, not merely proximity or promised advantage.
08 比 小象 4XIANG-U-08-05 046Yao外比於賢，以從上也。Openly aligning with the worthy outside oneself means following what is higher and better. Loyalty should attach to virtue, not mere familiarity.
The fourth line looks outward to someone worthy and willingly follows a higher standard. Such alignment can be honorable even across hierarchy. Loyalty is justified by the virtue of the person or cause, not by status alone.
08 比 小象 5XIANG-U-08-06 047Yao顯比之吉，位正中也。舍逆取順，失前禽也。邑人不誡，上使中也。The good fortune of open alliance comes from the ruler's correctly centered place. In the hunt, those moving against the drive are released while those following its course are taken, even at the cost of losing the animals ahead. The townspeople need no warning because the ruler guides them through centered conduct.
A ruler in the correct central place practices open alliance: in the hunt, animals that move away are allowed to escape, while willing followers remain. The imagery links authority with noncoercion, allowing order to emerge without trapping every possible dissenter.
08 比 小象 6XIANG-U-08-07 048Yao比之无首，无所終也。Association without a beginning or head cannot reach a proper conclusion. A community lacking a coherent basis has little prospect of lasting.
A relationship lacking a proper head or beginning cannot reach a sound completion. Community requires a legitimate organizing purpose and shared commitments. Numbers alone cannot compensate for the absence of direction or trust.
09 小畜 大象XIANG-U-09-01風行天上，小畜，君子以懿文德。Wind passes high across the sky, indicating a small gathering of strength. The exemplary person cultivates grace in conduct and refinement in character.
Wind passing above Heaven suggests limited restraint and the gradual gathering of small resources. Since decisive power is not yet available, the exemplary person refines cultural virtue, speech, and manners. Modest improvements prepare a stronger future without pretending it has already arrived.
09 小畜 小象 1XIANG-U-09-02 049Yao復自道，其義吉也。Returning to one’s own right path is fortunate. The movement succeeds because it accords with what ought to be done.
Returning along one's own rightful path restores direction without needless confrontation. The auspiciousness lies in agreeing with what is appropriate, not in proving independence. Small, timely self-corrections prevent larger deviations.
09 小畜 小象 2XIANG-U-09-03 050Yao牽復在中，亦不自失也。Being drawn back toward the right course still preserves the center. Joining others in correction need not mean losing independent judgment.
Being drawn back with others can preserve one's center. The relationship between cooperation and individuality is not a contradiction: the subject accepts help while retaining responsibility for the decision to change course.
09 小畜 小象 3XIANG-U-09-04 051Yao夫妻反目，不能正室也。The husband and wife turn against each other because the household relationship is not properly ordered. A damaged vehicle mirrors this internal discord.
Domestic conflict illustrates the cost of strength exercised without proportion. The inability to keep the household rightly ordered is not solved by louder insistence. Mutual recognition and a proper distribution of responsibilities are required.
09 小畜 小象 4XIANG-U-09-05 052Yao有孚。惕出。上合志也。Sincerity helps fear and danger recede because the yielding line shares the purpose of those above. Trust gives cooperation practical force.
When sincerity overcomes threatening obstacles, the earlier sense of danger loosens. Trust is not naïve exposure: it is the deliberate removal of hostility through credible conduct and a willingness to make amends.
09 小畜 小象 5XIANG-U-09-06 053Yao有孚攣如，不獨富也。Being bound together in good faith means not enjoying wealth alone. Prosperity has greater value when neighbors can share in it.
A network of trust distributes resources among neighbors instead of hoarding them. The line treats prosperity as relational; beneficial influence grows when one's own abundance strengthens the wider community rather than merely the household.
09 小畜 小象 6XIANG-U-09-07 054Yao既雨既處，德積載也。君子征凶，有所疑也。Rain has finally fallen because virtue and resources have accumulated. Yet further advance is suspect: completion calls for caution, not a new reckless campaign.
Rain has come, but the moon's fullness and the danger of continued advance warn against excess. The successful accumulation of a little strength does not make unbounded expansion wise. Completion requires recognizing when to stop.
10 履 大象XIANG-U-10-01上天下澤，履；君子以辨上下，定民志。Heaven stands above and the lake below, each in its proper place. The exemplary person clarifies roles and distinctions so that people understand their responsibilities.
Heaven above the marsh expresses ordered differences in height and place. The exemplary person distinguishes ranks and responsibilities to secure the people's aspirations. Proper order should serve fairness and social trust, not arbitrary humiliation.
10 履 小象 1XIANG-U-10-02 055Yao素履之往，獨行願也。Walking simply on one’s own path fulfills a personal resolve. A modest start needs no borrowed grandeur.
The first line walks simply, without pretension. Conduct grounded in an ordinary duty avoids blame because it does not claim privileges the position cannot support. Humility allows progress without theatrical self-assertion.
