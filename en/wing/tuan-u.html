Tuan Commentary I / TUAN-U
TUAN-U · 30 units · text_version: yizhuan-en-3.0.0 · review_status: draft
UnitOriginalTranslation
01 乾 彖辭TUAN-U-01-01大哉乾元，萬物資始，乃統天。雲行雨施，品物流形，大明終始，六位時成。時乘六龍以御天，乾道變化，各正性命，保合大和，乃利貞。首出庶物，萬國咸寧。
The Qian commentary describes the immense creative power of Heaven. All things receive their beginnings from it; clouds move, rain falls, and living forms take shape. The six lines are compared to six dragons moving with the proper times. As the heavenly way changes, each being develops according to its own nature while participating in a larger harmony. The passage ends by connecting this creative order with peace among the many states.
02 坤 彖辭TUAN-U-02-01至哉坤元，萬物資生，乃順承天。坤厚載物，德合无疆；含弘光大，品物咸亨。牝馬地類，行地无疆，柔順利貞。君子攸行，先迷失道，後順得常。西南得朋，乃與類行，東北喪朋，乃終有慶。安貞之吉，應地无疆。
The Kun commentary describes Earth as receptive, spacious, and sustaining. It carries and nourishes all living things by responding to Heaven rather than competing for leadership. The image of a mare conveys endurance and gentle perseverance. A noble person who insists on leading first may lose the way; one who follows the appropriate course finds direction. The references to southwest and northeast, companions and separation, emphasize the importance of position, relationship, and steadfastness.
03 屯 彖辭TUAN-U-03-01屯，剛柔始交而難生，動乎險中，大亨貞。雷雨之動滿盈，天造草昧，宜建侯而不寧。
Zhun concerns the difficulties of a beginning. Firm and yielding forces first meet, producing both the possibility of growth and immediate obstacles. Thunder and rain fill an unsettled world that has not yet found its order. The commentary does not treat the difficulty as a reason to abandon everything. Instead, it urges the establishment of leadership and organization while recognizing that the work of founding a stable order cannot be completed in comfort or haste.
04 蒙 彖辭TUAN-U-04-01蒙，山下有險，險而止，蒙。蒙亨，以亨行，時中也。匪我求童蒙，童蒙求我，志應也。初筮告，以剛中也。再三瀆，瀆則不告，瀆蒙也。蒙以養正，聖功也。
Meng describes ignorance as a condition in which danger is encountered and movement comes to a stop. Yet confusion can be transformed through education when teacher and student respond sincerely to each other. The learner must seek instruction rather than expect it to be imposed. A serious first question deserves an answer, whereas repeated disrespectful questioning undermines the relationship. Cultivating correctness from the beginning is presented as one of education’s most important achievements.
05 需 彖辭TUAN-U-05-01需，須也，險在前也；剛健而不陷，其義不困窮矣。需，有孚，光亨，貞吉，位乎天位，以正中也。利涉大川，往有功也。
Xu means waiting because danger lies ahead. Firm strength does not have to charge into a trap; its strength is shown by remaining steady without becoming trapped. Sincerity, clarity, and a central, appropriate position make waiting purposeful rather than passive. The reference to crossing a great river suggests that action can succeed when the conditions are right. The lesson is to distinguish deliberate preparation from fearful avoidance and reckless advance.
06 訟 彖辭TUAN-U-06-01訟，上剛下險，險而健，訟。訟，有孚，窒，惕，中吉，剛來而得中也。終凶，訟不可成也。利見大人，尚中正也。不利涉大川，入于淵也。
Song concerns disputes arising when danger and forceful assertion come together. Even a person who believes the claim is sincere may encounter obstruction and must remain alert. Stopping the argument before it becomes absolute is preferable to pursuing victory at any cost. The commentary praises an impartial and balanced authority and warns against undertaking a hazardous venture during conflict. Winning an endless quarrel is not the same as resolving it well.
07 師 彖辭TUAN-U-07-01師，眾也。貞，正也。能以眾正，可以王矣。剛中而應，行險而順，以此毒天下，而民從之，吉又何咎矣。
Shi refers to a multitude, particularly an army. The commentary connects legitimate command with correctness: a leader must direct the people toward a defensible purpose. Firmness in the central position is matched by the capacity to secure support. Military action is dangerous and causes suffering, yet its outcome is described as favorable when it is disciplined and undertaken for a proper cause. This is a statement of an ancient political ideal, not a modern endorsement of warfare.
08 比 彖辭TUAN-U-08-01比，吉也。比，輔也，下順從也。原筮，元永貞，无咎，以剛中也。不寧方來，上下應也。後夫凶，其道窮也。
Bi describes people drawing together and offering one another support. Good association requires a trustworthy center and a commitment that can last. People who feel insecure are drawn toward dependable leadership, while those who delay their response may lose the opportunity to join. The commentary explains the favorable judgment through mutual responsiveness between upper and lower positions. Closeness is valuable when it rests on constancy and appropriate relationships.
09 小畜 彖辭TUAN-U-09-01小畜，柔得位而上下應之，曰小畜。健而巽，剛中而志行，乃亨。密雲不雨，尚往也。自我西郊，施未行也。
Xiao Xu presents the accumulation of influence through a relatively small and yielding force. One yielding line occupies an effective position and receives the response of stronger lines. Inner strength works together with outward gentleness so that intentions can gradually be realized. Thick clouds gather without producing rain: the potential is there, but its benefits have not yet appeared. The image emphasizes restraint, accumulation, and the difference between preparation and fruition.
10 履 彖辭TUAN-U-10-01履，柔履剛也。說而應乎乾，是以履虎尾，不咥人，亨。剛中正，履帝位而不疚，光明也。
Lü concerns conduct under demanding conditions. A yielding force moves among stronger ones, and pleasure or harmony responds to the firmness of Heaven. For that reason, walking on a tiger’s tail does not necessarily end in a bite. Success comes through behavior that is appropriately measured rather than merely timid. The commentary also describes firm, centered leadership as bright and free from inward reproach, connecting conduct with moral responsibility.
11 泰 彖辭TUAN-U-11-01泰，小往大來，吉亨。則是天地交而萬物通也，上下交而其志同也。內陽而外陰，內健而外順，內君子而外小人，君子道長，小人道消也。
Tai portrays free communication between Heaven and Earth and between those above and below. When their influences meet, living things flourish and people can share their purposes. The firm and active principle is within, while the yielding and receptive principle is outside. The language of noble and petty people expresses an ancient moral-political order in which worthy conduct gains influence and destructive conduct loses ground. Prosperity depends on connection, not isolation.
12 否 彖辭TUAN-U-12-01否之匪人，不利君子貞，大往小來。則是天地不交而萬物不通也，上下不交而天下无邦也。內陰而外陽，內柔而外剛，內小人而外君子，小人道長，君子道消也。
Pi is the opposite condition: Heaven and Earth cease to exchange their influences, and communication between upper and lower levels fails. The large and constructive principle retreats while the small and obstructive one advances. The commentary pictures a political and social order that cannot function because its parts are disconnected. Worthy people are pushed aside while narrow interests gain strength. The warning concerns the consequences of isolation and the loss of mutual trust.
13 同人 彖辭TUAN-U-13-01同人，柔得位得中，而應乎乾，曰同人。同人曰，同人于野，亨。利涉大川，乾行也。文明以健，中正而應，君子正也。唯君子為能通天下之志。
Tong Ren describes fellowship that extends beyond a private faction. A yielding line holds a central position and responds to the strength of Heaven, making common purpose possible. To meet others openly in the fields is to seek cooperation without restricting it to one household or group. Inner clarity and outer strength should be held together through uprightness. The commentary gives the noble person the task of understanding and connecting the aspirations of people throughout the world.
14 大有 彖辭TUAN-U-14-01大有，柔得尊位，大中而上下應之，曰大有。其德剛健而文明，應乎天而時行，是以元亨。
Da You concerns abundance held in a responsible way. A yielding line occupies the honored central position and receives the response of strong lines above and below. Such abundance is not explained by possessions alone. Firm energy, clarity, and action suited to the proper time allow resources and influence to serve a larger order. The commentary associates great success with receptivity at the center and the support of others.
15 謙 彖辭TUAN-U-15-01謙，亨，天道下濟而光明，地道卑而上行。天道虧盈而益謙，地道變盈而流謙，鬼神害盈而福謙，人道惡盈而好謙。謙尊而光，卑而不可逾，君子之終也。
Qian explains why modesty can produce lasting achievement. Heaven lowers what is excessive and raises what is humble; Earth also redistributes what accumulates too high. The passage extends this pattern to spirits and human relations, saying that arrogance attracts opposition while humility wins support. A person in a high position becomes brighter through modesty, while a person in a low position gains dignity. Modesty is presented as a durable principle of conduct.
16 豫 彖辭TUAN-U-16-01豫，剛應而志行，順以動，豫。豫，順以動，故天地如之，而況建侯行師乎？天地以順動，故日月不過，而四時不忒；聖人以順動，則刑罰清而民服。豫之時義大矣哉！
Yu joins readiness, harmony, and movement that follows the appropriate time. Firm strength receives a response while action proceeds through receptiveness. The commentary compares this with the orderly movement of sun, moon, and seasons. Government and the use of punishment should also follow an intelligible order, allowing people to accept authority. Establishing political leadership or mobilizing an army is discussed within that ancient framework of timing and collective coordination.
17 隨 彖辭TUAN-U-17-01隨，剛來而下柔，動而說，隨。大亨貞，无咎，而天下隨時，隨之時義大矣哉！
Sui describes following without surrendering judgment. Firm strength places itself beneath the yielding, and movement is accompanied by a sense of ease. People follow because the relationship is responsive rather than merely coercive. The commentary stresses that the wider world changes with time; successful following therefore requires adjustment to circumstances while keeping to the right course. The meaning of timing is at the center of this hexagram.
18 蠱 彖辭TUAN-U-18-01蠱，剛上而柔下，巽而止，蠱。蠱，元亨，而天下治也。利涉大川，往有事也。先甲三日，後甲三日，終則有始，天行也。
Gu concerns the repair of what has become spoiled through neglect. Strong and yielding forces are positioned in a way that combines penetration with stopping, producing accumulated disorder. Yet the commentary treats the resulting problem as an opportunity for restoring order. Crossing a great river means undertaking necessary work. The days before and after the designated turning point suggest preparation, action, and review. An ending may also be a new beginning.
19 臨 彖辭TUAN-U-19-01臨，剛浸而長。說而順，剛中而應，大亨以正，天之道也。至于八月有凶，消不久也。
Lin describes a growing firm force that approaches and exercises influence. Joyful responsiveness and receptive conduct allow correct action to progress. The commentary also recalls a warning about danger in the eighth month: growth does not continue forever, and what is strong can begin to decline. A favorable period is therefore not a reason for complacency. Its value lies in understanding the cycle of increase and decrease.
20 觀 彖辭TUAN-U-20-01大觀在上，順而巽，中正以觀天下。觀，盥而不荐，有孚顒若，下觀而化也。觀天之神道，而四時不忒，聖人以神道設教，而天下服矣。
Guan is about observation and the influence of an example. Those in prominent positions should view the world with receptiveness, humility, and fairness. The ritual image of washing before the offering portrays a solemn attitude that invites respect even before the ceremony is complete. The commentary also considers the regular movement of the seasons. From such patterns, it explains how moral instruction can emerge through visible conduct rather than force alone.
21 噬嗑 彖辭TUAN-U-21-01頤中有物，曰噬嗑，噬嗑而亨。剛柔分，動而明，雷電合而章。柔得中而上行，雖不當位，利用獄也。
Shi He describes a blockage that must be bitten through before communication can resume. Firm and yielding forces are distinct, and vigorous movement is joined with clear perception, like thunder together with lightning. The commentary applies this image to deciding judicial cases, where determination must be matched by understanding. It is an ancient account of punishment and legal order, not a license for arbitrary severity.
22 賁 彖辭TUAN-U-22-01賁，亨；柔來而文剛，故亨。分剛上而文柔，故小利有攸往。剛柔交錯，天文也；文明以止，人文也。觀乎天文，以察時變；觀乎人文，以化成天下。
Bi concerns ornament, pattern, and culture. Yielding and firm forces decorate or temper one another, producing a limited but genuine capacity for progress. The patterned interplay of forces is called the order of Heaven; cultivated clarity and restraint become the order of human affairs. By observing natural patterns, people recognize changes in season and time. By attending to human culture, they develop customs and forms of education that shape social life.
23 剝 彖辭TUAN-U-23-01剝，剝也，柔變剛也。不利有攸往，小人長也。順而止之，觀象也。君子尚消息盈虛，天行也。
Bo portrays a process of stripping away. Yielding forces steadily replace the firm, making forward movement unwise. The commentary advises recognizing this tendency and knowing when to stop. The noble person observes increase and decline, fullness and emptiness, as recurring features of the natural order. When a structure is losing its support, forcing progress may only deepen its instability.
24 復 彖辭TUAN-U-24-01復亨；剛反，動而以順行，是以出入无疾，朋來无咎。反復其道，七日來復，天行也。利有攸往，剛長也。復其見天地之心乎？
Fu describes the return of firm, life-giving energy. Movement begins again and proceeds in a receptive way, so coming and going need not cause harm. The image of return after seven days expresses a recurring rhythm rather than a fixed promise of immediate success. The commentary associates the moment with growth and asks whether the renewal of life reveals something of the heart of Heaven and Earth.
25 无妄 彖辭TUAN-U-25-01无妄，剛自外來，而為主於內。動而健，剛中而應，大亨以正，天之命也。其匪正有眚，不利有攸往。无妄之往，何之矣？天命不佑，行矣哉？
Wu Wang means acting without falsehood or reckless intention. Firm strength enters and becomes the central influence, joining movement with vigor. Appropriate action is described as aligned with the order of Heaven. Yet an act that departs from correctness invites trouble, even if it appears energetic. The commentary questions the value of proceeding when conduct no longer agrees with the right course.
26 大畜 彖辭TUAN-U-26-01大畜，剛健篤實輝光，日新其德，剛上而尚賢。能止健，大正也。不家食吉，養賢也。利涉大川，應乎天也。
Da Xu concerns the storing and disciplining of great strength. Firmness, substantiality, and brightness are joined with daily renewal of character. The ability to restrain force is itself a major form of correctness. Honoring capable people and supporting their service beyond the private household bring the strength to public use. Crossing a great river represents undertaking significant work with resources and timing properly aligned.
27 頤 彖辭TUAN-U-27-01頤貞吉，養正則吉也。觀頤，觀其所養也；自求口實，觀其自養也。天地養萬物，聖人養賢，以及萬民；頤之時義大矣哉！
Yi examines nourishment in both its material and ethical senses. To nourish rightly means observing what is supported and how a person provides for themselves. Heaven and Earth sustain living things; a wise ruler supports the capable and cares for the wider population. The commentary treats nourishment as a serious responsibility: what people feed, cultivate, and maintain reveals their values.
28 大過 彖辭TUAN-U-28-01大過，大者過也。棟橈，本末弱也。剛過而中，巽而說行，利有攸往，乃亨。大過之時義大矣哉！
Da Guo describes a situation whose burden has exceeded its ordinary support. A roof beam bends because its ends are weak even though the middle is strong. The commentary does not say that all action must stop. Firmness can still be balanced by a yielding manner and a readiness to meet others. Extraordinary pressure calls for extraordinary discernment, especially about whether the supporting structure can bear the weight.
29 坎 彖辭TUAN-U-29-01習坎，重險也。水流而不盈，行險而不失其信。維心亨，乃以剛中也。行有尚，往有功也。天險不可升也，地險山川丘陵也，王公設險以守其國，坎之時用大矣哉！
Kan concerns repeated danger. Flowing water enters difficult places without losing its nature, offering an image of faithfulness under pressure. Inner strength remains central, and successful action requires an honest understanding of risk. The commentary also considers natural barriers in the sky and landscape, along with defensive works established by rulers. Danger must be understood, navigated, and sometimes used for protection.
30 離 彖辭TUAN-U-30-01離，麗也；日月麗乎天，百谷草木麗乎土，重明以麗乎正，乃化成天下。柔麗乎中正，故亨；是以畜牝牛吉也。
Li means attachment or dependence. The sun and moon are attached to Heaven, while grasses and grains depend on Earth. Light achieves its purpose when it is connected to what is right. A yielding line in a central and correct position allows clarity to operate without harshness. The image of raising a gentle cow connects successful cultivation with patient, receptive steadiness.
