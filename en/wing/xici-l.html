Appended Statements II Chapter 1 Unit 1XICI-L-01-01八卦成列，象在其中矣；因而重之，爻在其中矣；剛柔相推，變在其中矣；繫辭焉而命之，動在其中矣。吉凶悔吝者，生乎動者也。剛柔者，立本者也；變通者，趣時者也；吉凶者，貞勝者也。天地之道，貞觀者也；日月之道，貞明者也；天下之動，貞夫一者也。夫乾，確然示人易矣；夫坤，隤然示人簡矣。爻也者，效此者也；象也者，像此者也。爻象動乎內，吉凶見乎外，功業見乎變，聖人之情見乎辭。天地之大德曰生，聖人之大寶曰位，何以守位曰仁，何以聚人曰財，理財正辭，禁民為非曰義。Once the eight trigrams had been set in order, their images were contained within them. By doubling them, the lines of the hexagrams were contained within them. As the firm and yielding lines press upon one another, change is contained within them. By attaching statements and assigning meanings, movement is contained within them. Good fortune, misfortune, remorse, and regret arise from movement. Firmness and yielding establish the foundation; transformation and continuity follow the demands of the time; good and bad fortune are determined by steadfast correctness. The way of heaven and earth is revealed through constancy; the way of the sun and moon is revealed through constant illumination; and all movement under heaven is governed by constancy in the One. Qian clearly shows what is easy, while Kun gently shows what is simple. The lines imitate this, and the images represent it. The lines and images move within; good and bad fortune appear without; achievement becomes visible through change; and the intentions of the sages appear in the statements. The great virtue of heaven and earth is life-giving. The great treasure of the sage is their position. What preserves that position is humaneness; what gathers people is wealth. To manage wealth, rectify words, and prevent wrongdoing among the people is called righteousness.
Appended Statements II Chapter 2 Unit 1XICI-L-02-01古者包犧氏之王天下也，仰則觀象於天，俯則觀法於地，觀鳥獸之文，與地之宜，近取諸身，遠取諸物；於是始作八卦，以通神明之德，以類萬物之情。作結繩而為罔罟，以佃以漁，蓋取諸離。In ancient times, when Bao Xi ruled the world, the person looked upward and observed the images in heaven; the person looked downward and observed the patterns on earth. The person studied the markings of birds and beasts and the adaptations of each region. Near at hand the person took examples from their own body; farther away the person took examples from other things. The person then created the eight trigrams, in order to comprehend the virtue of spiritual intelligence and to classify the conditions of all things. The person knotted cords and made nets for hunting and fishing, taking the idea from Li.
Appended Statements II Chapter 2 Unit 2XICI-L-02-02包犧氏沒，神農氏作，斲木為耜，揉木為耒，耒耨之利，以教天下，蓋取諸益。日中為市，致天下之民，聚天下之貨，交易而退，各得其所，蓋取諸噬嗑。After Bao Xi died, Shen Nong arose. The person cut wood to make a ploughshare and bent wood to make a plough handle, teaching the world the benefit of ploughing and weeding; this was taken from Yi. At midday the person established markets, bringing together the people and goods of the world. They exchanged what they had and then withdrew, each obtaining what was appropriate; this was taken from Shi The person.
Appended Statements II Chapter 2 Unit 3XICI-L-02-03神農氏沒，黃帝、堯、舜氏作，通其變，使民不倦；神而化之，使民宜之；易窮則變，變則通，通則久；是以自天祐之，吉无不利。黃帝、堯、舜垂衣裳而天下治，蓋取諸乾坤。After Shen Nong died, Huang Di, Yao, and Shun arose. They carried change forward so that the people did not grow weary; they transformed things with spirit-like efficacy so that the people found them fitting. When the Yi reaches an extreme, it changes; through change it becomes continuous; through continuity it endures. Therefore, “Help comes from heaven; good fortune, nothing unfavorable.” Huang Di, Yao, and Shun let their upper and lower garments hang down, and the world was brought to order; this was taken from Qian and Kun.
Appended Statements II Chapter 2 Unit 4XICI-L-02-04刳木為舟，剡木為楫，舟楫之利以濟不通，致遠以利天下，蓋取諸渙。The passage makes this point: they hollowed wood to make boats and shaped wood to make oars. The usefulness of boats and oars opened routes that had been blocked and carried people far away for the benefit of the world; this was taken from Huan.
Appended Statements II Chapter 2 Unit 5XICI-L-02-05服牛乘馬，引重致遠以利天下，蓋取諸隨。重門擊柝以待暴客，蓋取諸豫。In everyday terms, the idea is this: they yoked oxen and rode horses, drawing heavy loads and reaching distant places for the benefit of the world; this was taken from Sui. They installed double gates and beat clappers to guard against violent intruders; this was taken from Yu.
Appended Statements II Chapter 2 Unit 6XICI-L-02-06斷木為杵，掘地為臼，臼杵之利，萬民以濟，蓋取諸小過。The passage makes this point: they cut wood to make pestles and dug the ground to make mortars. The usefulness of mortar and pestle benefited the multitude; this was taken from Xiao Guo.
Appended Statements II Chapter 2 Unit 7XICI-L-02-07弦木為弧，剡木為矢，弧矢之利，以威天下，蓋取諸睽。In everyday terms, the idea is this: they strung wood to make bows and sharpened wood to make arrows. The usefulness of bows and arrows inspired awe throughout the world; this was taken from Kui.
Appended Statements II Chapter 2 Unit 8XICI-L-02-08上古穴居而野處，後世聖人易之以宮室，上棟下宇，以待風雨，蓋取諸大壯。The passage makes this point: in high antiquity people lived in caves and stayed in the open. Later sages replaced these with houses and rooms, with a ridgepole above and eaves below to shelter them from wind and rain; this was taken from Da Zhuang.
Appended Statements II Chapter 2 Unit 9XICI-L-02-09古之葬者，厚衣之以薪，葬之中野，不封不樹，喪期无數，後世聖人易之以棺槨，蓋取諸大過。In everyday terms, the idea is this: in ancient burials, the dead were thickly wrapped in firewood and buried in the open country, without mound or planted trees, and there was no fixed mourning period. Later sages replaced this practice with inner and outer coffins; this was taken from Da Guo.
Appended Statements II Chapter 2 Unit 10XICI-L-02-10上古結繩而治，後世聖人易之以書契，百官以治，萬民以察，蓋取諸周易/夬。In everyday terms, the idea is this: in high antiquity, government was conducted with knotted cords. Later sages replaced them with written documents and tallies, so that officials could govern and the people could understand; this was taken from Guai.
Appended Statements II Chapter 3 Unit 1XICI-L-03-01是故易者，象也。象也者，像也。彖者，材也。爻也者，效天下之動者也。是故吉凶生而悔吝著也。Therefore the Yi consists of images, and an image is a likeness. The Judgments discuss the material of the hexagrams, while the lines imitate the movements of the world. From these arise good fortune and misfortune, and remorse and regret become manifest.
Appended Statements II Chapter 3 Unit 2XICI-L-03-02陽卦多陰，陰卦多陽，其故何也？陽卦奇，陰卦偶。其德行何也？陽一君而二民，君子之道也；陰二君而一民，小人之道也。In everyday terms, the idea is this: yang trigrams contain more yin lines, and yin trigrams contain more yang lines. Why is this? A yang trigram is odd, while a yin trigram is even. What does this imply about their conduct? In a yang trigram there is one ruler and two subjects; the way of the noble person. In a yin trigram there are two rulers and one subject; the way of the lesser person.
Appended Statements II Chapter 4 Unit 1XICI-L-04-01易曰：「憧憧往來，朋從爾思。」子曰：「天下何思何慮！天下同歸而殊途，一致而百慮，天下何思何慮！日往則月來，月往則日來，日月相推而明生焉。寒往則暑來，暑往則寒來，寒暑相推而歲成焉。往者，屈也；來者，信也。屈信相感而利生焉。尺蠖之屈，以求信也。龍蛇之蟄，以存身也。精義入神，以致用也。利用安身，以崇德也。過此以往，未之或知也。窮神知化，德之盛也。」The Yi says, “Restlessly coming and going, your friends follow your thoughts.” The Master said: “What thought or anxiety is there under heaven? Different paths lead to the same destination, and a hundred deliberations arrive at one result. What thought or anxiety is there under heaven? The sun departs and the moon comes; the moon departs and the sun comes. By their mutual succession, light is produced. Cold departs and heat comes; heat departs and cold comes. By their mutual succession, the year is completed. What departs contracts, and what comes expands; through the mutual influence of contraction and expansion, benefit arises. The inchworm contracts in order to extend; dragons and snakes hibernate in order to preserve themselves. Refining principles until they enter the subtle and applying them in practice brings usefulness; making use beneficial and settling oneself securely exalts virtue. Beyond this, perhaps nothing can be known. To exhaust the spiritual and understand transformation is the fullness of virtue.”.
Appended Statements II Chapter 4 Unit 2XICI-L-04-02易曰：「困于石，據于蒺蔾，入于其宮，不見其妻，凶。」子曰：「非所困而困焉，名必辱；非所據而據焉，身必危；既辱且危，死期將至，妻其可得見耶！」The Yi says, “Oppressed by rocks, the person rests on thorns. The person enters their house and does not see their wife: misfortune.” The Master said: “If one is oppressed by what should not oppress them, their name will surely be disgraced. If the person relies on what the person should not rely on, their person will surely be endangered. Once disgraced and endangered, death is near; how could the person see their wife?”.
Appended Statements II Chapter 4 Unit 3XICI-L-04-03易曰：「公用射隼于高墉之上，獲之，无不利。」子曰：「隼者，禽也；弓矢者，器也。射之者，人也。君子藏器於身，待時而動，何不利之有。動而不括，是以出而有獲，語成器而動者也。」The Yi says, “The duke shoots a falcon atop a high wall and obtains it; nothing is unfavorable.” The Master said: “The falcon is a bird; the bow and arrow are instruments; the shooter is a person. The noble person keeps the instrument concealed on their person and waits for the proper time to act. How could anything be unfavorable? Because the person acts without obstruction, the person goes forth and obtains their object. This speaks of preparing an instrument and then acting.”.
Appended Statements II Chapter 4 Unit 4XICI-L-04-04子曰：「小人不恥不仁，不畏不義，不見利不勸，不威不懲，小懲而大誡，此小人之福也。易曰：『履校滅趾，无咎。』此之謂也。善不積不足以成名，惡不積不足以滅身，小人以小善為无益而弗為也，以小惡為无傷而弗去也；故惡積而不可揜，罪大而不可解。易曰：『何校滅耳，凶。』」The Master said: “The lesser person is not ashamed of inhumanity and does not fear unrighteousness. Unless the person sees profit, the person is not encouraged; unless threatened, the person is not corrected. A small punishment that produces a great warning is a blessing for the lesser person. The Yi says, ‘Their feet are placed in the stocks and their toes are destroyed; no blame.’ This is what it means. If good deeds are not accumulated, they are insufficient to establish a reputation; if misfortune deeds are not accumulated, they are insufficient to destroy a person. The lesser person thinks a small good brings no benefit and does not do it; the person thinks a small misfortune causes no harm and does not abandon it. Therefore misfortune accumulates until it cannot be concealed, and guilt grows until it cannot be released. The Yi says, ‘The person wears a cangue and their ears are destroyed: misfortune.’”.
Appended Statements II Chapter 4 Unit 5XICI-L-04-05子曰：「危者，安其位者也。亡者，保其存者也。亂者，有其治者也。是故君子安而不忘危，存而不忘亡，治而不忘亂，是以身安而國家可保也。易曰：『其亡其亡，繫于苞桑。』」The Master said: “One who keeps danger in mind secures their position; one who keeps ruin in mind preserves what the person possesses; one who keeps disorder in mind maintains good order. Therefore, while safe, the noble person does not forget danger; while secure, the person does not forget ruin; while orderly, the person does not forget disorder. In this way their person is safe and their state and household can be preserved. The Yi says, ‘Will it perish? Will it perish? Bind it to a cluster of mulberry shoots.’”.
Appended Statements II Chapter 4 Unit 6XICI-L-04-06子曰：「德薄而位尊，知小而謀大，力小而任重，鮮不及矣。易曰：『鼎折足，覆公餗，其形渥，凶。』言不勝其任也。」The Master said: “When virtue is meager but position is honored, knowledge is slight but plans are great, strength is weak but responsibility is heavy, disaster is seldom avoided. The Yi says, ‘The legs of the cauldron break; the duke’s meal is overturned, and their body is soaked: misfortune.’ It means that the person is unequal to their charge.”.
Appended Statements II Chapter 4 Unit 7XICI-L-04-07子曰：「知幾其神乎！君子上交不諂，下交不瀆，其知幾乎！幾者，動之微，吉之先見者也。君子見幾而作，不俟終日。易曰：『介于石，不終日，貞吉。』介如石焉，寧用終日，斷可識矣。君子知微知彰，知柔知剛，萬夫之望。」The Master said: “Is not the person who knows the subtle beginnings almost spirit-like? In relations with superiors, the noble person does not flatter; in relations with inferiors, the person is not contemptuous. Does the person not understand subtle beginnings? A subtle beginning is the faint start of movement and the earliest appearance of good or bad fortune. The noble person sees it and acts without waiting for the day to end. The Yi says, ‘Firm as a rock, not waiting for the day to end; steadfastness brings good fortune.’ Firm as a rock; why wait for the day to end? The judgment can be known. The noble person knows the hidden and the manifest, the yielding and the firm; the person is the hope of the multitude.”.
Appended Statements II Chapter 4 Unit 8XICI-L-04-08子曰：「顏氏之子，其殆庶幾乎！有不善，未嘗不知；知之，未嘗復行也。易曰：『不遠復，无祇悔，元吉。』天地絪縕，萬物化醇；男女構精，萬物化生。易曰：『三人行，則損一人；一人行，則得其友。』言致一也。」The Master said: “The son of the Yan family came close indeed! Whenever the person did something wrong, the person always recognized it; once the person recognized it, the person never repeated it. The Yi says, ‘Returning from not far away, without reaching remorse: great good fortune.’ Heaven and earth mingle their vital forces, and all things are transformed and enriched. Male and female unite their essences, and the myriad creatures are transformed and born. The Yi says, ‘When three people travel, one is lost; when one person travels, the person finds a companion.’ This speaks of bringing things to unity.”.
Appended Statements II Chapter 4 Unit 9XICI-L-04-09子曰：「君子安其身而後動，易其心而後語，定其交而後求。君子脩此三者，故全也。危以動，則民不與也。懼以語，則民不應也。无交而求，則民不與也。莫之與，則傷之者至矣。易曰：『莫益之，或擊之。立心勿恒，凶。』」The Master said: “The noble person settles their own person before taking action, makes their mind easy before speaking, and establishes their relationships before making requests. By cultivating these three things, the noble person remains whole. If the person acts from danger, the people will not join them. If the person speaks from fear, the people will not respond. If the person asks without established relationships, the people will not support them. When no one supports them, those who harm them will arrive. The Yi says, ‘No one benefits them; someone may strike them. The person does not keep their heart constant: misfortune.’”.
Appended Statements II Chapter 5 Unit 1XICI-L-05-01子曰：「乾坤，其易之門邪！乾，陽物也；坤，陰物也。陰陽合德，而剛柔有體，以體天地之撰，以通神明之德。」其稱名也，雜而不越，於稽其類，其衰世之意邪！夫易，彰往而察來，而微顯闡幽，開而當名辨物，正言斷辭，則備矣。其稱名也小，其取類也大，其旨遠，其辭文，其言曲而中，其事肆而隱，因貳以濟民行，以明失得之報。The Master said: “Are Qian and Kun not the gateway of the Yi? Qian is the yang entity; Kun is the yin entity. Yin and yang unite their powers, while firmness and yielding take form, embodying the operations of heaven and earth and communicating the virtue of spiritual intelligence.” The names used are varied but do not exceed their proper bounds. When their categories are examined, do they not reveal concern about an age of decline? The Yi makes the past manifest and examines what is coming; it makes the subtle visible and discloses what is hidden. It opens things up, assigns fitting names, distinguishes beings, and determines its statements with correct words; therefore it is complete. Its names are small, but the categories it draws upon are great. Its meaning reaches far, its language is refined, its words are indirect yet hit the mark, and its matters are openly set forth yet contain hidden depth. It uses doubt to aid the conduct of the people and to clarify the consequences of loss and gain.

Appended Statements II Chapter 6 Unit 2XICI-L-06-02履，德之基也。謙，德之柄也。復，德之本也。恒，德之固也。損，德之脩也。益，德之裕也。困，德之辨也。井，德之地也。巽，德之制也。The passage makes this point: lu is the foundation of virtue; Qian is the handle of virtue; Fu is the root of virtue; Heng is the firmness of virtue; Sun is the cultivation of virtue; Yi is the abundance of virtue; Kun is the discernment of virtue; Jing is the ground of virtue; and Xun is the regulation of virtue.
Appended Statements II Chapter 6 Unit 3XICI-L-06-03履，和而至。謙，尊而光。復，小而辨於物。恒，雜而不厭。損，先難而後易。益，長裕而不没。困，窮而通。井，居其所而遷。巽，稱而隱。In everyday terms, the idea is this: lu is harmonious and reaches its goal. Qian is honored and shines. Fu is small yet distinguishes things. Heng is involved in many circumstances without becoming weary. Sun is difficult first and easy afterward. Yi grows in abundance without contrivance. Kun reaches an extremity yet finds a way through. Jing remains in its place yet conveys benefit. Xun weighs what is fitting while remaining unobtrusive.
Appended Statements II Chapter 6 Unit 4XICI-L-06-04履，以和行。謙，以制禮。復，以自知。恒，以一德。損，以遠害。益，以興利。困，以寡怨。井，以辯義。巽，以行權。The passage makes this point: lu harmonizes conduct. Qian regulates ritual. Fu enables self-knowledge. Heng unifies virtue. Sun keeps harm at a distance. Yi promotes benefit. Kun reduces resentment. Jing distinguishes what is right. Xun exercises discretion.
Appended Statements II Chapter 7 Unit 1XICI-L-07-01易之為書也不可遠，為道也屢遷；變動不居，周流六虛，上下无常，剛柔相易，不可為典要，唯變所適。其出入以度，外內使知懼。又明於憂患與故，无有師保，如臨父母。初率其辭而揆其方，既有典常，苟非其人，道不虛行。The Yi as a book must not be kept at a distance, for its way repeatedly changes. Its transformations do not remain in one place but circulate through the six positions. Rising and descending have no fixed rule; the firm and yielding lines exchange places. It cannot be confined to a rigid model, for only change is appropriate. Its coming and going follow measure, causing those outside and within to understand caution. It also clarifies anxiety, serious trouble, and their causes. Though one has no teacher or guardian, it is as if one were approaching one’s parents. At first, follow its words and consider their direction; then its constant principles become evident. Unless there is the right person, however, the way will not operate by itself.
Appended Statements II Chapter 7 Unit 2XICI-L-07-02易之為書也，原始要終以為質也。六爻相雜，唯其時物也。其初難知，其上易知，本末也。初辭擬之，卒成之終。若夫雜物撰德，辨是與非，則非其中爻不備。噫，亦要存亡吉凶，則居可知矣。知者觀其彖辭，則思過半矣。In everyday terms, the idea is this: the Yi as a book takes the origin as its starting point and the end as its completion. The six lines are intermingled, each according to its time and substance. The first line is difficult to understand, while the top line is easy, for they are beginning and end. The statement for the first line sketches the matter; the final line completes it. To mix the kinds of things, develop their qualities, and distinguish right from wrong cannot be fully accomplished without the middle lines. Ah! If one seeks only to understand survival and ruin, good fortune and misfortune, these can be known from one’s position. A wise person who observes the Judgment will understand more than half.
Appended Statements II Chapter 8 Unit 1XICI-L-08-01二與四同功而異位，其善不同：二多譽，四多懼，近也。柔之為道，不利近者，其要無咎，其用柔中也。三與五同功而異位，其遇不同：三多凶，五多功，貴賤之等也。其柔危，其剛勝邪？The second and fourth lines have the same function but different positions, so their good qualities differ: the second receives much praise, while the fourth has much fear because it is close to the ruler. The way of the yielding is not beneficial when close; its essential aim is to avoid blame through yielding and centrality. The third and fifth lines have the same function but different positions, so their encounters differ: the third has much misfortune, while the fifth has much achievement, because their ranks are noble and lowly. Is the yielding therefore endangered and the firm victorious?
Appended Statements II Chapter 8 Unit 2XICI-L-08-02《易》之為書也，廣大悉備：有天道焉，有人道焉，有地道焉，兼三材而兩之，故六。六者，非它也，三材之道也。道有變動，故曰爻；爻有等，故曰物；物相雜，故曰文；文不當，故吉凶生焉。The passage makes this point: the Yi as a book is broad, great, and all-embracing. It contains the way of heaven, the way of humanity, and the way of earth. It combines these Three Powers and doubles them; therefore there are six lines. The six are nothing other than the way of the Three Powers. The way contains change and movement, so the lines are called yao. The lines have ranks, so they are treated as distinct things. The things are intermingled, producing patterns. When a pattern is out of place, good fortune or misfortune arises.
Appended Statements II Chapter 8 Unit 3XICI-L-08-03《易》之興也，其當殷之末世，周之盛德邪！當文王與紂之事邪！是故其辭危，危者使平，易者使傾，其道甚大，百物不廢，懼以終始，其要无咎；此之謂易之道也。In everyday terms, the idea is this: did the Yi arise at the end of the Shang dynasty, when the virtue of Zhou was flourishing? Did it concern the events of King Wen and the tyrant Zhou? For this reason its statements are filled with danger. Awareness of danger brings peace; complacency leads to collapse. Its way is very great and omits none of all things. Caution at both beginning and end is essential, so that one remains without blame. This is called the way of the Yi.
Appended Statements II Chapter 9 Unit 1XICI-L-09-01夫乾，天下之至健也；德行恒易以知險。夫坤，天下之至順也；德行恒簡以知阻。能說諸心，能研諸侯之慮，定天下之吉凶，成天下之亹亹者。是故變化云為，吉事有祥，象事知器，占事知來。Qian is the strongest force under heaven; its constant conduct is easy, yet it understands danger. Kun is the most compliant force under heaven; its constant conduct is simple, yet it understands obstruction. They can delight the heart and examine the concerns of those in authority, determine good fortune and misfortune under heaven, and bring the world’s endeavors to completion. Therefore changes, transformations, words, and actions contain signs of fortunate events: by observing images one understands implements, and by divination one understands what is to come.
Appended Statements II Chapter 9 Unit 2XICI-L-09-02天地設位，聖人成能；人謀鬼謀，百姓與能。八卦以象告，爻彖以情言，剛柔雜居，而吉凶可見矣。變動以利言，吉凶以情遷。是故愛惡相攻而吉凶生，遠近相取而悔吝生，情偽相感而利害生。凡易之情，近而不相得，則凶或害之，悔且吝。Heaven and earth establish their positions, and the sages bring their capacities to completion. Human counsels and counsels sought from spirits allow the people to share in that capacity. The eight trigrams communicate through images; the line statements and Judgments speak through the conditions they express. Firm and yielding lines dwell together, so good fortune and misfortune can be seen. Changes and movements are discussed in terms of benefit; good fortune and misfortune shift according to circumstances. Therefore, when love and hate contend, good fortune and misfortune arise; when what is distant and what is near affect one another, remorse and regret arise; when truth and falsehood influence each other, benefit and harm arise. In every situation shown by the Yi, if things are near yet fail to accord, misfortune or harm follows, together with remorse and regret.
Appended Statements II Chapter 9 Unit 3XICI-L-09-03將叛者其辭慚，中心疑者其辭枝；吉人之辭寡，躁人之辭多；誣善之人其辭游，失其守者其辭屈。The passage makes this point: the words of one who is about to betray show shame. The words of one whose heart is doubtful are branching and evasive. The words of a fortunate person are few; the words of an agitated person are many. The words of one who falsely accuses the good are wandering; the words of one who has lost integrity are twisted.
