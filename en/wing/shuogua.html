Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 1 Unit 1SHUOGUA-01-01昔者，聖人之作《易》也，幽贊於神明而生蓍。參天兩地而倚數，觀變於陰陽而立卦；發揮於剛柔而生爻；和順於道德而理於義；窮理盡性以至於命。Anciently, when the sages made the Yi, in order to give mysterious assistance to the spiritual Intelligences, they produced the rules for the use of the divining plant. The number 3 was assigned to heaven, 2 to earth, and from these came the other numbers. They contemplated the changes in the divided and undivided lines by the process of manipulating the stalks, and formed the trigrams; from the movements that took place in the strong and weak lines, they produced their teaching about the separate lines. There ensued a harmonious conformity to the course of duty and to virtue, with a discrimination of what was right in each particular case. They therefore made an exhaustive discrimination of what was right, and effected the complete development of every nature, till they arrived in the Yi at what was appointed for it by Heaven.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 2 Unit 1SHUOGUA-02-01昔者，聖人之作《易》也，將以順性命之理。是以立天之道，曰陰與陽；立地之道，曰柔與剛；立人之道，曰仁與義。兼三才而兩之，故易六畫而成卦。分陰分陽，迭用柔剛，故易六位而成章。Anciently, when the sages made the Yi, it was with the design that its figures should be in conformity with the principles underlying the natures of men and things, and the ordinances for them appointed by Heaven. With this view they exhibited in them the way of heaven, calling the lines yin and yang; the way of earth, calling them the weak or soft and the strong or hard; and the way of men, under the names of benevolence and righteousness. Each trigram embraced those three Powers; and, being repeated, its full form consisted of six lines. A distinction was made of the places assigned to the yin and yang lines, which were variously occupied, now by the strong and now by the weak forms, and therefore the figure of each hexagram was completed.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 3 Unit 1SHUOGUA-03-01天地定位，山澤通氣，雷風相薄，水火不相射。八卦相錯，數往者順，知來者逆。是故易逆數也。The trigrams are described here in this way: the symbols of heaven and earth received their determinate positions; those for mountains and collections of water interchanged their influences; those for thunder and wind excited each other the more; and those for water and fire did each other no harm. Then among these eight symbols there was a mutual communication. The numbering of the past is a natural process; the knowledge of the coming is anticipation. Therefore in the Yi we have both anticipation and the natural process.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 4 Unit 1SHUOGUA-04-01雷以動之，風以散之，雨以潤之，日以烜之，艮以止之，兌以說之，乾以君之，坤以藏之。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: thunder sets things in motion; wind disperses them; rain moistens them; the sun warms them; Gen stops them; Dui gives them joy; Qian rules them; and Kun stores them.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 5 Unit 1SHUOGUA-05-01帝出乎震，齊乎巽，相見乎離，致役乎坤，說言乎兌，戰乎乾，勞乎坎，成言乎艮。The trigrams are described here in this way: the divine power comes forth in Zhen, brings all things into order in Xun, makes them visible in Li, receives their service in Kun, brings joy in Dui, struggles in Qian, labors in Kan, and completes them in Gen.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 5 Unit 2SHUOGUA-05-02萬物出乎震，震，東方也。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: all things emerge in Zhen, which corresponds to the east.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 5 Unit 3SHUOGUA-05-03齊乎巽，巽，東南也，齊也者，言萬物之潔齊也。The trigrams are described here in this way: all things are brought into order in Xun, which corresponds to the southeast. Here, order means that all things become clean and evenly arranged.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 5 Unit 4SHUOGUA-05-04離也者，明也，萬物皆相見，南方之卦也。聖人南面而聽天下，嚮明而治，蓋取諸此也。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: li means brightness. It is the trigram of the south, where all things become visible to one another. The sage faces south, listens to the world, and governs toward the light; this image comes from Li.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 5 Unit 5SHUOGUA-05-05坤也者，地也，萬物皆致養焉，故曰致役乎坤。The trigrams are described here in this way: kun means earth. All things receive nourishment from it; therefore all things render their service in Kun.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 5 Unit 6SHUOGUA-05-06兌，正秋也。萬物之所說也。故曰說；言乎兌。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: dui corresponds to autumn, the season when all things rejoice; therefore joy is expressed in Dui.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 5 Unit 7SHUOGUA-05-07戰乎乾，乾，西北之卦也。言陰陽相薄也。The trigrams are described here in this way: the struggle takes place in Qian, the trigram of the northwest, where yin and yang press against each other.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 5 Unit 8SHUOGUA-05-08坎者，水也，正北方之卦也，勞卦也，萬物之所歸也，故曰勞乎坎。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: kan means water and corresponds to the north. It is the trigram of labor, where all things return; therefore labor is expressed in Kan.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 5 Unit 9SHUOGUA-05-09艮，東北之卦也，萬物之所成，終而所成始也，故曰成言乎艮。The trigrams are described here in this way: gen corresponds to the northeast. There all things complete their ending and begin again; therefore completion is expressed in Gen.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 6 Unit 1SHUOGUA-06-01神也者。妙萬物而為言者也。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: here, spirit means the subtle power through which all things are transformed and made effective.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 6 Unit 2SHUOGUA-06-02動萬物者，莫疾乎雷。The trigrams are described here in this way: nothing moves all things more quickly than thunder.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 6 Unit 3SHUOGUA-06-03橈萬物者，莫疾乎風。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: nothing bends and disperses all things more effectively than wind.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 6 Unit 4SHUOGUA-06-04燥萬物者，莫熯乎火。The trigrams are described here in this way: nothing dries all things more thoroughly than fire.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 6 Unit 5SHUOGUA-06-05說萬物者，莫說乎澤。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: nothing brings greater joy to all things than a lake or marsh.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 6 Unit 6SHUOGUA-06-06潤萬物者，莫潤乎水。The trigrams are described here in this way: nothing moistens and enriches all things more fully than water.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 6 Unit 7SHUOGUA-06-07終萬物、始萬物者，莫盛乎艮。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: nothing completes all things and begins them again more fully than Gen.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 6 Unit 8SHUOGUA-06-08故水火相逮，雷風不相悖，山澤通氣，然後能變化，既成萬物也。The trigrams are described here in this way: water and fire support each other; thunder and wind do not oppose each other; mountain and lake exchange vital energy. Through these relationships, change becomes possible and all things are completed.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 7 Unit 1SHUOGUA-07-01乾，健也。坤，順也。震，動也。巽，入也。坎，陷也。離，麗也。艮，止也。兌，說也。The trigrams are described here in this way: qian means strength; Kun, receptiveness; Zhen, movement; Xun, entering; Kan, danger; Li, attachment and brightness; Gen, stopping; and Dui, joy.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 8 Unit 1SHUOGUA-08-01乾為馬，坤為牛，震為龍，巽為雞，坎為豕，離為雉，艮為狗，兌為羊。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: qian corresponds to the horse; Kun to the ox; Zhen to the dragon; Xun to the rooster; Kan to the pig; Li to the pheasant; Gen to the dog; and Dui to the sheep.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 9 Unit 1SHUOGUA-09-01乾為首，坤為腹，震為足，巽為股，坎為耳，離為目，艮為手，兌為口。The trigrams are described here in this way: qian corresponds to the head; Kun to the abdomen; Zhen to the feet; Xun to the thighs; Kan to the ears; Li to the eyes; Gen to the hands; and Dui to the mouth.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 10 Unit 1SHUOGUA-10-01乾，天也，故稱乎父；坤，地也，故稱乎母； 震一索而得男，故謂之長男；巽一索而得女，故謂之長女； 坎再索而得男，故謂之中男；離再索而得女，故謂之中女； 艮三索而得男，故謂之少男；兌三索而得女，故謂之少女。The trigrams are described here in this way: qian is heaven and is called the father; Kun is earth and is called the mother. Zhen receives the first yang line and is called the eldest son. Xun receives the first yin line and is called the eldest daughter. Kan receives the second yang line and is called the middle son. Li receives the second yin line and is called the middle daughter. Gen receives the third yang line and is called the youngest son. Dui receives the third yin line and is called the youngest daughter.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 1SHUOGUA-11-01乾為天、為圜、為君、為父、為玉、為金、為寒、為冰、為大赤、為良馬、為老馬、為瘠馬、為駁馬、為木果。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: qian corresponds to heaven, a circle, a ruler, a father, jade, metal, cold, ice, deep red, a good horse, an old horse, a thin horse, a piebald horse, and tree fruit.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 2SHUOGUA-11-02坤為地、為母、為布、為釜、為吝嗇、為均、為子母牛、為大輿、為文、為眾、為柄。The trigrams are described here in this way: kun corresponds to earth, a mother, cloth, a cooking pot, frugality, evenness, a cow with its calf, a large wagon, pattern, a multitude, and a handle.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 3SHUOGUA-11-03其於地也為黑。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: among kinds of earth, Kun corresponds to black soil.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 4SHUOGUA-11-04震為雷、為龍、為玄黃、為敷、為大涂、為長子、為決躁、為蒼筤竹、為萑葦。The trigrams are described here in this way: zhen corresponds to thunder, the dragon, dark yellow, spreading growth, a great road, the eldest son, decisiveness and restlessness, young green bamboo, and reeds.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 5SHUOGUA-11-05其於馬也，為善鳴、為馵足，為的顙。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: among horses, Zhen corresponds to one that neighs well, has a marked hind foot, or has a white forehead.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 6SHUOGUA-11-06其於稼也，為反生。The trigrams are described here in this way: among crops, Zhen corresponds to plants that return to life.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 7SHUOGUA-11-07其究為健，為蕃鮮。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: at its fullest development, Zhen becomes vigorous, lush, and fresh.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 8SHUOGUA-11-08巽為木、為風、為長女、為繩直、為工、為白、為長、為高、為進退、為不果、為臭。The trigrams are described here in this way: xun corresponds to wood, wind, the eldest daughter, a straight cord, craftsmanship, white, length, height, advance and retreat, indecision, and scent.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 9SHUOGUA-11-09其於人也，為寡髮、為廣顙、為多白眼、為近利市三倍。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: among people, Xun corresponds to sparse hair, a broad forehead, much white in the eyes, and pursuing nearby profit, even seeking threefold gain in the market.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 10SHUOGUA-11-10其究為躁卦。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: at its fullest development, Xun becomes the trigram of restlessness.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 11SHUOGUA-11-11坎為水、為溝瀆、為隱伏、為矯輮、為弓輪。The trigrams are described here in this way: kan corresponds to water, channels and ditches, concealment, bending and straightening, the bow, and the wheel.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 12SHUOGUA-11-12其於人也，為加憂、為心病、為耳痛、為血卦、為赤。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: among people, Kan corresponds to increased worry, illness of the heart, ear pain, blood, and the color red.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 13SHUOGUA-11-13其於馬也，為美脊、為亟心、為下首、為薄蹄、為曳。The trigrams are described here in this way: among horses, Kan corresponds to a fine back, an anxious heart, a lowered head, thin hooves, and a dragging gait.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 14SHUOGUA-11-14其於輿也，為多眚、為通、為月、為盜。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: among vehicles, Kan corresponds to one with many defects. It also corresponds to penetration, the moon, and a thief.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 15SHUOGUA-11-15其於木也，為堅多心。The trigrams are described here in this way: among trees, Kan corresponds to wood that is hard and has much inner pith.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 16SHUOGUA-11-16離為火、為日、為電、為中女、為甲冑、為戈兵。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: li corresponds to fire, the sun, lightning, the middle daughter, armor and helmets, spears, and weapons.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 17SHUOGUA-11-17其於人也，為大腹、為乾卦、為鱉、為蟹、為蠃、為蚌、為龜。The trigrams are described here in this way: among people and creatures, Li corresponds to a large belly, dryness, the soft-shelled turtle, crab, snail, mussel, and tortoise.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 18SHUOGUA-11-18其於木也，為科上槁。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: among trees, Li corresponds to a hollow trunk with a dry upper part.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 19SHUOGUA-11-19艮為山、為徑路、為小石、為門闕、為果蓏、為閽寺、為指、為狗、為鼠、為黔喙之屬。The trigrams are described here in this way: gen corresponds to a mountain, a narrow path, a small stone, a gateway, fruits and gourds, a gatekeeper or eunuch, a finger, a dog, a rat, and dark-billed creatures.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 20SHUOGUA-11-20其於木也，為堅多節。The trigrams are described here in this way: among trees, Gen corresponds to wood that is hard and has many joints.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 21SHUOGUA-11-21兌為澤、為少女、為巫、為口舌、為毀折、為附決。This passage describes the trigrams as follows: dui corresponds to a lake or marsh, the youngest daughter, a ritual medium, the mouth and tongue, breaking and damage, and fruit that is attached but ready to fall.
Discussion of Trigrams Chapter 11 Unit 22SHUOGUA-11-22其於地也，為剛鹵，為妾、為羊。The trigrams are described here in this way: among kinds of earth, Dui corresponds to hard salty ground. It also corresponds to a concubine and a sheep.
