Explaining the Trigrams / SHUOGUA
SHUOGUA · 47 units · text_version: yizhuan-en-2.0.0 · review_status: draft

第一章 第 1 段SHUOGUA-01-01昔者，聖人之作《易》也，幽贊於神明而生蓍。參天兩地而倚數，觀變於陰陽而立卦；發揮於剛柔而生爻；和順於道德而理於義；窮理盡性以至於命。The sages are said to have used yarrow-stalk divination to investigate hidden patterns. Numerical relationships of Heaven and Earth, changes between yin and yang, and the interplay of firm and yielding lines gave rise to trigrams and individual lines. The text describes this system as a way of connecting reflection on nature with moral conduct and a sense of life's larger order.
第二章 第 1 段SHUOGUA-02-01昔者，聖人之作《易》也，將以順性命之理。是以立天之道，曰陰與陽；立地之道，曰柔與剛；立人之道，曰仁與義。兼三才而兩之，故易六畫而成卦。分陰分陽，迭用柔剛，故易六位而成章。The three powers are Heaven, Earth, and humanity. Heaven is discussed through yin and yang, Earth through yielding and firmness, and human affairs through humaneness and rightness. By treating each power in two aspects, the text accounts for the six lines of a hexagram. This is an interpretive design, not a claim that human relationships have only six possible forms.
第三章 第 1 段SHUOGUA-03-01天地定位，山澤通氣，雷風相薄，水火不相射。八卦相錯，數往者順，知來者逆。是故易逆數也。Heaven and Earth take their positions; mountains and lakes exchange influences; thunder and wind interact; water and fire stand in complementary tension. The eight trigrams are arranged together to consider patterns already known and changes yet to emerge. The text contrasts reviewing the past with reasoning toward the future, explaining the distinctive direction of interpretation in the Changes.
第四章 第 1 段SHUOGUA-04-01雷以動之，風以散之，雨以潤之，日以烜之，艮以止之，兌以說之，乾以君之，坤以藏之。Thunder stirs things to movement, wind disperses, rain nourishes, and sunlight warms. Gen brings a halt, Dui brings joy, Qian provides direction, and Kun receives and stores. The passage identifies differing functions within one evolving natural process. No single force performs every task; generation, distribution, growth, completion, and rest each require their proper role.
第五章 第 1 段SHUOGUA-05-01帝出乎震，齊乎巽，相見乎離，致役乎坤，說言乎兌，戰乎乾，勞乎坎，成言乎艮。The passage follows an ordered cycle through the eight trigrams. Life begins to stir in Zhen, becomes orderly in Xun, appears in the brightness of Li, and is nurtured through Kun. Dui marks joy in maturity, Qian contesting forces, Kan labor and return, and Gen completion that permits another beginning. This is a symbolic arrangement of time and direction.
第五章 第 2 段SHUOGUA-05-02萬物出乎震，震，東方也。Zhen is assigned to the east, the direction where things begin to emerge. The statement belongs to a larger directional sequence connecting the eight trigrams with changes in the natural year. Its emphasis is the first stirring of life rather than a rule about the geographical east in every context.
第五章 第 3 段SHUOGUA-05-03齊乎巽，巽，東南也，齊也者，言萬物之潔齊也。Xun is assigned to the southeast, where developing things become orderly and distinct. The word for becoming aligned suggests growth attaining a coordinated form. Within the directional sequence, this is a stage after initial emergence in Zhen and before the full visibility associated with Li.
第五章 第 4 段SHUOGUA-05-04離也者，明也，萬物皆相見，南方之卦也。聖人南面而聽天下，嚮明而治，蓋取諸此也。Li signifies brightness and is placed in the south. In daylight, things become visible to one another. The traditional image of a ruler facing south to govern draws on this connection with clarity and illumination. The passage uses direction and light to express a model of public awareness and effective rule.
第五章 第 5 段SHUOGUA-05-05坤也者，地也，萬物皆致養焉，故曰致役乎坤。Kun represents the Earth as the receptive ground on which living things are nourished. The phrase describes the work of life being sustained by the capacity of the ground to carry and support it. The point is not inactivity but the ongoing labor of giving things the conditions in which they can mature.
第五章 第 6 段SHUOGUA-05-06兌，正秋也。萬物之所說也。故曰說；言乎兌。Dui corresponds to the height of autumn, when crops and other products of the year reach maturity. It is associated with pleasure or satisfaction. In the seasonal pattern, joy follows the effort of growth and gathering; the text treats that delight as one recognizable feature of the Dui trigram.
第五章 第 7 段SHUOGUA-05-07戰乎乾，乾，西北之卦也。言陰陽相薄也。Qian is placed in the northwest in this directional arrangement. The phrase about battle refers to yin and yang drawing against each other as the seasons turn. It depicts an energetic change of balance rather than a literal prediction of war in that geographic direction.
第五章 第 8 段SHUOGUA-05-08坎者，水也，正北方之卦也，勞卦也，萬物之所歸也，故曰勞乎坎。Kan signifies water and is placed in the north. It is connected with labor, difficulty, and the return of living things to a hidden or resting condition. Within the yearly cycle, northern darkness and winter activity suggest preservation through a demanding phase.
第五章 第 9 段SHUOGUA-05-09艮，東北之卦也，萬物之所成，終而所成始也，故曰成言乎艮。Gen occupies the northeast. It is the position where things finish one phase and become ready to begin another. Stopping is therefore not the opposite of all growth: a genuine ending may also be the condition for renewal. The passage makes Gen the hinge between completion and fresh emergence.
第六章 第 1 段SHUOGUA-06-01神也者。妙萬物而為言者也。The word shen, often translated as spirit or the marvelous, is used here to describe the extraordinary subtlety of transformation. The passage is concerned with effects that are apparent even when their workings cannot easily be traced. It should be understood within the text's philosophical and cosmological vocabulary.
第六章 第 2 段SHUOGUA-06-02動萬物者，莫疾乎雷。No force brings such sudden movement among things as thunder. Its sound and impact make it a vivid emblem of the start of change. In the sequence of natural forces, thunder represents activation rather than the full development or completion that follows.
第六章 第 3 段SHUOGUA-06-03橈萬物者，莫疾乎風。Wind is presented as the force that most readily bends, stirs, and disperses things. Its movement is less abrupt than thunder but reaches widely. The classical comparison uses wind to show how influence can enter many places without needing a single direct blow.
第六章 第 4 段SHUOGUA-06-04燥萬物者，莫熯乎火。Fire is described as the strongest of these familiar forces for drying things. The passage identifies a particular observable action of heat, not the entire meaning of Li. Such simple natural observations provide the vocabulary for the larger system of corresponding images.
第六章 第 5 段SHUOGUA-06-05說萬物者，莫說乎澤。The lake or marsh is connected with delight and refreshment. Dui's association with joy grows out of the way water-filled lowlands can sustain and enliven living things. The passage names one recognizable quality of that image as part of the trigram's broader symbolic range.
第六章 第 6 段SHUOGUA-06-06潤萬物者，莫潤乎水。Water is named the great moistening and nourishing force. Its ability to soak the earth and support growth supplies the comparison. The text gives water this particular function within the complementary forces of nature, while other passages also use it as an image of danger and depth.
第六章 第 7 段SHUOGUA-06-07終萬物、始萬物者，莫盛乎艮。Gen has a distinctive role at the boundary between ending and beginning. Mountains and stopping express completion, but completion also allows another course to start. In the broader passage, Gen closes a cycle without implying that transformation itself has ceased forever.
第六章 第 8 段SHUOGUA-06-08故水火相逮，雷風不相悖，山澤通氣，然後能變化，既成萬物也。Water and fire interact, thunder and wind do not cancel each other, and mountains and lakes exchange influence. Together these contrasting forces make transformation possible and bring things to completion. The passage stresses coexistence and coordination rather than reducing nature to a single element.
第七章 第 1 段SHUOGUA-07-01乾，健也。坤，順也。震，動也。巽，入也。坎，陷也。離，麗也。艮，止也。兌，說也。This sentence offers a concise glossary for the eight trigrams: Qian is vigor, Kun receptivity, Zhen movement, Xun penetration, Kan danger or a pit, Li attachment, Gen stopping, and Dui delight. These are central associations for interpreting images; they are not complete definitions for every use of each trigram.
第八章 第 1 段SHUOGUA-08-01乾為馬，坤為牛，震為龍，巽為雞，坎為豕，離為雉，艮為狗，兌為羊。The trigrams are matched with animals: Qian with the horse, Kun the ox, Zhen the dragon, Xun the rooster, Kan the pig, Li the pheasant, Gen the dog, and Dui the sheep. Such correspondences illustrate how the same eight symbols were used to organize several different fields of observation.
第九章 第 1 段SHUOGUA-09-01乾為首，坤為腹，震為足，巽為股，坎為耳，離為目，艮為手，兌為口。A second series connects each trigram with a body part. Qian represents the head; Kun, the abdomen; Zhen, the feet; Xun, the thighs; Kan, the ears; Li, the eyes; Gen, the hands; and Dui, the mouth. These are conventional symbolic associations in the text, not anatomical or medical diagnoses.
第十章 第 1 段SHUOGUA-10-01乾，天也，故稱乎父；坤，地也，故稱乎母；震一索而得男，故謂之長男；巽一索而得女，故謂之長女；坎再索而得男，故謂之中男；離再索而得女，故謂之中女；艮三索而得男，故謂之少男；兌三索而得女，故謂之少女。The eight trigrams are arranged as a family: Qian and Kun stand for father and mother. Zhen and Xun represent eldest son and daughter, Kan and Li the middle son and daughter, and Gen and Dui the youngest son and daughter. The names express how yin and yang lines are situated within the three-line figures.
第十一章 第 1 段SHUOGUA-11-01乾為天、為圜、為君、為父、為玉、為金、為寒、為冰、為大赤、為良馬、為老馬、為瘠馬、為駁馬、為木果。Qian is associated with heaven, circularity, the ruler and father, jade and metal, cold and ice, deep red, several kinds of horse, and fruit growing on trees. This catalog records an expansive system of images. Its purpose is to show how firmness and height can be recognized through diverse objects.
第十一章 第 2 段SHUOGUA-11-02坤為地、為母、為布、為釜、為吝嗇、為均、為子母牛、為大輿、為文、為眾、為柄。Kun's associations include earth, mother, cloth, a cooking vessel, thrift, even distribution, a cow with its calf, a large vehicle, patterned form, a multitude, and a handle. These images emphasize carrying, receiving, providing, and sustaining. The list should be kept as a traditional symbolic inventory.
第十一章 第 3 段SHUOGUA-11-03其於地也為黑。In the continuation of Kun's associations, the color black is assigned to it when considering the Earth. This brief statement completes a longer catalog. It is a conventional image within the trigram tradition rather than a literal statement about the color of all soil.
第十一章 第 4 段SHUOGUA-11-04震為雷、為龍、為玄黃、為敷、為大涂、為長子、為決躁、為蒼筤竹、為萑葦。Zhen is connected with thunder and dragon, dark-yellow color, unfolding, broad roads, eldest sons, quick movement, young green bamboo, and reeds. The list gathers things marked by stirring, emergence, and energetic growth. The diversity of items shows that associations are analogies, not single literal definitions.
第十一章 第 5 段SHUOGUA-11-05其於馬也，為善鳴、為馵足，為的顙。In describing horses, the Zhen image is associated with loud neighing, a distinctive hoof or leg, and a marked forehead. These concrete animal features extend Zhen's wider association with visible activity and strong movement. Some traditional animal terms are textually difficult, so their details require specialized philological review.
第十一章 第 6 段SHUOGUA-11-06其於稼也，為反生。For growing crops, Zhen is associated with shoots that emerge in an inverted or unusual fashion. The description is part of a catalog of natural images connected with this trigram. It illustrates the ancient habit of identifying distinctive forms of growth as symbolic parallels.
第十一章 第 7 段SHUOGUA-11-07其究為健，為蕃鮮。The concluding characteristics ascribed to Zhen are vigor and lush freshness. After its many images of thunder, animals, and plants, the catalog returns to the qualities of forceful beginning and flourishing growth. These words should be read as imagery rather than as a prediction about a specific event.
第十一章 第 8 段SHUOGUA-11-08巽為木、為風、為長女、為繩直、為工、為白、為長、為高、為進退、為不果、為臭。Xun is associated with wood, wind, the eldest daughter, a straight measuring line, craftsmen, white, length, height, advancing and retreating, indecision, and scent. Its recurring idea is influence that enters or extends through things. The catalog includes both physical attributes and patterns of conduct.
第十一章 第 9 段SHUOGUA-11-09其於人也，為寡髮、為廣顙、為多白眼、為近利市三倍。In human appearance, Xun is associated with sparse hair, a broad forehead, and conspicuous whites of the eyes. The text also mentions a threefold gain in the marketplace. This is an old catalog of images extending from bodily signs to commerce, and it is not a validated way to predict an individual's characteristics or income.
第十一章 第 10 段SHUOGUA-11-10其究為躁卦。The final association in this Xun sequence is an agitated or restless quality. Although Xun is often described as gentle penetration, the catalog also allows for instability and repeated movement. Its symbolism is broader than a simple equation of gentleness with passivity.
第十一章 第 11 段SHUOGUA-11-11坎為水、為溝瀆、為隱伏、為矯輮、為弓輪。Kan is associated with water, ditches and channels, concealment, bending and straightening, bows, and wheels. These images have to do with depth, flow, difficulty, and shaped movement. Their diversity reflects the use of the trigram as a network of analogies rather than a literal inventory of water-related objects.
第十一章 第 12 段SHUOGUA-11-12其於人也，為加憂、為心病、為耳痛、為血卦、為赤。When applied to people, Kan is associated with added anxiety, heart trouble, ear pain, blood, and red coloring. These are part of a historical divinatory symbolism and should not be interpreted as medically reliable symptom predictions. The commentary records the associations without treating them as clinical facts.
第十一章 第 13 段SHUOGUA-11-13其於馬也，為美脊、為亟心、為下首、為薄蹄、為曳。For horses, Kan is linked to a fine back, anxious disposition, a lowered head, thin hooves, and dragging movement. The list extends the trigram's themes of tension and difficulty to recognizable animal features. Its terms preserve an older descriptive vocabulary that may have variant scholarly interpretations.
第十一章 第 14 段SHUOGUA-11-14其於輿也，為多眚、為通、為月、為盜。When applied to a vehicle, Kan is associated with many defects; the same textual sequence also names passage, the moon, and theft. The varying kinds of images show a broad associative method. They should be presented as listed by the classical source, rather than forced into a single modern explanation.
第十一章 第 15 段SHUOGUA-11-15其於木也，為堅多心。For trees, Kan is associated with wood that is hard and dense at its center. This brief image adds another material property to the trigram's catalog. The link lies in the idea of a strong inner core enclosed within a more vulnerable exterior.
第十一章 第 16 段SHUOGUA-11-16離為火、為日、為電、為中女、為甲冑、為戈兵。Li is associated with fire, the sun, lightning, the middle daughter, armor, and weapons. Brightness, visibility, and protective or dangerous instruments appear together in this symbolic list. It captures the varied uses of the fire image in ritual, observation, and human affairs.
第十一章 第 17 段SHUOGUA-11-17其於人也，為大腹、為乾卦、為鱉、為蟹、為蠃、為蚌、為龜。The source continues with a difficult list attached to Li, including a large belly and several shelled creatures such as turtles, crabs, and shellfish. Its wording also contains a potentially problematic item in the supplied base text. The images have been retained at a general descriptive level pending specialist textual collation.
第十一章 第 18 段SHUOGUA-11-18其於木也，為科上槁。For trees, Li is linked with an upper portion that has become dry or withered. The image connects brightness and heat with a visible condition of wood. This is a particular entry in the traditional symbolism, not a universal botanical classification.
第十一章 第 19 段SHUOGUA-11-19艮為山、為徑路、為小石、為門闕、為果蓏、為閽寺、為指、為狗、為鼠、為黔喙之屬。Gen is associated with mountains, pathways, small stones, gates, fruit, gatekeepers, fingers, dogs, mice, and creatures with dark beaks. Many examples suggest boundaries, thresholds, firmness, or stopping places. The passage uses natural and social objects to expand the trigram's range.
第十一章 第 20 段SHUOGUA-11-20其於木也，為堅多節。For wood, Gen describes hardness and many joints or knots. The image fits its wider association with mountains, firmness, and distinct points where movement is checked. It is one item in a symbolic correspondence system, not a technical definition of timber.
第十一章 第 21 段SHUOGUA-11-21兌為澤、為少女、為巫、為口舌、為毀折、為附決。Dui is linked to marshes, the youngest daughter, shamans, mouth and speech, breaking, and separation. Its range joins pleasurable expression with openings and divisions. The association with speech helps explain why other passages connect Dui with joyful communication and persuasion.
第十一章 第 22 段SHUOGUA-11-22其於地也，為剛鹵，為妾、為羊。In the final entries, Dui is associated with hard salty soil, a secondary wife, and sheep. The passage continues an old image system in which natural environments, domestic roles, and animals can all exemplify a trigram. These historic categories are best preserved with their original context.
