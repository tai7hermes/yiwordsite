Sequence of the Hexagrams / XUGUA
XUGUA · 63 units · text_version: yizhuan-en-3.0.0 · review_status: draft
UnitOriginalTranslation
上篇 第 1 段XUGUA-01-01有天地，然後萬物生焉。
Heaven and Earth provide the setting in which the myriad things come into being. The sequence begins with this broad account of generation before describing particular stages of growth.
上篇 第 2 段XUGUA-01-02盈天地之間者，唯萬物，故受之以屯；屯者盈也，屯者物之始生也。
Once living things fill the space between Heaven and Earth, the sequence moves to Zhun. Its difficulty and fullness describe the crowded, uncertain first moments of emerging life.
上篇 第 3 段XUGUA-01-03物生必蒙，故受之以蒙；蒙者蒙也，物之穉也。
Things newly born are still immature and uncomprehending, so Meng follows Zhun. The transition turns from initial emergence to the need for instruction and enlightenment.
上篇 第 4 段XUGUA-01-04物穉不可不養也，故受之以需；需者飲食之道也。
Young life needs nourishment. Xu therefore follows Meng and is explained through eating and drinking, which sustain growth before more complicated social activities arise.
上篇 第 5 段XUGUA-01-05飲食必有訟，故受之以訟。
The need to obtain and distribute food can create competing claims. Song, contention or litigation, follows Xu as the sequence moves from nourishment to dispute.
上篇 第 6 段XUGUA-01-06訟必有眾起，故受之以師；師者眾也。
Disputes can summon groups of people into collective action. Shi follows Song because its name refers to an assembled multitude, including an army.
上篇 第 7 段XUGUA-01-07眾必有所比，故受之以比；比者比也。
A gathering needs relationships of support and alignment. Bi follows Shi to represent people drawing close to one another after forming a larger group.
上篇 第 8 段XUGUA-01-08比必有所畜也，故受之以小畜。
Once people are allied, they can begin to accumulate limited resources. Xiao Xu follows Bi and describes a modest process of restraint and gathering.
上篇 第 9 段XUGUA-01-09物畜然後有禮，故受之以履。
Accumulated resources need an orderly manner of use. Lu follows Xiao Xu and brings the topic of appropriate conduct and social forms into the sequence.
上篇 第 10 段XUGUA-01-10履而泰，然後安，故受之以泰；泰者通也。
Proper conduct can foster peaceful communication. Tai follows Lu as a condition of openness, ease, and stability after order has been established.
上篇 第 11 段XUGUA-01-11物不可以終通，故受之以否。
Nothing remains open and harmonious forever. Pi follows Tai to represent the possible reversal from communication into obstruction and estrangement.
上篇 第 12 段XUGUA-01-12物不可以終否，故受之以同人。
Obstruction cannot last forever either. Tong Ren follows Pi, suggesting that renewed fellowship is one way relationships move beyond separation.
上篇 第 13 段XUGUA-01-13與人同者，物必歸焉，故受之以大有。
People who cooperate can attract further support and resources. Da You follows Tong Ren, representing a larger possession made possible by shared endeavor.
上篇 第 14 段XUGUA-01-14有大者不可以盈，故受之以謙。
Great abundance brings the danger of overfilling and pride. Qian, humility, follows Da You as a necessary restraint when resources or power have expanded.
上篇 第 15 段XUGUA-01-15有大而能謙，必豫，故受之以豫。
When abundance is joined to modesty, a community can become joyful and prepared. Yu therefore follows Qian in this sequence of social development.
上篇 第 16 段XUGUA-01-16豫必有隨，故受之以隨。
Joy and readiness draw people into participation. Sui follows Yu and emphasizes following, responding, and moving together.
上篇 第 17 段XUGUA-01-17以喜隨人者，必有事，故受之以蠱；蠱者事也。
Following others in a cheerful spirit leads to obligations and work. Gu follows Sui and concerns the tasks of setting damaged affairs right.
上篇 第 18 段XUGUA-01-18有事而后可大，故受之以臨；臨者大也。
After problems are confronted and repaired, greater responsibility becomes possible. Lin follows Gu and signifies approaching or overseeing what has grown.
上篇 第 19 段XUGUA-01-19物大然後可觀，故受之以觀。
An enterprise that has become substantial can be observed and assessed. Guan follows Lin, placing examination after the enlargement of affairs.
上篇 第 20 段XUGUA-01-20可觀而后有所合，故受之以噬嗑；嗑者合也。
Observation makes discernment and connection possible. Shi He follows Guan, describing the act of biting through an obstruction so that separated parts can join.
上篇 第 21 段XUGUA-01-21物不可以苟合而已，故受之以賁；賁者飾也。
Mere joining is not enough for lasting order; there must also be proper form. Bi, adornment, follows Shi He and concerns patterned expression.
上篇 第 22 段XUGUA-01-22致飾然後亨，則盡矣，故受之以剝；剝者剝也。
Decoration can reach excess and exhaustion. Bo follows Bi and shows how outward forms may peel away when their basis is depleted.
上篇 第 23 段XUGUA-01-23物不可以終盡，剝窮上反下，故受之以復。
Exhaustion is not the final state of things. Fu follows Bo because decline, once it reaches its limit, opens the possibility of return.
上篇 第 24 段XUGUA-01-24復則不妄矣，故受之以无妄。
Returning to the proper course reduces reckless deviation. Wu Wang follows Fu, expressing an uncontrived or sincere manner of acting.
上篇 第 25 段XUGUA-01-25有无妄然後可畜，故受之以大畜。
When conduct is free from reckless distortion, resources and virtue can be stored. Da Xu therefore follows Wu Wang as a form of substantial cultivation.
上篇 第 26 段XUGUA-01-26物畜然後可養，故受之以頤；頤者養也。
What has been stored can nourish life. Yi follows Da Xu and addresses both receiving sustenance and caring responsibly for others.
上篇 第 27 段XUGUA-01-27不養則不可動，故受之以大過。
Nourishment makes action possible, but accumulated energy may exceed normal limits. Da Guo follows Yi to depict an extraordinary burden or imbalance.
上篇 第 28 段XUGUA-01-28物不可以終過，故受之以坎；坎者陷也。
An extreme condition cannot continue without exposing danger. Kan follows Da Guo and represents falling into difficulty or repeated pits.
上篇 第 29 段XUGUA-01-29陷必有所麗，故受之以離；離者麗也。
A person in danger must find something to hold to. Li follows Kan and emphasizes attachment, support, and illumination as the upper canon closes.
下篇 第 1 段XUGUA-02-01有天地然後有萬物，有萬物然後有男女，有男女然後有夫婦，有夫婦然後有父子，有父子然後有君臣，有君臣然後有上下，有上下然後禮儀有所錯。
The lower canon begins by tracing relations from Heaven and Earth to living things, then to men and women, spouses, generations, political ranks, and ritual order. The passage presents an ancient genealogy of social institutions through differentiation and relationship.
下篇 第 2 段XUGUA-02-02夫婦之道，不可以不久也，故受之以恆；恆者久也。
The relationship of spouses is expected to endure, so Heng follows Xian. Constancy becomes the principle that can sustain a bond beyond its first attraction.
下篇 第 3 段XUGUA-02-03物不可以久居其所，故受之以遯；遯者退也。
No living thing can remain in one position forever. Dun follows Heng to mark the time when withdrawal becomes appropriate.
下篇 第 4 段XUGUA-02-04物不可終遯，故受之以大壯。
Withdrawal cannot be a permanent response to every situation. Da Zhuang follows Dun as strength returns and action becomes possible again.
下篇 第 5 段XUGUA-02-05物不可以終壯，故受之以晉；晉者進也。
Strength is not a final state; it can develop into forward movement. Jin follows Da Zhuang and expresses advancement.
下篇 第 6 段XUGUA-02-06進必有所傷，故受之以明夷；夷者傷也。
Advancement can expose a person or a cause to injury. Ming Yi follows Jin, recalling the risk that light may be obscured when it moves forward.
下篇 第 7 段XUGUA-02-07傷於外者，必反其家，故受之以家人。
Those wounded in the outside world return to their household for support. Jia Ren follows Ming Yi and turns attention from public adversity to family order.
下篇 第 8 段XUGUA-02-08家道窮必乖，故受之以睽；睽者乖也。
Even a household's order can be exhausted and become divided. Kui follows Jia Ren as the condition of estrangement arising within established relations.
下篇 第 9 段XUGUA-02-09乖必有難，故受之以蹇；蹇者難也。
Estrangement creates practical obstacles. Jian follows Kui and depicts difficulty in finding a passable route.
下篇 第 10 段XUGUA-02-10物不可終難，故受之以解；解者緩也。
Hardship cannot be the permanent destiny of a situation. Xie follows Jian and signifies the loosening of obstructions and release from difficulty.
下篇 第 11 段XUGUA-02-11緩必有所失，故受之以損。
Release and relaxation may also allow something to be lost. Sun follows Xie and represents reduction after a period of easing.
下篇 第 12 段XUGUA-02-12損而不已，必益，故受之以益。
Reduction may create room for renewed increase. Yi follows Sun, setting gain and loss alongside each other as changing stages.
下篇 第 13 段XUGUA-02-13益而不已，必決，故受之以夬；夬者決也。
Increase that continues unchecked eventually demands a decision. Guai follows Yi and marks the point at which a firm resolution is required.
下篇 第 14 段XUGUA-02-14決必有所遇，故受之以姤；姤者遇也。
A decision changes relations and brings new encounters. Gou follows Guai, with meeting as its central idea.
下篇 第 15 段XUGUA-02-15物相遇而后聚，故受之以萃；萃者聚也。
Things that meet may then gather into a larger body. Cui follows Gou and symbolizes coming together.
下篇 第 16 段XUGUA-02-16聚而上者，謂之升，故受之以升。
Once a group is assembled, a new level of development can emerge. Sheng follows Cui and describes upward growth.
下篇 第 17 段XUGUA-02-17升而不已，必困，故受之以困。
Rise without limit can lead to exhaustion or confinement. Kun follows Sheng, showing the cost of unrestrained ascent.
下篇 第 18 段XUGUA-02-18困乎上者，必反下，故受之以井。
When pressure above becomes unbearable, one returns to the foundations below. Jing follows Kun and uses the well as an image of sustained communal support.
下篇 第 19 段XUGUA-02-19井道不可不革，故受之以革。
Even a well-established source of support requires renewal. Ge follows Jing and signifies reform or transformation.
下篇 第 20 段XUGUA-02-20革物者莫若鼎，故受之以鼎。
After an old arrangement has been changed, a new one must be established. Ding follows Ge and evokes a ritual vessel used to nourish and order society.
下篇 第 21 段XUGUA-02-21主器者莫若長子，故受之以震；震者動也。
The keeper of a significant vessel is represented by the eldest son. Zhen follows Ding and introduces movement, responsibility, and activation.
下篇 第 22 段XUGUA-02-22物不可以終動，止之，故受之以艮；艮者止也。
Movement cannot last without pauses. Gen follows Zhen as the principle of stopping at an appropriate time.
下篇 第 23 段XUGUA-02-23物不可以終止，故受之以漸；漸者進也。
Stopping is not an end in itself. Jian, gradual progress, follows Gen when movement resumes in measured stages.
下篇 第 24 段XUGUA-02-24進必有所歸，故受之以歸妹。
Progress calls for a destination or affiliation. Gui Mei follows Jian and uses marriage to depict an arrival into relationship.
下篇 第 25 段XUGUA-02-25得其所歸者必大，故受之以豐；豐者大也。
Having found a place of belonging, things can flourish. Feng follows Gui Mei and denotes fullness or abundance.
下篇 第 26 段XUGUA-02-26窮大者必失其居，故受之以旅。
Great fullness can become unstable, leaving people without their former place. Lu, the traveler, follows Feng.
下篇 第 27 段XUGUA-02-27旅而無所容，故受之以巽；巽者入也。
A traveler may lack a secure place of welcome. Xun follows Lu and points toward entering, yielding, and finding a way through new surroundings.
下篇 第 28 段XUGUA-02-28入而后說之，故受之以兌；兌者說也。
Entry and communication can lead to pleasure. Dui follows Xun and expresses joy or agreeable exchange.
下篇 第 29 段XUGUA-02-29說而后散之，故受之以渙；渙者離也。
After a period of delight, things can disperse. Huan follows Dui and represents separation or the clearing away of congestion.
下篇 第 30 段XUGUA-02-30物不可以終離，故受之以節。
Dispersal cannot continue without limit. Jie follows Huan and introduces bounds, proportion, and restraint.
下篇 第 31 段XUGUA-02-31節而信之，故受之以中孚。
Appropriate limits create conditions for dependable trust. Zhong Fu follows Jie, emphasizing inward sincerity.
下篇 第 32 段XUGUA-02-32有其信者，必行之，故受之以小過。
Trust should be brought into conduct. Xiao Guo follows Zhong Fu and depicts small departures or carefully limited action.
下篇 第 33 段XUGUA-02-33有過物者，必濟，故受之既濟。
After moving beyond an obstacle, completion becomes possible. Ji Ji follows Xiao Guo as an image of affairs having been brought across.
下篇 第 34 段XUGUA-02-34物不可窮也，故受之以未濟；終焉。
No completion can close the process of change forever. Wei Ji follows Ji Ji, leaving the work unfinished and the possibility of a new beginning open.
