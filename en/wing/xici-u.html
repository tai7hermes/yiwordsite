Appended Statements I Chapter 1 Unit 1XICI-U-01-01天尊地卑，乾坤定矣。卑高以陳，貴賤位矣。動靜有常，剛柔斷矣。方以類聚，物以羣分，吉凶生矣。在天成象，在地成形，變化見矣。是故剛柔相摩，八卦相荡，鼓之以雷霆，潤之以風雨，日月運行，一寒一暑。乾道成男，坤道成女；乾知大始，坤作成物；乾以易知，坤以簡能；易則易知，簡則易從；易知則有親，易從則有功；有親則可久，有功則可大；可久則賢人之德，可大則賢人之業。易簡，而天下之理得矣。天下之理得，而成位乎其中矣。Heaven is honored and earth is low; therefore Qian and Kun are determined. As low and high are arranged, noble and humble positions are established. Movement and stillness have their constants; therefore firmness and yielding are distinguished. Directions gather by kind, and things divide into groups; therefore good fortune and misfortune arise. In heaven these become images; on earth they become forms; therefore change and transformation become visible. Firmness and yielding rub against one another, and the eight trigrams stimulate one another. Thunder and lightning stir them; wind and rain moisten them; sun and moon move in their courses; now cold, now heat. The way of Qian forms the male; the way of Kun forms the female. Qian directs the great beginnings, while Kun brings things to completion. Qian knows through ease, and Kun accomplishes through simplicity. What is easy is easy to know; what is simple is easy to follow. What is easy to know gains affinity; what is easy to follow gains achievement. Affinity can endure; achievement can grow great. Endurance is the virtue of the worthy; greatness is the work of the worthy. Through ease and simplicity, the principles of the world are obtained; once those principles are obtained, a place is established amid heaven and earth.
Appended Statements I Chapter 2 Unit 1XICI-U-02-01聖人設卦觀象，繫辭焉而明吉凶；剛柔相推而生變化。是故吉凶者，失得之象也。悔吝者，憂虞之象也。變化者，進退之象也。剛柔者，晝夜之象也。六爻之動，三極之道也。是故君子所居而安者，易之序也。所樂而玩者，爻之辭也。是故君子居則觀其象而玩其辭，動則觀其變而玩其占；是以自天祐之，吉无不利。The sages established the hexagrams, observed their images, and attached statements to clarify good fortune and misfortune. Firm and yielding lines press upon one another and produce change and transformation. Therefore good fortune and misfortune are images of gain and loss; remorse and regret are images of anxiety and concern; change and transformation are images of advance and retreat; firmness and yielding are images of day and night. The movement of the six lines expresses the way of the Three Powers. Therefore the noble person finds security in the order of the Yi and takes delight in studying the line statements. At rest, the person observes the images and reflects on the words; in action, the person observes the changes and reflects on the prognostications. In this way, “Help comes from heaven; good fortune, nothing unfavorable.”.
Appended Statements I Chapter 3 Unit 1XICI-U-03-01彖者，言乎象者也。爻者，言乎變者也。吉凶者，言乎其失得也。悔吝者，言乎其小疵也。无咎者，善補過也。是故列貴賤者存乎位，齊小大者存乎卦，辯吉凶者存乎辭，憂悔吝者存乎介，震无咎者存乎悔。是故卦有小大，辭有險易，辭也者，各指其所之。The Judgments speak about the images of the hexagrams; the line statements speak about change. Good fortune and misfortune speak about gain and loss; remorse and regret speak about minor faults; “no blame” means correcting faults well. Therefore the ranking of noble and humble depends on position; the balancing of small and great depends on the hexagram; distinguishing good fortune and misfortune depends on the statements; concern over remorse and regret depends on the boundary between choices; freedom from blame through alarm depends on repentance. Hexagrams therefore differ as small or great, and their statements as dangerous or easy; each statement points to the direction in which the situation is moving.
Appended Statements I Chapter 4 Unit 1XICI-U-04-01易與天地準，故能彌綸天地之道。仰以觀於天文，俯以察於地理，是故知幽明之故。原始反終，故知死生之說。精氣為物，遊魂為變，是故知鬼神之情狀。與天地相似，故不違；知周乎萬物，而道濟天下，故不過；旁行而不流，樂天知命，故不憂；安土敦乎仁，故能愛；範圍天地之化而不過，曲成萬物而不遺，通乎晝夜之道而知，故神无方而易无體。The Yi accords with heaven and earth and therefore can encompass their way. Looking upward, one observes the patterns of heaven; looking downward, one examines the arrangements of earth, and therefore understands the causes of what is hidden and manifest. Tracing beginnings and returning to endings, one understands the account of life and death. Concentrated vital energy becomes things; the wandering soul produces change, and therefore one understands the conditions of spirits. Because the Yi resembles heaven and earth, it does not oppose them. Its knowledge embraces all things, and its way aids the world, so it does not overstep. It extends in every direction without being swept away; delighting in heaven and understanding destiny, it is free of anxiety. At peace in its place and deep in humaneness, it can love. It frames the transformations of heaven and earth without excess, completes all things without omission, and penetrates the way of day and night. Therefore spirit has no fixed direction and the Yi has no fixed form.
Appended Statements I Chapter 5 Unit 1XICI-U-05-01一陰一陽之謂道：繼之者善也，成之者性也；仁者見之謂之仁，知者見之謂之知；百姓日用而不知，故君子之道鮮矣。顯諸仁，藏諸用，鼓萬物而不與聖人同憂，盛德大業至矣哉！富有之謂大業，日新之謂盛德，生生之謂易，成象之謂乾，效法之謂坤，極數知來之謂占，通變之謂事，陰陽不測之謂神。In everyday terms, the idea is this: one yin and one yang are called the Way. What continues it is goodness; what completes it is human nature. The humane see it and call it humaneness; the wise see it and call it wisdom. The common people use it every day without knowing it, so the way of the noble person is rarely recognized. It manifests in humaneness and is hidden in its functions; it animates all things without sharing the sages’ anxieties. How complete are its abundant virtue and great work! Rich possession is called great work; daily renewal is called abundant virtue; producing life without end is called change; completing images is called Qian; imitating the model is called Kun; carrying number to its limit to know what is coming is called divination; penetrating change is called undertaking; what cannot be fathomed in yin and yang is called spirit.
Appended Statements I Chapter 6 Unit 1XICI-U-06-01夫易，廣矣！大矣！以言乎遠則不禦，以言乎邇則靜而正，以言乎天地之間則備矣！夫乾，其靜也專，其動也直，是以大生焉。夫坤，其靜也翕，其動也闢，是以廣生焉。廣大配天地，變通配四時，陰陽之義配日月，易簡之善配至德。The passage makes this point: how broad and great the Yi is! Speaking of what is distant, it has no limit; speaking of what is near, it is still and correct; speaking of the space between heaven and earth, it is complete. Qian in stillness is concentrated and in movement is straight; therefore it gives rise to greatness. Kun in stillness is closed and in movement opens; therefore it gives rise to breadth. Breadth and greatness correspond to heaven and earth; change and continuity correspond to the four seasons; the meaning of yin and yang corresponds to sun and moon; and the excellence of ease and simplicity corresponds to the highest virtue.
Appended Statements I Chapter 7 Unit 1XICI-U-07-01子曰：「易其至矣乎！夫易，聖人所以崇德而廣業也，知崇禮卑，崇效天，卑法地。天地設位，而易行乎其中矣。成性存存，道義之門。」The Master said: “Has not the Yi reached the highest point? Through it the sages exalted virtue and broadened their work. Knowledge is lofty and ritual is humble: loftiness models heaven, while humility follows earth. Heaven and earth establish their positions, and change moves between them. When one repeatedly preserves and completes one’s nature, the gateway of the Way and righteousness opens.”.
Appended Statements I Chapter 8 Unit 1XICI-U-08-01聖人有以見天下之賾，而擬諸其形容，象其物宜，是故謂之象。聖人有以見天下之動，而觀其會通，以行其典禮，繫辭焉以斷其吉凶，是故謂之爻。言天下之至賾而不可惡也，言天下之至動而不可亂也。擬之而後言，議之而後動，擬議以成其變化。The passage makes this point: the sages perceived the complexities of the world, represented them through visible forms, and imaged what was appropriate to each thing; therefore these are called images. The sages perceived the movements of the world, observed how they met and communicated, enacted enduring principles and rites, and attached statements to determine good fortune and misfortune; therefore these are called lines. They speak of the world’s utmost complexity without arousing aversion and of its utmost movement without creating disorder. One first models a matter and then speaks, first deliberates and then acts; through modeling and deliberation, change and transformation are completed.
Appended Statements I Chapter 8 Unit 2XICI-U-08-02「鳴鶴在陰，其子和之。我有好爵，吾與爾靡之。」子曰：「君子居其室，出其言善，則千里之外應之，況其邇者乎！居其室，出其言不善，則千里之外違之，況其邇者乎！言出乎身加乎民；行發乎邇見乎遠。言行，君子之樞機；樞機之發，榮辱之主也。言行，君子之所以動天地也，可不慎乎！」「同人，先號咷而後笑。」子曰：「君子之道，或出或處，或默或語。二人同心，其利斷金。同心之言，其臭如蘭。」“A calling crane is in the shade; its young answers it. I have a fine cup, and I will share it with you.” The Master said: “If a noble person stays in their room and speaks well, people respond from a thousand miles away; how much more those who are near. If the person stays in their room and speaks badly, people oppose them from a thousand miles away; how much more those who are near. Words issue from oneself and reach the people; actions begin nearby and become visible far away. Speech and action are the hinge and trigger of the noble person. When that hinge and trigger move, they govern honor and disgrace. Through speech and action the noble person moves heaven and earth; can the person fail to be careful?” “In Tong Ren, first there is wailing and then laughter.” The Master said: “The way of two noble persons may lead one to serve or withdraw, to remain silent or speak. When two people are of one heart, their sharpness cuts metal; words from united hearts are fragrant like orchids.”.
Appended Statements I Chapter 8 Unit 3XICI-U-08-03「初六，藉用白茅，无咎。」子曰：「苟錯諸地而可矣。藉之用茅，何咎之有！慎之至也。夫茅之為物薄而用可重也，慎斯術也以往，其无所失矣。」“At the beginning, use white cogon grass as a mat; no blame.” The Master said: “It would have been enough simply to place the offering on the ground. What blame could there be in placing it on grass? This is the utmost care. Cogon grass is a slight thing, yet its use can be weighty. If one proceeds with such care, nothing will be lost.”.
Appended Statements I Chapter 8 Unit 4XICI-U-08-04「勞謙，君子有終，吉。」子曰：「勞而不伐，有功而不德，厚之至也；語以其功下人者也。德言盛，禮言恭；謙也者，致恭以存其位者也。」“Toiling yet modest, the noble person has a good end: good fortune.” The Master said: “To labor without boasting, to achieve merit without claiming virtue; this is the height of generosity. It means placing oneself below others despite one’s achievements. Virtue speaks of fullness; ritual speaks of reverence. Modesty is carrying reverence to its utmost so as to preserve one’s position.”.
Appended Statements I Chapter 8 Unit 5XICI-U-08-05「亢龍有悔。」子曰：「貴而无位，高而无民，賢人在下位而无輔，是以動而有悔也。」“The overreaching dragon has regret.” The Master said: “Noble yet without a position, high yet without people, with worthy persons below but no support; therefore action brings regret.”.
Appended Statements I Chapter 8 Unit 6XICI-U-08-06「不出戶庭，无咎。」子曰：「亂之所生也，則言語以為階。君不密則失臣，臣不密則失身，幾事不密則害成；是以君子慎密而不出也。」“Not going beyond the courtyard, no blame.” The Master said: “Disorder arises when speech becomes its stairway. If a ruler is not discreet, the person loses their minister; if a minister is not discreet, the person loses their life; if incipient affairs are not kept discreet, their completion is harmed. Therefore the noble person is careful about secrecy and does not let words go forth.”.
Appended Statements I Chapter 8 Unit 7XICI-U-08-07子曰：「作易者，其知盜乎！易曰：『負且乘，致寇至。』負也者，小人之事也。乘也者，君子之器也。小人而乘君子之器，盜思奪之矣。上慢下暴，盜思伐之矣。慢藏誨盜，冶容誨淫。易曰：『負且乘，致寇至。』盜之招也。」The Master said: “Did the makers of the Yi understand how robbers arise? The Yi says, ‘The person bears a burden yet rides in a carriage, thereby bringing robbers upon themself.’ Bearing burdens is the work of a common person; a carriage is the conveyance of a noble person. When a common person rides in a noble person’s carriage, robbers are tempted to seize it. When those above are careless and those below are violent, attackers are tempted to strike. Careless storage teaches theft, and provocative display encourages lust. Therefore the Yi says, ‘The person bears a burden yet rides in a carriage, thereby bringing robbers upon themself.’ It describes how one invites robbers.”.
Appended Statements I Chapter 9 Unit 1XICI-U-09-01天一地二，天三地四，天五地六，天七地八，天九地十。天數五，地數五，五位相得而各有合。天數二十有五，地數三十，凡天地之數，五十有五，此所以成變化，而行鬼神也。大衍之數五十，其用四十有九，分而為二以象兩，掛一以象三，揲之以四以象四時，歸奇於扐以象閏，五歲再閏，故再扐而後掛。In everyday terms, the idea is this: one belongs to heaven, two to earth; three to heaven, four to earth; five to heaven, six to earth; seven to heaven, eight to earth; nine to heaven, ten to earth. Heaven has five numbers and earth has five numbers. Their five positions correspond and each has its partner. The numbers of heaven total twenty-five and those of earth total thirty; together the numbers of heaven and earth total fifty-five. By these, changes and transformations are completed and the actions of spirits are carried out. The number of the Great Expansion is fifty, of which forty-nine are used. They are divided into two to symbolize the two powers; one is set aside to symbolize the third; they are counted by fours to symbolize the four seasons; the remainders are placed between the fingers to symbolize the intercalary month. In five years there are two intercalary months, so the remainder is taken twice before the next operation.
Appended Statements I Chapter 9 Unit 2XICI-U-09-02乾之策，二百一十有六，坤之策，百四十有四，凡三百有六十，當期之日。二篇之策，萬有一千五百二十，當萬物之數也。是故四營而成易，十有八變而成卦，八卦而小成；引而伸之，觸類而長之，天下之能事畢矣。顯道神德行，是故可與酬酢，可與祐神矣。子曰：「知變化之道者，其知神之所為乎！」The yarrow stalk numbers for Qian are 216 and for Kun 144, totaling 360, corresponding to the days of the year. The stalk numbers of the two parts total 11,520, corresponding to the number of all things. Therefore four operations complete one change; eighteen changes complete a hexagram; the eight trigrams form the small completion. Extend and develop them, encounter each category and enlarge it, and every possible affair under heaven is encompassed. They reveal the Way and make virtuous conduct spirit-like; therefore they can respond to all circumstances and assist spiritual powers. The Master said: “Whoever knows the way of change and transformation knows what spirit does.”.
Appended Statements I Chapter 10 Unit 1XICI-U-10-01易有聖人之道四焉：以言者尚其辭，以動者尚其變，以制器者尚其象，以卜筮者尚其占。是以君子將有為也，將有行也，問焉而以言，其受命也如響，无有遠近幽深，遂知來物。非天下之至精，其孰能與於此！The Yi contains four ways of the sages: those who speak value its statements; those who act value its changes; those who make implements value its images; those who divine value its prognostications. Therefore, when a noble person intends to undertake or perform something, the person asks through words. The answer receives their command like an echo; whether far or near, hidden or deep, it enables them to know what is coming. Unless it were the finest essence under heaven, what could participate in this?
Appended Statements I Chapter 10 Unit 2XICI-U-10-02參伍以變，錯綜其數，通其變，遂成天下之文，極其數，遂定天下之象；非天下之至變，其孰能與於此！The passage makes this point: through repeated comparison, numbers are changed and interwoven. By penetrating their changes, the patterns of the world are formed; by carrying their numbers to the limit, the images of the world are determined. Unless it were the most transformative power under heaven, what could participate in this?
Appended Statements I Chapter 10 Unit 3XICI-U-10-03易，无思也，无為也，寂然不動，感而遂通天下之故。非天下之至神，其孰能與於此！In everyday terms, the idea is this: the Yi has no thought and takes no action. Silent and unmoving, when stimulated it penetrates the causes of all things under heaven. Unless it were the most spirit-like power under heaven, what could participate in this?
Appended Statements I Chapter 10 Unit 4XICI-U-10-04夫易，聖人之所以極深而研幾也！唯深也，故能通天下之志；唯幾也，故能成天下之務；唯神也，故不疾而速，不行而至。The passage makes this point: through the Yi the sages reached the deepest and examined the subtlest beginnings. Because it is deep, it can penetrate the intentions of the world; because it attends to subtle beginnings, it can complete the world’s affairs; because it is spirit-like, it is swift without haste and arrives without traveling.
Appended Statements I Chapter 10 Unit 5XICI-U-10-05子曰「易有聖人之道四焉」者，此之謂也。When the Master said, “The Yi contains four ways of the sages,” this is what the person meant.
Appended Statements I Chapter 11 Unit 1XICI-U-11-01子曰：「夫易，何為者也？夫易，開物成務，冒天下之道，如斯而已者也。」是故聖人以通天下之志，以定天下之業，以斷天下之疑。是故蓍之德圓而神，卦之德方以知，六爻之義易以貢；聖人以此洗心，退藏於密，吉凶與民同患，神以知來，知以藏往，其孰能與此哉？古之聦明叡知神武而不殺者夫！是以明於天之道，而察於民之故，是興神物以前民用；聖人以此齊戒以神明其德夫！是故闔戶謂之坤，闢戶謂之乾，一闔一闢謂之變；往來不窮謂之通，見乃謂之象，形乃謂之器，制而用之謂之法，利用出入，民咸用之謂之神。是故易有太極，是生兩儀，兩儀生四象，四象生八卦，八卦定吉凶，吉凶生大業。是故法象莫大乎天地；變通莫大乎四時；縣象著明莫大乎日月；崇高莫大乎富貴；備物致用，立成器以為天下利，莫大乎聖人；探賾索隱，鉤深致遠，以定天下之吉凶，成天下之亹亹者，莫大乎蓍龜。是故天生神物，聖人則之。天地變化，聖人效之。天垂象，見吉凶，聖人象之。河出圖，洛出書，聖人則之。易有四象，所以示也。繫辭焉，所以告也。定之以吉凶，所以斷也。The Master said: “What does the Yi do? It opens up things, completes undertakings, and encompasses the ways of everyone in the world; nothing more than this.” Therefore the sages used it to communicate the intentions of the world, establish its undertakings, and resolve its doubts. The virtue of the yarrow stalks is round and spirit-like; the virtue of the hexagrams is square and wise; the meanings of the six lines change so as to provide guidance. With these the sages purified their hearts and withdrew into secrecy, sharing the people’s concerns about good and bad fortune. Through spirit they knew what was coming; through wisdom they stored what had passed. Who could do this except the ancient sages, whose intelligence, insight, spiritual power, and martial strength did not depend on killing? Understanding the way of heaven and examining the conditions of the people, they established the numinous objects to guide the people’s use. The sages purified and disciplined themselves so as to make their virtue spiritually luminous. A closed door is called Kun; an open door is called Qian. One closing and one opening are called change; coming and going without exhaustion are called continuity. What becomes visible is called an image; what takes form is called an implement; what is regulated for use is called a model; what benefits people in going out and coming in and is used by all is called spirit. Therefore the Yi has the Great Ultimate, which produces the Two Forms; the Two Forms produce the Four Images; the Four Images produce the Eight Trigrams. The eight trigrams determine good and bad fortune, and good and bad fortune give rise to great undertakings. No models and images are greater than heaven and earth; no change and continuity are greater than the four seasons; no suspended images shine more clearly than sun and moon; nothing exalted is greater than wealth and rank; in preparing things for use and establishing instruments for the benefit of the world, none are greater than the sages; in probing complexity, seeking the hidden, drawing up what is deep, and reaching what is distant; thereby determining good and bad fortune and completing the world’s endeavors; nothing is greater than the yarrow stalks and tortoise shell. Heaven produced numinous things, and the sages took them as models. Heaven and earth change and transform, and the sages imitated them. Heaven displayed images that revealed good and bad fortune, and the sages represented them. The Yellow River produced the Chart and the Luo River produced the Writing, and the sages took them as models. The Yi has four kinds of images in order to reveal; statements are attached in order to communicate; good and bad fortune are determined in order to decide.
Appended Statements I Chapter 12 Unit 1XICI-U-12-01易曰：「自天祐之，吉无不利」。子曰：「祐者，助也。天之所助者，順也。人之所助者，信也。履信思乎順，又以尚賢也，是以『自天祐之，吉无不利。』也。」The Yi says, “Help comes from heaven; good fortune, nothing unfavorable.” The Master said: “Help means assistance. Heaven assists those who are compliant; people assist those who are trustworthy. One practices trustworthiness, keeps compliance in mind, and also honors the worthy. Therefore, ‘Help comes from heaven; good fortune, nothing unfavorable.’”.
Appended Statements I Chapter 12 Unit 2XICI-U-12-02子曰：「書不盡言，言不盡意。」然則聖人之意，其不可見乎？子曰：「聖人立象以盡意，設卦以盡情偽，繫辭以盡其言，變而通之以盡利，鼓之舞之以盡神。」The Master said: “Writing does not exhaust words, and words do not exhaust meaning.” Then can the intentions of the sages not be seen? The Master said: “The sages established images to express meaning fully, set up hexagrams to disclose true and false conditions fully, attached statements to express their words fully, developed change and continuity to realize benefit fully, and stirred and animated them to express spiritual efficacy fully.”.
Appended Statements I Chapter 12 Unit 3XICI-U-12-03乾坤，其易之縕邪！乾坤成列，而易立乎其中矣。乾坤毀，則无以見易，易不可見，則乾坤或幾乎息矣。是故形而上者謂之道，形而下者謂之器，化而裁之謂之變，推而行之謂之通，舉而錯之天下之民謂之事業。是故夫象，聖人有以見天下之賾，而擬諸其形容，象其物宜，是故謂之象。聖人有以見天下之動，而觀其會通，以行其典禮，繫辭焉以斷其吉凶，是故謂之爻。極天下之賾者存乎卦；鼓天下之動者存乎辭；化而裁之存乎變；推而行之存乎通；神而明之，存乎其人；默而成之，不言而信，存乎德行。Are Qian and Kun not the inner core of the Yi? When Qian and Kun are set in order, the Yi stands between them. If Qian and Kun were destroyed, there would be no means to perceive the Yi; if the Yi could not be perceived, Qian and Kun would nearly cease. Therefore what precedes physical form is called the Way; what follows physical form is called an implement. Transforming and shaping are called change; extending and carrying forward are called continuity; applying them to all people under heaven is called an undertaking. As for images: the sages perceived the complexities of the world, represented them through visible forms, and imaged what was appropriate to each thing; therefore these are called images. The sages perceived the movements of the world, observed how they met and communicated, enacted enduring principles and rites, and attached statements to determine good and bad fortune; therefore these are called lines. What exhausts the world’s complexities resides in the hexagrams; what stirs the world’s movements resides in the statements; shaping transformation resides in change; extending it into action resides in continuity. Making it spiritually clear depends on the person; silently completing it and being trusted without words depend on virtuous conduct.
