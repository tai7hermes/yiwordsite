Miscellaneous Hexagrams / ZAGUA
ZAGUA · 31 units · text_version: yizhuan-en-3.0.0 · review_status: draft

Original text pending collation; translations under continuous revision.

UnitOriginalTranslation
一篇 第 1 段ZAGUA-01-01乾剛坤柔，比樂師憂。
Qian is associated with firmness, Kun with yielding; Bi suggests the pleasure of closeness, while Shi carries the anxiety of marshaling an army. The passage compares opposite qualities to sharpen the reader's sense of each hexagram.
一篇 第 2 段ZAGUA-01-02臨、觀之義，或與或求。
Lin and Guan are set together through giving and seeking. One describes approaching or extending attention; the other describes observing and looking for understanding. Their contrast lies in the direction of engagement.
一篇 第 3 段ZAGUA-01-03屯見而不失其居，蒙雜而著。
Zhun shows new life becoming visible without losing its ground, while Meng depicts an initially confused condition growing clearer. The pair contrasts emerging stability with the work of enlightenment.
一篇 第 4 段ZAGUA-01-04震起也，艮止也。
Zhen begins or stirs movement; Gen brings movement to a stop. These paired trigrams display a basic rhythm of activation and restraint.
一篇 第 5 段ZAGUA-01-05損、益，盛衰之始也。
Sun and Yi are described as beginnings of decline and growth. Reducing and increasing are not isolated events but neighboring stages within larger cycles.
一篇 第 6 段ZAGUA-01-06大畜時也，无妄災也。
Da Xu is closely tied to the appropriate time for storing and restraining power; Wu Wang is linked here with unexpected misfortune. The pair warns against confusing readiness with immunity from events beyond control.
一篇 第 7 段ZAGUA-01-07萃聚，而升不來也。
Cui means gathering, while Sheng concerns rising. Coming together and moving upward are related but distinct processes; an assembled group does not automatically produce advancement.
一篇 第 8 段ZAGUA-01-08謙輕，而豫怠也。
Qian, humility, makes a person light or unburdened, whereas Yu can drift toward ease and idleness. The comparison warns that pleasure without discipline may lose its constructive purpose.
一篇 第 9 段ZAGUA-01-09噬嗑食也，賁无色也。
Shi He is associated with biting or eating through an obstruction, while Bi is finally linked with the absence of excessive color. Effective connection and restrained ornament are compared.
一篇 第 10 段ZAGUA-01-10兌見，而巽伏也。
Dui shows itself outwardly in delight and expression; Xun enters quietly and works beneath the surface. Visible joy contrasts with less obvious influence.
一篇 第 11 段ZAGUA-01-11隨无故也，蠱則飭也。
Sui involves following without clinging to former causes; Gu requires correcting and setting affairs in order. Adaptation and repair are different responses to changing circumstances.
一篇 第 12 段ZAGUA-01-12剝爛也，復反也。
Bo represents decay and peeling away; Fu represents return. One shows a process reaching its end, the other the beginning of recovery.
一篇 第 13 段ZAGUA-01-13晉晝也，明夷誅也。
Jin is compared with the clarity of daytime, whereas Ming Yi describes brightness wounded or suppressed. Public visibility contrasts with an obscured light.
一篇 第 14 段ZAGUA-01-14井通，而困相遇也。
Jing is associated with a well's continuing access and supply, while Kun involves the meeting of difficulties and constraints. A reliable common resource contrasts with confinement.
一篇 第 15 段ZAGUA-01-15咸速也，恒久也。
Xian acts through immediate mutual response, whereas Heng is concerned with duration. Attraction and constancy are related, but a quick connection is not yet a lasting commitment.
一篇 第 16 段ZAGUA-01-16渙離也，節止也。
Huan scatters or separates; Jie restrains and sets boundaries. A process of release is balanced by the need for proportion.
一篇 第 17 段ZAGUA-01-17解緩也，蹇難也。
Xie eases pressure and loosens difficulty; Jian describes the difficulty that first blocks the path. The two hexagrams are opposite movements in a problem's course.
一篇 第 18 段ZAGUA-01-18睽外也，家人內也。
Kui refers to divergence toward the outside, while Jia Ren concerns the inner order of a household. Difference between groups is contrasted with order within a shared home.
一篇 第 19 段ZAGUA-01-19否、泰，反其類也。
Pi and Tai are contrasting kinds: obstruction and openness. The paired states show that communication and estrangement can exchange places.
一篇 第 20 段ZAGUA-01-20大壯則止，遯則退也。
Da Zhuang reminds a strong person to stop at the right point, while Dun counsels withdrawal when conditions are unsuitable. Strength is not always an instruction to advance.
一篇 第 21 段ZAGUA-01-21大有眾也，同人親也。
Da You describes possession of much and the gathering of many supporters; Tong Ren describes affinity and cooperation. Material abundance and human fellowship are distinguished.
一篇 第 22 段ZAGUA-01-22革去故也，鼎取新也。
Ge removes the old, and Ding brings the new into use. Reform requires both taking away an exhausted arrangement and creating something that can replace it.
一篇 第 23 段ZAGUA-01-23小過過也，中孚信也。
Xiao Guo concerns going somewhat beyond a normal limit; Zhong Fu is inward trustworthiness. A small exception in conduct is contrasted with a dependable moral center.
一篇 第 24 段ZAGUA-01-24豐多故也，親寡旅也。
Feng entails many affairs and complications because fullness increases what must be managed. Lu depicts the traveler with few close companions and limited familiarity.
一篇 第 25 段ZAGUA-01-25離上，而坎下也。
Li, like fire, moves upward; Kan, like water, moves downward. Their opposite directions provide a simple way of contrasting these two images.
一篇 第 26 段ZAGUA-01-26小畜寡也，履不處也。
Xiao Xu collects only limited resources, while Lu calls for active, careful movement rather than settling indefinitely. Accumulation and conduct are different needs.
一篇 第 27 段ZAGUA-01-27需不進也，訟不親也。
Xu delays action while danger remains ahead; Song shows people drawn into disputes where closeness has broken down. Waiting and contention are distinct responses to difficulty.
一篇 第 28 段ZAGUA-01-28大過顛也，姤遇也，柔遇剛也。
Da Guo is an excessive burden that threatens overturning, while Gou signifies an encounter of yielding with firmness. Pressure and unexpected meeting are set side by side.
一篇 第 29 段ZAGUA-01-29漸女歸待男行也，頤養正也，既濟定也。
Jian uses the image of a woman waiting for marriage rites to unfold; Yi concerns nourishment in the proper way; Ji Ji signifies a settled completion. The text compresses three different dimensions of orderly development.
一篇 第 30 段ZAGUA-01-30歸妹女之終也，未濟男之窮也。
Gui Mei signifies a woman's marriage as the close of one social phase; Wei Ji presents a man's efforts reaching difficulty without final completion. These statements reflect the text's ancient role language and do not impose modern gender expectations.
一篇 第 31 段ZAGUA-01-31夬決也，剛決柔也。君子道長，小人道消也。
Guai means decisive resolution as strong lines overcome a yielding obstacle. The passage interprets this as the growth of the cultivated person's way and the decline of petty conduct.
