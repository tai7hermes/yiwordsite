Tuan Commentary II / TUAN-L
TUAN-U · 34 units · text_version: yizhuan-en-3.0.0 · review_status: draft

Original text pending collation; translations under continuous revision.

UnitOriginalTranslation
31 咸 彖辭TUAN-L-31-01咸，感也。柔上而剛下，二氣感應以相與，止而說，男下女，是以亨利貞，取女吉也。天地感而萬物化生，聖人感人心而天下和平；觀其所感，而天地萬物之情可見矣！
Xian means mutual influence or response. Yielding and firm forces meet, and the two energies affect one another. Stillness combines with joy, and the man’s deferential approach to the woman belongs to the passage’s ancient marriage symbolism. The commentary broadens the theme: Heaven and Earth respond and produce life; wise people respond to human feeling and support peace. Observing relationships reveals how beings influence one another.
32 恆 彖辭TUAN-L-32-01恆，久也。剛上而柔下，雷風相與，巽而動，剛柔皆應，恆。恆亨无咎，利貞；久于其道也，天地之道，恆久而不已也。利有攸往，終則有始也。日月得天，而能久照，四時變化，而能久成，聖人久于其道，而天下化成；觀其所恆，而天地萬物之情可見矣！
Heng means continuity. Thunder and wind work together, while firm and yielding lines find their counterparts. Constancy is not mere immobility; it is commitment to a right course through continuing change. The sun and moon shine through regular movement, and the seasons complete their cycles by changing. The commentary asks what a person remains faithful to over time, since duration has value only when it serves an appropriate principle.
33 遯 彖辭TUAN-L-33-01遯亨，遯而亨也。剛當位而應，與時行也。小利貞，浸而長也。遯之時義大矣哉！
Dun concerns withdrawal at the proper time. Retreat is not automatically defeat: it may be the very means of preserving a worthwhile course. Firm strength still occupies an appropriate position, but a growing yielding influence alters the circumstances. Small matters can remain stable while broader confrontation becomes unwise. The commentary highlights the importance of recognizing when to step back.
34 大壯 彖辭TUAN-L-34-01大壯，大者壯也。剛以動，故壯。大壯利貞；大者正也。正大而天地之情可見矣！
Da Zhuang describes strength at its height. Firm energy is capable of decisive movement, but true greatness requires righteousness. The commentary rejects the assumption that power alone makes an action appropriate. Its final statement connects what is great and upright with the deeper character of Heaven and Earth.
35 晉 彖辭TUAN-L-35-01晉，進也。明出地上，順而麗乎大明，柔進而上行。是以康侯用錫馬蕃庶，晝日三接也。
Jin means advancement. Brightness rises above the Earth, and receptive conduct aligns itself with what is illuminating. A yielding line rises into an influential position. The narrative of a lord receiving horses and frequent audiences with the king illustrates recognition and promotion in an ancient political setting. Advancement is meaningful when it follows clarity and responsive conduct.
36 明夷 彖辭TUAN-L-36-01明入地中，明夷。內文明而外柔順，以蒙大難，文王以之。利艱貞，晦其明也，內難而能正其志，箕子以之。
Ming Yi depicts light going beneath the Earth and becoming obscured. Inner clarity may need to be protected by outward modesty during great hardship. The commentary invokes King Wen and Jizi as figures who endured danger without abandoning their inner commitments. Difficulty is not interpreted as a reason to surrender correctness; sometimes wisdom requires keeping one’s light out of view.
37 家人 彖辭TUAN-L-37-01家人，女正位乎內，男正位乎外，男女正，天地之大義也。家人有嚴君焉，父母之謂也。父父，子子，兄兄，弟弟，夫夫，婦婦，而家道正；正家而天下定矣。
Jia Ren discusses order within a household through the roles recognized by ancient society. Its statements about men and women reflect historical family arrangements rather than a universal modern rule. Parents are expected to guide the family, and each relationship carries responsibilities. The commentary connects orderly family conduct with the stability of the larger political community.
38 睽 彖辭TUAN-L-38-01睽，火動而上，澤動而下；二女同居，其志不同行；說而麗乎明，柔進而上行，得中而應乎剛；是以小事吉。天地睽，而其事同也；男女睽，而其志通也；萬物睽，而其事類也；睽之時用大矣哉！
Kui concerns difference and separation. Fire moves upward while water in the marsh moves downward, suggesting directions that do not naturally coincide. Even people who share a home may have different purposes. The commentary nevertheless points out that Heaven and Earth remain jointly productive despite differences. The lesson is not that all distinctions disappear, but that a limited form of cooperation may still be possible.
39 蹇 彖辭TUAN-L-39-01蹇，難也，險在前也。見險而能止，知矣哉！蹇利西南，往得中也；不利東北，其道窮也。利見大人，往有功也。當位貞吉，以正邦也。蹇之時用大矣哉！
Jian means obstruction. Danger lies ahead, and the ability to stop upon recognizing it is called wisdom. The directions southwest and northeast symbolize different degrees of practical difficulty. Seeking the help of a capable person and preserving the right position are both praised. The passage places discernment and support above stubborn persistence.
40 解 彖辭TUAN-L-40-01解，險以動，動而免乎險，解。解利西南，往得眾也。其來復吉，乃得中也。有攸往夙吉，往有功也。天地解，而雷雨作，雷雨作，而百果草木皆甲坼，解之時義大矣哉！
Jie describes release from difficulty through timely movement. After a danger has been resolved, returning to an ordinary position can be fortunate; when necessary tasks remain, acting early can produce results. The commentary compares release to thunder and rain breaking a period of confinement, allowing plants to emerge and grow. Removing an obstruction restores possibilities that had been held back.
41 損 彖辭TUAN-L-41-01損，損下益上，其道上行。損而有孚，元吉，无咎，可貞，利有攸往。曷之用？二簋可用享；二簋應有時。損剛益柔有時，損益盈虛，與時偕行。
Sun describes reduction, especially the transfer of resources or influence from below to above. Sincere reduction can be appropriate, but it must occur at the right time and in the right measure. The commentary uses a modest ritual offering to show that simplicity may express genuine reverence. Firmness and yielding, gain and loss, fullness and emptiness are all adjusted in response to the time.
42 益 彖辭TUAN-L-42-01益，損上益下，民說无疆，自上下下，其道大光。利有攸往，中正有慶。利涉大川，木道乃行。益動而巽，日進无疆。天施地生，其益无方。凡益之道，與時偕行。
Yi means increase. The passage describes benefit flowing from above to those below, creating broad satisfaction among the people. Those in authority become more effective when they support others rather than merely accumulate resources. Correct and central action brings favorable results. Just as a boat enables a river crossing, well-directed benefit creates new practical possibilities. Increase should follow changing circumstances.
43 夬 彖辭TUAN-L-43-01夬，決也，剛決柔也。健而說，決而和，揚于王庭，柔乘五剛也。孚號有厲，其危乃光也。告自邑，不利即戎，所尚乃窮也。利有攸往，剛長乃終也。
Guai describes a decisive break. A strong majority confronts a remaining obstacle, but judgment should be public and truthful rather than driven by aggression. The court and the local community are settings for making the danger known. Resorting immediately to arms is discouraged. The commentary favors a firm but measured resolution of conflict when the time has become ripe.
44 姤 彖辭TUAN-L-44-01姤，遇也，柔遇剛也。勿用取女，不可與長也。天地相遇，品物咸章也。剛遇中正，天下大行也。姤之時義大矣哉！
Gou means encountering. Yielding and firm forces unexpectedly meet, creating both generative possibility and a warning about allowing one influence to grow unchecked. The marriage language belongs to the traditional symbolism of the text. The commentary also observes that Heaven and Earth meet and living things take form. What matters is the character and consequence of an encounter.
45 萃 彖辭TUAN-L-45-01萃，聚也；順以說，剛中而應，故聚也。王假有廟，致孝享也。利見大人亨，聚以正也。用大牲吉，利有攸往，順天命也。觀其所聚，而天地萬物之情可見矣。
Cui means gathering. People come together when receptive conduct, mutual pleasure, and a reliable center work in concert. The ruler’s visit to an ancestral temple is an image of shared ritual and common belonging. Yet gathering must serve a proper purpose and recognize legitimate leadership. The commentary invites attention not only to the number of people assembled but also to what draws them together.
46 升 彖辭TUAN-L-46-01柔以時升，巽而順，剛中而應，是以大亨。用見大人，勿恤；有慶也。南征吉，志行也。
Sheng means rising or gradual ascent. Yielding strength advances according to its time, supported by modest conduct and a strong central response. Visiting a capable leader is favorable; movement toward the south symbolizes a purposeful journey. The commentary understands growth as something achieved by steady alignment, not by forceful self-promotion.
47 困 彖辭TUAN-L-47-01困，剛掩也。險以說，困而不失其所，亨；其唯君子乎？貞大人吉，以剛中也。有言不信，尚口乃窮也。
Kun describes confinement, especially the obscuring of firm strength. A person can still preserve an inner sense of rightness while facing severe constraints. The commentary praises the noble person who remains true to position and principle. A central, firm character matters more than an abundance of explanations, for words alone cannot restore trust when circumstances have become difficult.
48 井 彖辭TUAN-L-48-01巽乎水而上水，井；井養而不窮也。改邑不改井，乃以剛中也。汔至亦未繘井，未有功也。羸其瓶，是以凶也。
Jing uses the image of a well. A town may change, but the well continues to nourish those who come and go. The essential resource remains useful only if it can be drawn and shared. An unfinished draw or a broken vessel defeats the effort just before success. The commentary distinguishes enduring public value from the practical work required to make that value accessible.
49 革 彖辭TUAN-L-49-01革，水火相息，二女同居，其志不相得，曰革。巳日乃孚；革而信也。文明以說，大亨以正，革而當，其悔乃亡。天地革而四時成，湯武革命，順乎天而應乎人，革之時義大矣哉！
Ge describes transformation when opposed forces can no longer remain as they are. Water and fire conflict, suggesting a need for change. Reform gains trust only when its time has arrived. The commentary links rightful change with the turning of the seasons and with ancient political revolutions. Change without legitimacy is unstable; change undertaken at the proper time can resolve regret.
50 鼎 彖辭TUAN-L-50-01鼎，象也。以木巽火，亨飪也。聖人亨以享上帝，而大亨以養聖賢。巽而耳目聰明，柔進而上行，得中而應乎剛，是以元亨。
Ding uses the ritual cooking vessel as an image of nourishment and ordered transformation. Wood feeds fire, and the vessel prepares offerings for the divine and food for worthy people. The commentary links receptive intelligence with an effective central position. Great success comes not from the vessel itself but from the proper use of resources in service of ritual and community.
51 震 彖辭TUAN-L-51-01震，亨。震來虩虩，恐致福也。笑言啞啞，后有則也。震驚百里，驚遠而懼邇也。出可以守宗廟社稷，以為祭主也。
Zhen means shaking or thunder. Sudden fear can become the beginning of care and good fortune if it leads to disciplined conduct. After the shock, one may recover enough composure to speak and laugh. The thunder that startles distant and nearby people becomes an image of leadership able to maintain its duties even under disturbance.
52 艮 彖辭TUAN-L-52-01艮，止也。時止則止，時行則行，動靜不失其時，其道光明。艮其止，止其所也。上下敵應，不相與也。是以不獲其身，行其庭不見其人，无咎也。
Gen means stopping. The appropriate action is not always motion: there are times to halt and times to proceed. True clarity depends on acting and resting at the right moment. The commentary describes stopping where one should stop and letting go of self-centered attachment. The absence of mutual entanglement between the lines makes freedom from blame possible.
53 漸 彖辭TUAN-L-53-01漸之進也，女歸吉也。進得位，往有功也。進以正，可以正邦也。其位剛，得中也。止而巽，動不窮也。
Jian, meaning gradual progress, likens orderly advancement to a marriage conducted according to accepted rites. Reaching the proper position makes constructive achievement possible. Inner stillness and outward gentleness allow progress without exhaustion. The text connects personal development and public order through the idea that a stable result requires a suitable process.
54 歸妹 彖辭TUAN-L-54-01歸妹，天地之大義也。天地不交，而萬物不興，歸妹人之終始也。說以動，所歸妹也。征凶，位不當也。无攸利，柔乘剛也。
Gui Mei treats marriage and the continuation of generations as a major relationship between Heaven, Earth, and humanity. Yet the marriage described here is marked by problematic positions and an imbalance between yielding and firm forces. Acting simply because one is pleased does not guarantee a good outcome. The commentary reflects ancient marriage conventions while examining the consequences of a relationship entered under unsuitable conditions.
55 豐 彖辭TUAN-L-55-01豐，大也。明以動，故豐。王假之，尚大也。勿憂宜日中，宜照天下也。日中則昃，月盈則食，天地盈虛，與時消息，而況于人乎？況于鬼神乎？
Feng means abundance or greatness. Brightness joined with vigorous movement produces a flourishing condition. A ruler at the height of prosperity is compared to the midday sun, whose light reaches far. Yet the very same image also warns that the sun must decline after noon and the full moon must wane. Prosperity and change are inseparable.
56 旅 彖辭TUAN-L-56-01旅，小亨，柔得中乎外，而順乎剛，止而麗乎明，是以小亨，旅貞吉也。旅之時義大矣哉！
Lü concerns the traveler, who lacks a fixed home and has only limited resources. A yielding line occupies the central position outside, cooperating with firm strength. Stillness and attachment to clarity permit modest progress. A traveler can act successfully by remaining correct and cautious, but the situation is not the same as secure residence.
57 巽 彖辭TUAN-L-57-01重巽以申命，剛巽乎中正而志行。柔皆順乎剛，是以小亨，利有攸往，利見大人。
Xun describes repeated penetration and the careful transmission of instructions. Firm strength remains responsive to central correctness, while yielding lines cooperate with it. The result is limited but workable progress. Seeing a capable person and undertaking measured action are favorable because influence grows through persistence and clarity rather than sudden force.
58 兌 彖辭TUAN-L-58-01兌，說也。剛中而柔外，說以利貞，是以順乎天，而應乎人。說以先民，民忘其勞；說以犯難，民忘其死；說之大，民勸矣哉！
Dui means joy and openness. Firmness is held within while yielding appears outwardly, so pleasant conduct need not abandon principle. When leaders foster confidence and shared purpose, people may willingly undertake work and face hardship. The commentary presents joy as a powerful social bond when grounded in correctness and genuine human response.
59 渙 彖辭TUAN-L-59-01渙，亨。剛來而不窮，柔得位乎外而上同。王假有廟，王乃在中也。利涉大川，乘木有功也。
Huan concerns dispersal and the removal of blockage. Firm strength does not become trapped, and yielding influence finds an appropriate position. The ruler’s temple represents a common center that can reassemble people after division. Crossing a great river by boat illustrates movement made possible by suitable means. Dispersal can therefore clear a path toward renewed connection.
60 節 彖辭TUAN-L-60-01節，亨，剛柔分，而剛得中。苦節不可貞，其道窮也。說以行險，當位以節，中正以通。天地節而四時成，節以制度，不傷財，不害民。
Jie describes measured limits. Firm and yielding positions are differentiated, while central firmness prevents arbitrary excess. Severe restriction that causes needless suffering cannot last. Proper limits, by contrast, help institutions function and protect resources. Just as the seasons arise through orderly divisions, a good system sets bounds without exhausting wealth or harming the people.
61 中孚 彖辭TUAN-L-61-01中孚，柔在內而剛得中。說而巽，孚，乃化邦也。豚魚吉，信及豚魚也。利涉大川，乘木舟虛也。中孚以利貞，乃應乎天也。
Zhong Fu concerns inner sincerity. Yielding forces lie within, while firmness occupies central positions; openness and humility make trust effective. The image of even small creatures being reached by sincerity expresses how far integrity may extend. A hollow wooden boat can cross a river, linking inner openness with practical action. Sincerity becomes valuable when joined with correctness.
62 小過 彖辭TUAN-L-62-01小過，小者過而亨也。過以利貞，與時行也。柔得中，是以小事吉也。剛失位而不中，是以不可大事也。有飛鳥之象焉，有飛鳥遺之音，不宜上宜下，大吉；上逆而下順也。
Xiao Guo concerns a slight excess that may be acceptable in small matters but dangerous in major ones. Yielding lines occupy central positions while firm lines are poorly placed. The bird in flight symbolizes the risk of rising too high. The commentary recommends lowering one’s aim and adapting to circumstance rather than taking on an undertaking beyond one’s capacity.
63 既濟 彖辭TUAN-L-63-01既濟，亨，小者亨也。利貞，剛柔正而位當也。初吉，柔得中也。終止則亂，其道窮也。
Ji Ji means completion. The lines stand in comparatively appropriate positions, allowing limited success and initial good fortune. Yet completion creates its own danger: if vigilance stops, order may deteriorate into confusion. The commentary cautions that a favorable beginning is not a guarantee of an enduring result.
64 未濟 彖辭TUAN-L-64-01未濟，亨；柔得中也。小狐汔濟，未出中也。濡其尾，无攸利；不續終也。雖不當位，剛柔應也。
Wei Ji means not yet completed. Even without an ideal arrangement of the lines, firm and yielding forces respond to one another. The small fox almost crosses the stream but wets its tail before reaching safety. The image warns against losing care at the final stage. Because completion has not yet been achieved, appropriate effort and mutual response remain necessary.
