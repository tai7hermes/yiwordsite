31 咸 大象XIANG-L-31-01山上有澤，咸；君子以虛受人。A lake rests upon a mountain: this is Influence. The exemplary person welcomes others with an open and receptive mind.
A mountain is high yet makes room for a lake: influence begins in receptivity, not domination. An open heart listens carefully while retaining the judgment needed to separate honest counsel from mere flattery.
31 咸 小象 1XIANG-L-31-02 181Yao咸其拇，志在外也。The stirring of the big toe shows that the intention is already directed outward, although movement has barely begun.
A movement in the toe is the faintest beginning of action. The wish has already gone outside oneself, yet desire alone is not a warrant to rush; recognize the impulse before allowing it to govern conduct.
31 咸 小象 2XIANG-L-31-03 182Yao雖凶，居吉，順不害也。Remaining still is fortunate even amid danger, because yielding appropriately avoids harm.
Restraint is fortunate because a responsive person need not follow every summons. Yielding to the actual conditions, rather than making an anxious advance, preserves safety and creates room for better mutual understanding.
31 咸 小象 3XIANG-L-31-04 183Yao咸其股，亦不處也。志在隨人，所執下也。Feeling the influence in the thighs prevents remaining still; merely following others reveals a low and dependent purpose.
The thigh is pulled by impulses from elsewhere, showing someone who cannot settle on a purpose. Mere imitation of another person's wishes sacrifices inner direction; genuine influence requires a judgment of one's own.
31 咸 小象 4XIANG-L-31-05 184Yao貞吉悔亡，未感害也。憧憧往來，未光大也。Steadfastness removes regret because influence has not brought harm. Restless thoughts, however, cannot accomplish something broad.
Constancy removes regret before the attraction becomes harmful. Repeated anxious coming and going, by contrast, narrows attention to winning a response and prevents the expansive moral influence envisaged by the text.
31 咸 小象 5XIANG-L-31-06 185Yao咸其脢，志末也。Influence felt in the back shows that the aspiration has reached only an outer or limited part of the person.
The back is less responsive than the face or hands: this image suggests a feeling that remains peripheral. One may respond outwardly yet fail to bring serious intention or the deeper self into the encounter.
31 咸 小象 6XIANG-L-31-07 186Yao咸其輔，頰，舌，滕口說也。Influence confined to jaw, cheeks, and tongue is little more than persuasive speech, not deep or lasting feeling.
Cheeks and tongue portray persuasion reduced to verbal performance. Words can initiate contact, but speech that lacks sincere conduct cannot produce the deeper resonance associated with this hexagram.
32 恆 大象XIANG-L-32-01雷風，恆；君子以立不易方。Thunder and wind operate together in Constancy. The exemplary person establishes a firm direction and does not casually abandon it.
Wind and thunder work in recurring patterns without becoming identical. Endurance therefore means a steady ethical direction maintained through changing circumstances, not mechanical stubbornness or an inability to revise methods.
32 恆 小象 1XIANG-L-32-02 187Yao浚恆之凶，始求深也。Seeking excessive depth or permanence from the outset invites misfortune; durability must develop over time.
Demanding profound commitment at the very beginning confuses aspiration with maturity. Durability grows through proof and time; excessive expectations can break a relationship before its foundations have formed.
32 恆 小象 2XIANG-L-32-03 188Yao九二悔亡，能久中也。The second line loses regret because it can sustain a balanced course for a long time.
The second line persists in the middle, avoiding both impatient change and rigid excess. Regret disappears because a balanced commitment can survive difficult intervals without being carried to extremes.
32 恆 小象 3XIANG-L-32-04 189Yao不恆其德，无所容也。When a person cannot maintain virtue consistently, there is no secure place for that person to be accepted.
If character alters with every temptation, others cannot know what to rely on. A person who lacks consistency eventually loses both trust and a stable place among others.
32 恆 小象 4XIANG-L-32-05 190Yao久非其位，安得禽也。Remaining a long time in an unsuitable position cannot produce the game sought in the hunt.
Long residence in an unsuitable station does not bring success. Persistence has value only when paired with a fitting responsibility; repeated effort in the wrong place may simply repeat failure.
32 恆 小象 5XIANG-L-32-06 191Yao婦人貞吉，從一而終也。夫子制義，從婦凶也。The text praises a wife's constancy within ancient marital roles, while warning that a husband must judge duty himself; this reflects historical role expectations.
The wording reflects ancient gender roles, not a rule for modern equality. Its deeper contrast is between fidelity to a rightful commitment and a decision-maker's duty to judge circumstances according to justice rather than merely submit.
32 恆 小象 6XIANG-L-32-07 192Yao振恆在上，大无功也。Agitated persistence at the highest place achieves little. Restlessness is the opposite of genuine constancy.
Restlessness at the summit destroys what steady effort built below. Endless agitation is not perseverance; after achievement, true continuity requires restraint and the capacity to preserve what matters.
33 遯 大象XIANG-L-33-01天下有山，遯；君子以遠小人，不惡而嚴。A mountain stands beneath Heaven, depicting Retreat. The exemplary person keeps distance from unworthy influences without hatred, while maintaining dignity.
Retreat creates moral distance without personal hatred. The exemplary person draws firm boundaries against corrupting influence, remaining dignified and refusing to turn necessary separation into bitterness.
33 遯 小象 1XIANG-L-33-02 193Yao遯尾之厲，不往何災也。Retreating last is dangerous; if one refrains from going forward, the danger need not arise.
The one at the tail of a withdrawal is exposed. Moving forward merely to appear brave compounds the danger; sometimes remaining still is the only effective way to avoid disaster.
33 遯 小象 2XIANG-L-33-03 194Yao執用黃牛，固志也。The yellow oxhide signifies a determination bound so firmly that it cannot easily be loosened.
The ox-hide binding represents resolve that cannot easily be shaken. Withdrawal succeeds when a clear purpose holds firm against both social pressure and the lure of returning too soon.
33 遯 小象 3XIANG-L-33-04 195Yao系遯之厲，有疾憊也。畜臣妾吉，不可大事也。Retreat entangled by attachments brings weakness and distress. Managing minor household affairs is possible, but great undertakings are inappropriate.
Attachments make withdrawal painfully slow and exhaust the person caught between obligations. Small domestic arrangements may still be managed, but this is not the moment to undertake sweeping ventures.
33 遯 小象 4XIANG-L-33-05 196Yao君子好遯，小人否也。An exemplary person can choose a graceful retreat, whereas a petty person finds such withdrawal difficult.
For one who values principle, relinquishing an unsuitable place can be welcome. Those bound by appetite or status cannot let go, because they mistake possession for genuine strength.
33 遯 小象 5XIANG-L-33-06 197Yao嘉遯貞吉，以正志也。Timely and worthy retreat is fortunate because it sets the intention right.
A graceful retreat is guided by an upright intention, not resentment. By keeping the objective clear, one withdraws with integrity and avoids converting prudence into defeatism.
33 遯 小象 6XIANG-L-33-07 198Yao肥遯，无不利；无所疑也。A generous and unencumbered retreat encounters no disadvantage because nothing remains to cause doubt.
Complete withdrawal is spacious because doubt and clinging have been settled. There is no remaining struggle to prove oneself; the person can act freely in a different direction.
34 大壯 大象XIANG-L-34-01雷在天上，大壯；君子以非禮弗履。Thunder resounds in Heaven in Great Strength. The exemplary person refuses to take any step contrary to proper conduct.
Thunder above heaven depicts strength reaching its height. The corresponding discipline is to avoid any step that breaches propriety: power becomes legitimate only when self-restraint directs it.
34 大壯 小象 1XIANG-L-34-02 199Yao壯于趾，其孚窮也。Strength expressed only in the toes soon reaches its limit. Bold movement without judgment cannot last.
Power concentrated in the toes suggests an urge to charge before the whole person is ready. Confidence cannot compensate for a bad starting position, and an impulsive first move may exhaust one's prospects.
34 大壯 小象 2XIANG-L-34-03 200Yao九二貞吉，以中也。The second line is fortunate through constancy because it occupies a balanced position.
The second line keeps strength within the middle way. Its fortunate firmness shows that measured action is more durable than a display of force.
34 大壯 小象 3XIANG-L-34-04 201Yao小人用壯，君子罔也。A petty person uses brute strength; the exemplary person declines to act that way.
The lesser person uses strength to overpower; the exemplary person refuses to let raw capacity become the standard of right. Moral restraint distinguishes authority from aggression.
34 大壯 小象 4XIANG-L-34-05 202Yao藩決不羸，尚往也。The fence breaks and the ram's horns are freed; obstacles have opened, making continued advancement possible.
The hedge has opened and no longer catches the ram's horns. Once the obstruction genuinely clears, continued advance is appropriate; restraint does not require perpetual hesitation.
34 大壯 小象 5XIANG-L-34-06 203Yao喪羊于易，位不當也。Losing the ram results from occupying an unsuitable position, not simply from a shortage of force.
Losing the sheep in an easy place reflects a mismatch between one's position and one's means. When success appears effortless, complacency can lead to loss.
34 大壯 小象 6XIANG-L-34-07 204Yao不能退，不能遂，不詳也。艱則吉，咎不長也。Unable either to retreat or to advance, the ram faces a grave impasse. Patient endurance can keep blame from continuing.
Unable either to retreat or finish the advance, one has misread the situation. A sober acknowledgment of difficulty, rather than further struggling blindly, can prevent the fault from lasting.
35 晉 大象XIANG-L-35-01明出地上，晉；君子以自昭明德。Light rises above Earth in Progress. The exemplary person makes personal virtue increasingly bright and visible.
Sunlight rising over the earth makes progress visible. Advancement is ethically meaningful when a person first cultivates and displays clear character, rather than merely gaining rank or recognition.
35 晉 小象 1XIANG-L-35-02 205Yao晉如，摧如；獨行正也。裕无咎；未受命也。Progress initially meets resistance, yet upright solitary conduct remains valid. Being patient before receiving a mandate avoids blame.
Although the advance is checked, walking alone in what is right retains value. Because no commission has yet been granted, patience and generosity prevent frustration from turning into wrongdoing.
35 晉 小象 2XIANG-L-35-03 206Yao受茲介福，以中正也。Great blessings come through occupying a central and correct position.
Substantial blessing comes through centrality and correctness, not chance alone. Progress rooted in fairness creates a sound basis for trust and reward.
35 晉 小象 3XIANG-L-35-04 207Yao眾允之志，上行也。The trust of the multitude allows an aspiration to move upward.
The acceptance of many people permits the intention to rise. Collective confidence is a moral resource; advancement cannot be secured solely by private ambition.
35 晉 小象 4XIANG-L-35-05 208Yao鼫鼠貞厲，位不當也。The grasping field mouse faces danger because its position is unsuitable.
The greedy field mouse is misplaced, taking resources from a station it does not deserve. Its danger lies in acquisitiveness that makes an already insecure position still less defensible.
35 晉 小象 5XIANG-L-35-06 209Yao失得勿恤，往有慶也。Do not become consumed by gains or losses: proceeding with purpose can bring celebration.
Fretting over every gain and loss obstructs a larger course. The text counsels perseverance in a worthy endeavor, not indifference to accountability.
35 晉 小象 6XIANG-L-35-07 210Yao維用伐邑，道未光也。Using strength only to discipline one's own city shows that the larger way has not yet shone fully.
When advancement culminates in military action against one's own settlement, its illumination is incomplete. Coercion may settle a dispute while exposing the failure to gain consent by better means.
36 明夷 大象XIANG-L-36-01明入地中，明夷；君子以蒞眾，用晦而明。Light enters Earth in Darkening of the Light. The exemplary person leads others with wisdom that does not always display itself.
The light is buried beneath the earth but need not be extinguished. In troubled times the wise person moderates outward display while guarding inner discernment and compassion.
36 明夷 小象 1XIANG-L-36-02 211Yao君子于行，義不食也。The traveler goes without food because loyalty to what is right outweighs personal comfort.
Refusing food on a compromised journey dramatizes refusal of patronage at the price of principle. Survival matters, but dependence on injustice may demand a still greater sacrifice.
36 明夷 小象 2XIANG-L-36-03 212Yao六二之吉，順以則也。The second line is fortunate because its responsiveness follows appropriate standards.
Yielding is not surrender when it follows proper standards. The second line can accommodate circumstances without relinquishing its rule of conduct.
36 明夷 小象 3XIANG-L-36-04 213Yao南狩之志，乃大得也。The aim of the southern hunt is fulfilled when the principal source of harm is found.
The southern hunt evokes confronting the source of obscurity rather than striking at symptoms. The intention achieves something large by finding the decisive point of intervention.
36 明夷 小象 4XIANG-L-36-05 214Yao入于左腹，獲心意也。Entering the left side signifies gaining insight into the concealed intentions of the threatening power.
Entering the left belly means penetrating concealed motives. Under oppressive conditions a careful understanding of what power actually wants is essential before choosing how to respond.
36 明夷 小象 5XIANG-L-36-06 215Yao箕子之貞，明不可息也。Jizi's constancy shows that inner light must not be extinguished even when outward circumstances are dark.
The example of Jizi shows that integrity can be concealed for protection without being abandoned. External darkness does not authorize inward moral extinction.
36 明夷 小象 6XIANG-L-36-07 216Yao初登于天，照四國也。后入于地，失則也。At first the light rose and illuminated the four regions; later it fell into darkness through loss of proper measure.
A ruler once illuminated the four quarters but later sank beneath the earth. The downfall follows the loss of governing standards: brilliant authority without discipline cannot endure.
37 家人 大象XIANG-L-37-01風自火出，家人；君子以言有物，而行有恆。Wind issues from fire in The Family. The exemplary person speaks with substance and acts consistently.
Wind issuing from fire portrays influence spreading from intimate daily life. Credible words and consistent action form the foundation of a well-ordered household and, by extension, trustworthy public life.
37 家人 小象 1XIANG-L-37-02 217Yao閑有家，志未變也。Establishing household boundaries early prevents regret while intentions have not yet shifted.
Boundaries established early prevent a household's guiding purpose from drifting. Rules should create safety and mutual responsibility, not become an excuse for arbitrary control.
37 家人 小象 2XIANG-L-37-03 218Yao六二之吉，順以巽也。The second line's good fortune comes from receptive and accommodating conduct.
The historical line praises dutiful accommodation within household roles. Its enduring lesson is reliable responsibility combined with tact; the ancient assignment of those roles need not be carried into modern relationships.
37 家人 小象 3XIANG-L-37-04 219Yao家人嗃嗃，未失也；婦子嘻嘻，失家節也。Firm household discipline is not necessarily mistaken; excessive joking without boundaries disrupts family order.
Rigorous conduct can avoid disorder, but unrestrained joking may dissolve shared boundaries. The distinction is not between joy and severity: it is whether affection has a trustworthy framework.
37 家人 小象 4XIANG-L-37-05 220Yao富家大吉，順在位也。The prosperity of the household is fortunate because each person fulfills an appropriate position.
Prosperity comes when members fulfill fitting responsibilities and cooperate. Wealth is presented as the fruit of ordered partnership rather than the work of one dominating person.
37 家人 小象 5XIANG-L-37-06 221Yao王假有家，交相愛也。The ruler reaching the family shows reciprocal affection and mutual care.
The ruler's influence reaches the family through reciprocal affection. A household is genuinely ordered when its members care for one another, not merely obey an imposed hierarchy.
37 家人 小象 6XIANG-L-37-07 222Yao威如之吉，反身之謂也。Dignity brings good fortune when it begins with examining and correcting oneself.
A commanding presence becomes auspicious through self-examination. The person who demands discipline from others must first hold personal conduct to the same standards.
38 睽 大象XIANG-L-38-01上火下澤，睽；君子以同而異。Fire rises while the lake lies below: Opposition. The exemplary person recognizes shared ground without erasing real differences.
Fire rises while the lake's water descends, expressing divergent tendencies. The exemplary person can share a larger association without erasing distinctive judgments and identities.
38 睽 小象 1XIANG-L-38-02 223Yao見惡人，以辟咎也。Encountering a disagreeable person may prevent greater blame when handled with caution.
Contact with a disagreeable person may be necessary to avert more serious conflict. Civil engagement here is prudent risk management, not approval of that person's conduct.
38 睽 小象 2XIANG-L-38-03 224Yao遇主于巷，未失道也。Meeting the master in a narrow lane does not mean the right course has been lost.
Meeting the rightful principal in a narrow lane shows that duty can be fulfilled by an unexpected route. Respect the substance of a relationship instead of insisting upon a perfect setting.
38 睽 小象 3XIANG-L-38-04 225Yao見輿曳，位不當也。无初有終，遇剛也。A dragged carriage signals an unsuitable place. The difficult beginning can still end well when firmness is encountered.
A dragged carriage indicates that the third line occupies an unsuitable place. Yet a strong counterpart eventually makes a better ending possible; initial discord need not decide the whole relationship.
38 睽 小象 4XIANG-L-38-05 226Yao交孚无咎，志行也。Mutual trust overcomes estrangement and makes the purpose attainable.
Trust allows separate intentions to become effective together. In an atmosphere of opposition, a sincere alliance is worth more than a superficial appearance of agreement.
38 睽 小象 5XIANG-L-38-06 227Yao厥宗噬膚，往有慶也。Reunion with one's own kin allows forward movement to bring good fortune.
The image of kin biting through skin suggests closeness that can break through obstacles. It celebrates the restoration of practical connection across a divided group.
38 睽 小象 6XIANG-L-38-07 228Yao遇雨之吉，群疑亡也。Rain dissolves the accumulated suspicions; what seemed hostile can become a fortunate meeting.
Rain resolves the tense atmosphere and dispels accumulated suspicion. Reconciliation comes when people cease treating imagined hostility as proven fact.
39 蹇 大象XIANG-L-39-01山上有水，蹇；君子以反身修德。Water stands above a mountain in Obstruction. The exemplary person turns inward to examine conduct and cultivate virtue.
Water blocks the way over a mountain. Obstruction calls first for self-examination and the cultivation of virtue, not simply more force applied to the same impasse.
39 蹇 小象 1XIANG-L-39-02 229Yao往蹇來譽，宜待也。Forward movement encounters difficulty, while return earns respect. This is a time to wait.
An advance would only meet obstruction, whereas returning brings respect. Waiting is a deliberate response to conditions, not evidence of cowardice.
39 蹇 小象 2XIANG-L-39-03 230Yao王臣蹇蹇，終无尤也。A minister struggles repeatedly for the ruler rather than personal advantage, and ultimately deserves no reproach.
The king's servant repeatedly faces hardship in carrying out duty. The absence of blame rests on fidelity to legitimate responsibility, not an assurance that every mission will succeed.
39 蹇 小象 3XIANG-L-39-04 231Yao往蹇來反，內喜之也。Returning from obstruction brings joy to those within one's circle.
Returning from obstruction delights those close at hand. A difficult outward quest may be less urgent than a neglected obligation or need within one's own circle.
39 蹇 小象 4XIANG-L-39-05 232Yao往蹇來連，當位實也。Returning to join others after difficulty is suitable because the position is solid.
Retreat reconnects the fourth line with others because the position has substance. Partnership offers a better way through difficulty than solitary insistence.
39 蹇 小象 5XIANG-L-39-06 233Yao大蹇朋來，以中節也。During great obstruction, companions arrive when one maintains a measured center.
In severe distress, companions come because the central position has been maintained with due measure. Sustained integrity can attract needed help when command alone cannot.
39 蹇 小象 6XIANG-L-39-07 234Yao往蹇來碩，志在內也。利見大人，以從貴也。Returning instead of forcing the road yields substance; seeking a worthy superior accords with the inward purpose.
Returning produces something substantial because the heart remains committed to the inner group. Seeking a worthy senior figure is beneficial when it means joining judgment to trustworthy leadership.
40 解 大象XIANG-L-40-01雷雨作，解；君子以赦過宥罪。Thunder and rain break out in Release. The exemplary person pardons errors and treats offenses with measured leniency.
Thunder and rain release pent-up pressure. Once a crisis loosens its grip, the ethical task is to distinguish deliberate injury from human error and restore order through proportionate mercy.
40 解 小象 1XIANG-L-40-02 235Yao剛柔之際，義无咎也。At the meeting of firmness and gentleness, no blame arises when their relation is fitting.
The meeting of strong and yielding elements is appropriate, so no blame arises. Release depends upon restoring the right relation between differing capacities.
40 解 小象 2XIANG-L-40-03 236Yao九二貞吉，得中道也。The second line's perseverance is fortunate because it has reached the middle way.
The second line is fortunate because it holds the middle course. Resolve is required, but a cure for disorder should not reproduce the excess it is meant to correct.
40 解 小象 3XIANG-L-40-04 237Yao負且乘，亦可丑也，自我致戎，又誰咎也。Carrying a load while riding in privilege invites disgrace and attackers; one cannot blame others for risks produced by one's own behavior.
One who carries a laborer's burden while claiming a privileged vehicle invites resentment. The resulting threat is self-provoked: misused status erodes legitimacy and invites retaliation.
40 解 小象 4XIANG-L-40-05 238Yao解而拇，未當位也。Freeing the big toe indicates removal of a constraint, yet the present position is still not fully appropriate.
Even when an immediate restraint is removed, the position may still be unsuitable. Liberation remains incomplete until the conditions that produced dependency are corrected.
40 解 小象 5XIANG-L-40-06 239Yao君子有解，小人退也。When the exemplary person achieves release, petty and obstructive influences withdraw.
When a worthy person genuinely resolves the difficulty, destructive opportunists recede. Good leadership removes the advantage that disorder offered them.
40 解 小象 6XIANG-L-40-07 240Yao公用射隼，以解悖也。The noble's shot at the hawk removes a disorderly source of trouble and restores proper order.
The duke's shot at a hawk removes a disruptive presence from a high place. The image supports targeted correction of harm rather than indiscriminate punishment.
41 損 大象XIANG-L-41-01山下有澤，損；君子以懲忿窒欲。A lake lies below a mountain in Decrease. The exemplary person restrains anger and curbs excessive desires.
The lake beneath the mountain evokes a deliberate reduction. The first things to diminish are anger and excessive craving, because private excess damages judgment before it harms possessions.
41 損 小象 1XIANG-L-41-02 241Yao已事遄往，尚合志也。Quickly assisting another after one's own duties are done brings the intention into accord with those above.
Once one's immediate task is finished, prompt help can align with another's purpose. Giving should serve a real need rather than gratify the giver's vanity.
41 損 小象 2XIANG-L-41-03 242Yao九二利貞，中以為志也。The second line favors constancy because balance has become the core intention.
The second line makes the middle way its intention. Beneficial reduction is measured; one must not sacrifice the integrity required to be useful.
41 損 小象 3XIANG-L-41-04 243Yao一人行，三則疑也。When three travel together, suspicion may divide them; simpler companionship can allow trust to form.
When three travel together, suspicion may multiply, while a clear partnership fosters coherence. The point concerns attention and trust, not a universal prohibition against collaboration.
41 損 小象 4XIANG-L-41-05 244Yao損其疾，亦可喜也。Removing an illness or defect is cause for joy, for decrease can be healing rather than deprivation.
To reduce illness or a fault is cause for joy. The most valuable form of loss may be the removal of something that weakened the person.
41 損 小象 5XIANG-L-41-06 245Yao六五元吉，自上佑也。The fifth line's great fortune is described as help received from above.
The fifth line receives aid figured as descending from above. The blessing points to fortune and support arising beyond personal calculation, without exempting anyone from ethical responsibility.
41 損 小象 6XIANG-L-41-07 246Yao弗損益之，大得志也。Helping others without unnecessary self-diminishment allows a significant aspiration to be fulfilled.
The culmination turns reduction into increase: an individual is no longer depleted and can contribute to others. Fulfillment here is shared capacity, not merely private accumulation.
42 益 大象XIANG-L-42-01風雷，益；君子以見善則遷，有過則改。Wind and thunder combine in Increase. On seeing goodness, the exemplary person adopts it; on finding an error, corrects it.
Wind and thunder mutually strengthen one another. Increase begins with practical moral learning: move toward good conduct when you see it and correct a fault as soon as you recognize it.
42 益 小象 1XIANG-L-42-02 247Yao元吉无咎，下不厚事也。Great good fortune without blame reflects the need not to overload those in lower positions.
A great undertaking can be fortunate, but the person below must not be overloaded. The ethical test of increase includes whether burdens are distributed fairly.
42 益 小象 2XIANG-L-42-03 248Yao或益之，自外來也。Unexpected benefit arrives from outside, showing that increase is not always self-produced.
Help that arrives from outside reveals the limits of solitary achievement. Accepting assistance gratefully can be wiser than insisting on self-sufficiency.
42 益 小象 3XIANG-L-42-04 249Yao益用凶事，固有之也。Providing support during calamity belongs to one's established responsibilities.
The third line can turn calamity into an occasion for constructive gain because strength was cultivated beforehand. Crisis reveals reserves of character and organization that ordinary success conceals.
42 益 小象 4XIANG-L-42-05 250Yao告公從，以益志也。The duke accepts the report because it advances a beneficial purpose.
A proposal accepted by the ruler advances a common intention. The gain is larger when communication connects different levels of responsibility rather than serving a faction.
42 益 小象 5XIANG-L-42-06 251Yao有孚惠心，勿問之矣。惠我德，大得志也。Sincere generosity need not be questioned; receiving goodwill in return greatly fulfills one's intention.
A genuinely generous heart needs no elaborate display of its sincerity. Benefits become durable when others respond to reliable kindness with trust.
42 益 小象 6XIANG-L-42-07 252Yao莫益之，偏辭也。或擊之，自外來也。No one helps and someone attacks because the words are partial; harm then arrives from outside.
When language becomes partisan and self-serving, people withdraw help and hostility arrives from outside. A demand for increase that refuses reciprocity defeats itself.
43 夬 大象XIANG-L-43-01澤上于天，夬；君子以施祿及下，居德則忌。The lake rises into Heaven in Breakthrough. The exemplary person distributes benefits downward and avoids hoarding virtue as private property.
Water held above heaven must eventually be released. Decisive action ought to distribute benefit downward, while hoarding public goods or moral credit is a warning sign of abused power.
43 夬 小象 1XIANG-L-43-02 253Yao不勝而往，咎也。Advancing without being able to win produces blame; determination must be joined to capacity.
Going forth without the means to prevail incurs blame. Conviction must be joined to realistic preparation; a rash cause can damage even a worthy end.
43 夬 小象 2XIANG-L-43-03 254Yao莫夜有戎，得中道也。Even with soldiers appearing at night, a centered stance prevents panic.
Vigilance through the night preserves the middle course in moments of threat. Preparation is not panic when it is proportionate to the risk.
