Cauldron
The
H50 · text_version: zhouyi-en-1.0.0 · review_status: draft

Original text pending collation; translations under continuous revision.

LineOriginalTranslation
JudgmentH50-JUDGMENT鼎：元吉，亨。The Cauldron. Supreme good fortune and success.
Line 1H50-L1初六：鼎顛趾，利出否，得妾以其子，无咎。The cauldron is overturned, its legs pointing upward; this is favorable for removing what is spoiled. One takes a concubine for the sake of her son. No blame.
Line 2H50-L2九二：鼎有實，我仇有疾，不我能即，吉。The cauldron with the things (to be cooked) in it. (If its subject can say), ' My enemy dislikes me, but he cannot approach me,' good fortune follows.
Line 3H50-L3九三：鼎耳革，其行塞，雉膏不食，方雨虧悔，終吉。The cauldron’s handles are altered, so it cannot be moved. Its rich food is not eaten. When rain comes, regret lessens. Good fortune in the end.
Line 4H50-L4九四：鼎折足，覆公餗，其形渥，凶。The cauldron’s legs break; the prince’s meal is spilled and the vessel is soiled. Misfortune.
Line 5H50-L5六五：鼎黃耳金鉉，利貞。The cauldron with yellow ears and rings of metal in them. There will be advantage through being steadfast and principled.
Line 6H50-L6上九：鼎玉鉉，大吉，无不利。The cauldron has a jade carrying ring. Great good fortune; nothing is unfavorable.
