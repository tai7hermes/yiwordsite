Cauldron The

Original text pending collation; translations under continuous revision. · hexagram_id: H50 · text_version: zhouyi-en-1.0.0 · review_status: draft

Judgment H50-JUDGMENT

Original：鼎：元吉，亨。

Translation：The Cauldron. Supreme good fortune and success.

Line 1 H50-L1

Original：初六：鼎顛趾，利出否，得妾以其子，无咎。

Translation：The cauldron is overturned, its legs pointing upward; this is favorable for removing what is spoiled. One takes a concubine for the sake of her son. No blame.

Line 2 H50-L2

Original：九二：鼎有實，我仇有疾，不我能即，吉。

Translation：The cauldron with the things (to be cooked) in it. (If its subject can say), ' My enemy dislikes me, but he cannot approach me,' good fortune follows.

Line 3 H50-L3

Original：九三：鼎耳革，其行塞，雉膏不食，方雨虧悔，終吉。

Translation：The cauldron’s handles are altered, so it cannot be moved. Its rich food is not eaten. When rain comes, regret lessens. Good fortune in the end.

Line 4 H50-L4

Original：九四：鼎折足，覆公餗，其形渥，凶。

Translation：The cauldron’s legs break; the prince’s meal is spilled and the vessel is soiled. Misfortune.

Line 5 H50-L5

Original：六五：鼎黃耳金鉉，利貞。

Translation：The cauldron with yellow ears and rings of metal in them. There will be advantage through being steadfast and principled.

Line 6 H50-L6

Original：上九：鼎玉鉉，大吉，无不利。

Translation：The cauldron has a jade carrying ring. Great good fortune; nothing is unfavorable.
