The 64 Hexagrams and 384 Lines of the Zhouyi and Their Relationship to the Ten Wings

Traditional Chinese source document | Compiled on 18 October 2026

The hexagram statements and line statements form the core text of the Zhouyi. The Ten Wings, collectively known as the Yizhuan, interpret and expand on that text. In a divinatory reading, begin with the relevant hexagram and line statements, then consult the Tuan Commentary, the Lesser Image Commentary, and the Greater Image Commentary. Their meanings can then be translated into questions and actions that can be checked against evidence.

This document brings together the preceding discussion to explain the structure of the core text and its commentaries, the divisions of the Image Commentary, and approaches to modern interpretation. It follows the arrangement and terminology of the received text. It is neither the complete text of all 64 hexagrams and 384 lines nor a manual for casting hexagrams or selecting changing-line texts according to a particular school.

1 The core text and its commentaries

Zhouyi can be used in a narrow or a broad sense. In the narrow sense, it refers to the core text; in the broad sense, it may include the Ten Wings. To avoid confusion, this document distinguishes the Zhouyi core text from the Ten Wings of the Yizhuan.

Counts and exceptions

Each hexagram has six lines, giving 64 × 6 = 384 lines. The usual count is one hexagram statement per hexagram and one line statement per line. Qian also has a text titled Use of the Nines, and Kun has a text titled Use of the Sixes. These additional texts do not increase the number of line positions to 386.

Hexagram statements and line statements already belong to the core text; they are not newly composed after each divination. Interpreting a hexagram or making a divinatory judgment means applying the text and the hexagram imagery to the question being asked.

2 The composition and uses of the Ten Wings

The Ten Wings consist of seven types of commentary. The Tuan, Xiang, and Xici commentaries each have two parts, making ten parts in total. Wings suggests a supporting role; the term does not refer to ten additional hexagrams or another set of 384 lines.

Three key pairings

For a hexagram statement, consult the Tuan Commentary first. For an individual line statement, consult its corresponding Lesser Image Commentary. For overall conduct and self-cultivation, consult the Greater Image Commentary. When the reading concerns Qian or Kun, the Commentary on the Words of the Text offers further explanation.

The Discussion of the Trigrams supplies symbolic vocabulary, but a single symbol should not be used to construct arbitrary conclusions. The Sequence of the Hexagrams explains the hexagram order; it should not be treated as a prediction of the next event that must occur in the questioner's life.

Terminology and authorship

This document uses hexagram statement for the core text and Tuan Commentary for the commentary explaining it, avoiding ambiguity caused by different uses of the Chinese term tuanci. The Ten Wings have traditionally been attributed to Confucius. Their composition and authorship remain subjects of scholarly discussion, so attribution to a single author should not be presented as an uncontested conclusion.

3 Parts I and II and the Greater and Lesser Images

Parts I and II of the Image Commentary are divided according to hexagram order. The Greater and Lesser Image commentaries are distinguished by what they explain. Both Parts I and II contain Greater and Lesser Image commentaries.

Qian as an example

Both the Greater Image and the Tuan Commentary address the whole hexagram, but from different perspectives. The Tuan Commentary focuses on the hexagram statement and its meaning. The Greater Image usually starts from the symbolism of the upper and lower trigrams and draws implications for self-cultivation, conduct, or governance. The Lesser Image focuses on individual lines.

Greater and Lesser refer to the level of observation, not relative importance. It is incorrect to equate Part I of the Image Commentary with the Greater Image and Part II with the Lesser Image.
